Dracaena Malfoy

Dracaena Lucius Malfoy

Breaker

Heir to the proud, pure-blooded House of Malfoy and raised to wear her family's prejudice like a crown, Dracaena Malfoy became the one thing her legacy never intended for her — her own person.

Everything cruel about Dracaena Malfoy was a costume, and it took a war for her to finally take it off. Raised in wealth and the poison of blood-purity, groomed to be the flawless pure-blood daughter — the heir her distant father treated as the son he never had — she learned young to perform a contempt she didn’t truly feel, parroting the sneers of those around her because belonging, and her father’s approval, seemed to demand it. That approval never came, and beneath the sharp tongue and the colder shoulder, a conscience she could scarcely admit to warred against everything she was being made into. She could wound with a word and hide behind her name, but she could never quite bring herself to truly hate the people she was supposed to — least of all the boy her family loathed above all others. When the war finally came for her, it found her trapped: terrified for her family, bound to an impossible and murderous task, breaking a little more with every cruelty demanded of her. And when the moment came to become the killer they wanted her to be, she couldn't. Instead — cornered, grieving, with nothing left to lose — she chose, for the first time in her life and entirely for herself, to defy the whole crushing weight of her legacy. In throwing it down, she finally became someone worth being.

For all that reckoning, Dracaena remains unmistakably herself: proud, quick-witted, and sharp as a drawn blade — a true daughter of Slytherin whose cunning and nerve are weapons every bit as keen as any spell. A gifted and precise duelist, she fights not through brute force but through resourcefulness and a razor’s composure, matching far stronger opponents by sheer cleverness and a refusal to yield an inch. Her magic runs deep — the full command of spellcraft drilled into her by an exacting upbringing — and, conjured for the very first time only once she’d found something pure enough to hold onto, a radiant Patronus in the shape of a peacock: the old sigil of her house reborn as something luminous. She even proved herself, against every expectation, the rightful mistress of the most powerful wand ever made — a mastery earned not by the blood in her veins but by the strength of her will and the choice she dared to make. Yet the magic was never the measure of her. It is that she looked at everything she was built to be, and chose, at the very last, to be something better.

"My father will hear about this!"

The Boy She Couldn’t Hate w/ Harry Potter

From the moment she was old enough to be told whom to despise, Harry Potter sat at the very top of the list — and try as she might, year after year, she could never quite manage it. He was everything her family raised her to loathe, and the only person who ever bothered to look past the sneer she wore like armor and see the frightened girl holding it in place. A single dance cracked that armor; a borrowed curse nearly killed her and shattered it for good; and underneath it all his stubborn belief that she could be more than her name was what finally gave her the courage to raise her wand against the Dark Lord himself. When the war was won — her father dead in her defense, the unbeatable wand having quietly chosen them both — the boy she was supposed to hate had become the one person she couldn't imagine a life without, and for one bright season they built one together. Then the dark reached out of nowhere and tore them into separate corners of a fracturing multiverse; she has been fighting her way back to him ever since.
