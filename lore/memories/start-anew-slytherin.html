Memory

Start Anew, Slytherin

Dracaena Malfoy Memory · The Wizarding World

The stool was smaller than she had imagined, and the Hall louder, and the hat — when they set it over her eyes — smelled of dust and of a great many other children who had sat exactly here and been, for one breath, no one at all. Dracaena kept her chin level. She had been told how this went. She had been told, in fact, since before she could spell the word, what she was and where she would go, and she had never once been given the chance to disagree.

“A Malfoy,” the hat murmured, somewhere behind her ear, with the unhurried amusement of a thing that had met a thousand of her. “Well. There’s no mystery in the name, is there. The name wants Slytherin. The name has always wanted Slytherin.”

“Yes,” she thought back, before she could stop herself — and then, because the silence waited: “Everyone keeps saying so.”

“Ah.” A pause, considering. “And what do you say?”

It was not a question she had been prepared for. No one had thought to ask it; it had seemed, to all of them, already answered. She sat with it a moment, aware of the Hall holding its breath beyond the brim of the hat, aware of a table of green and silver that expected her the way one expects the sun. She thought of her father’s certainty, which had never been a cruelty exactly, only a wall — and she found, to her own quiet surprise, that she did not want to be walked through a door simply because it had been held open her whole life.

“I say,” she answered slowly, “that if I’m going there, I’d rather go because I chose it. Not because it was decided for me.”

The hat was quiet for what felt like a long time. Then, so only she could hear, it seemed almost to smile. “Now that,” it said, “is the first genuinely Slytherin thing you’ve done. Ambition is wanting the throne. Cunning is making the inheritance your own. Very well, Miss Malfoy — go and be the version of it they didn’t expect.”

“SLYTHERIN!” it called to the Hall — the same word they had all known it would say, and yet, to the only person who mattered, an entirely different one. She lifted the hat from her own head before the professor could, and walked to the green and silver not as a conclusion but as a decision, and sat among them already a little apart, already beginning, in the smallest and most private way, again.
