Marked as an infant by a curse that should have killed him and spared by the power of his mother’s love, Harry Potter grew from an unwanted orphan into the Boy Who Lived — the wizard fated to bring down the darkest sorcerer the world had ever known.

Harry never asked to be a legend, and that is precisely what made him one. Orphaned by Voldemort before he could walk and raised without a scrap of affection by relatives who resented him, he learned at eleven that he was a wizard, that his parents had been murdered, and that the whole magical world already knew his name for surviving a night he couldn’t even remember. The weight of being the Chosen One would have crushed a prouder boy. But what set Harry apart was never raw talent — others were cleverer, others more gifted — it was his courage, his fierce and unwavering loyalty to the people he loved, and above all a capacity for love and self-sacrifice, inherited from the mother who died for him, that his enemy was utterly incapable of understanding. He wanted no glory and no power; he fought only to protect, and he extended, again and again, a hand of compassion and a second chance to those the rest of the world had already written off. He had a rare gift for making others braver than they believed they could be. And in the end, when it came to it, he walked willingly to his own death to spare everyone else — and it was that love, not any spell, that finally unmade the Dark Lord.

For all that his heart was his greatest strength, Harry was no slouch with a wand. Bravest and most capable in the face of dark magic, he mastered the Patronus Charm while still a boy — a shining silver stag he can summon against the deepest despair — and flew a broom with a natural brilliance that made him the youngest Seeker in a century. His strange, scar-born bond with Voldemort left him able to speak the language of serpents, a double-edged gift that was as much burden as weapon. And famously, he won his hardest-fought duels not with cruelty or flash but with a single, simple disarming spell and an unbreakable nerve. Yet the wand was never where his true power lay. It was in the thing Voldemort, for all his terrible might, never once grasped — the quiet, stubborn strength of an ordinary boy’s heart, which proved in the end to be stronger than the darkest magic ever cast.

The Boy She Couldn’t Hate w/ Dracaena Malfoy

For years Harry wrote Dracaena Malfoy off as exactly what she presented — spoiled, cruel, his sharpest rival at every turn. It was only once he started truly watching her, convinced she was hiding something, that he began to see what lay underneath: not a villain, but a girl breaking under a weight no one should have to carry, performing a cruelty that fit her worse the longer the war dragged on. The more he understood the less he could hate her, and telling her she was better than everything she'd been raised to be — again and again, until she believed it herself — may be the most important thing he ever did; it was what gave her the nerve, in the end, to turn her wand on the Dark Lord and help him win. They came out the other side of the war having chosen each other, and walked out of Hogwarts to build the ordinary life neither of them had ever been allowed. When the dark tore that life apart and scattered them across the multiverse, finding her again became the promise Harry means to keep above every other.

Changelog

Balance changes since this Champion's release. Newest first.

v1.0.1 October 19, 2026
  • Nerf Durability pass — MAX HP reduced 8,000 → 7,600, RES reduced 210 → 170, CRIT reduced 180% → 175%.
  • Buff MAG increased 370 → 400.
  • Buff Magical Blasts (Basic) — damage increased 170% → 230% MAG.
  • Rework Stupefy — damage increased 300% → 350% MAG; cooldown increased 3 → 4 turns.
  • Buff Expelliarmus — damage increased 350% → 450% MAG.
  • Rework Impedimenta — damage increased 350% → 380% MAG; -15% SPD removed; "Impediment Jinx" reduced 2 stacks → 1 stack.
  • Rework Protego — "Shield Charm" reduced 2 stacks → 1 stack; now counters incoming attacks with 220% MAG for 2 turns (up to 3 times). The Core's 300% MAG Shield Charm counter was removed.
  • Buff Flying Broomstick — airborne duration increased 2 → 3 turns.
  • Nerf The Boy Who Lived (Core Passive) — post-resurrection fatal-blow endure removed; resurrection timing clarified to "after 3 complete turns pass since death".
  • Rework Love and Protection — Protego can now be used on allies within 5 panels in addition to self; ally casts apply only "Shield Charm" (no counters).
  • Rework Non-Lethal Methods — healing reduced 4% → 3% HP; "Confidence" now granted to allies within 10 panels for 1 turn upon inflicting "Impediment Jinx" or "Stun" (was 3 turns upon defeating an enemy).
  • Rework Invisibility Cloak (ULTIMATUM) — persistent "Invisible" reduced 3 → 2 turns; now also grants 3 stacks of "Pinpoint" for 2 turns.
  • Nerf Savior of The Wizarding World (Leader) — "Wizarding World Universe" removed from the +18% MAG and RES line.

Harry launched as a Breaker wearing a Striker's badge — the lowest proactive damage of any Striker, cheaper and more frequent hard control than the roster's actual Breakers, and near-Defender effective HP from a Core that both resurrected him at 80% and let him endure a second fatal blow. This pass moves his budget from durability and control into damage: MAG, his basic and his headline spells all rise, while the endure, a slice of HP and RES, the second jinx stack and the slow are cut, and Stupefy's guaranteed Stun now comes around less often. Protego's counters move out of the Core into the spell itself (capped at three), so the Core no longer forces every build into Protego, and ally-cast shields protect without countering. Invisibility Cloak becomes an ambush ULTIMATUM whose Pinpoint stacks finally put his high CRIT to work, giving him the burst finisher every other Striker has. Non-Lethal Methods now rewards disarming rather than killing — matching its name — at a single turn of Confidence, and the Leader drops its universe-wide clause so it stays within the Link economy, where broad Links carry weak Leaders. He remains magic-tanky and physically fragile, with control that sets up his damage rather than replacing it.
