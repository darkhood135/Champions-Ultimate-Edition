Lord Voldemort

Tom Marvolo Riddle

Berserker

Born Tom Marvolo Riddle, the loveless orphan who would stop at nothing to escape death rose to become Lord Voldemort — the Dark Lord, the most dangerous wizard of any age, whose very name a generation was too afraid to speak.

Everything Lord Voldemort became grew out of a single terror: that he might one day die like anyone else. Conceived under a love potion and born in a Muggle orphanage to a mother who would not live even for his sake, he grew into a boy who already took what he wanted, hoarding small trophies stolen from the children he frightened, certain from the start that he was more than the people around him. Hogwarts gave him a world worthy of that certainty. He charmed his teachers, gathered followers rather than friends, opened the Chamber of Secrets as Slytherin’s heir, and murdered the Muggle father who had abandoned him, casting off the name they shared and rearranging the letters of himself into the title he would make the world fear. Then he did what no wizard had ever dared: he tore his soul apart and hid the pieces, so that while even one remained, he could not die. It was the purest expression of who he was — a genius whose arrogance outran even his brilliance, so sure of his own cunning that he never imagined anyone could find what he had hidden, and so sure of his own right that he believed the Elder Wand itself belonged to him by birthright. He feared death, despised the Muggle blood in his own veins, and held love in contempt as the weakness of lesser minds. On one night in Godric’s Hollow, a mother’s love for her son turned his own curse back on him and left him less than a ghost for thirteen years. He never forgave the world for it, and he never once understood what had beaten him.

When he rose again from a cauldron in a graveyard, it was as something far more terrible than any legend had dared describe. The reborn Dark Lord moves with a speed and strength no wizard should possess, and his power is less a skill than a force of nature. Fire, stone, and the very air reshape themselves to his will, and the dark that pours off him presses down into the souls of those around him until the bravest cannot stand, cannot lift a wand, can barely breathe. The anchored pieces of his soul wrap him in a black radiance that turns aside blade, bullet, and lesser curse alike, and he can dissolve into living smoke and reform wherever he wishes, so that ordinary force finds nothing to strike. He flies unaided, speaks the tongue of serpents, and tears through minds as easily as he plants lies inside them. He wields the Killing Curse, the one spell no shield can stop, as carelessly as a man brushing dust from his sleeve, with the great serpent Nagini, the last anchor of his soul, forever coiled at his side. Yet for every power he tore out of the world, Voldemort never troubled to learn the oldest magic of all. He dismissed love as weakness to the very end, and love was the one thing he could never touch, never break, and never survive.

"There is no good and evil, there is only power, and those too weak to seek it."

Neither Can Live w/ Harry Potter

Of all the infants in Britain, Voldemort chose this one. Acting on half a prophecy, he marked the Potter boy as his equal with his own hand, and the curse meant to end that child rebounded off a mother’s love and left the Dark Lord less than a ghost. Every humiliation since traces back to that night. A boy who should have been nothing slipped through his fingers in a hidden chamber, in a graveyard and in the Ministry’s own atrium. Their wands refused to fight each other, and the bond between their minds, which Voldemort turned into a weapon, turned against him every time. The resemblance galls him most: two orphans, two Parselmouths, two boys who found their first true home at Hogwarts. It means the only thing that ever divided them is the one thing he has always dismissed as weakness. To the very end he believed Harry Potter lived on luck and a dead woman’s borrowed protection. He died believing it, his own curse hurled back at him by a wand he was certain was his by right. That wand had quietly chosen the boy instead, and the traitor girl at his side.

The Prophecy They Made w/ Frieza

Voldemort has met few beings he could not look down on, and the galactic emperor is one of the rare ones he must look at as an equal. Frieza rules by terror on a scale the Dark Lord has only dreamed of, and beneath it all he wants exactly what Voldemort wants: never to die. Voldemort finds the emperor’s hunt for immortality faintly contemptible, a conqueror reduced to begging a dragon for the eternity Voldemort tore out of his own soul with his own hands. Frieza, for his part, sees the wizard as a talented warlord of one small, backward world, frightening in his way and nothing more. Neither notices how plainly the other mirrors him. Each heard a prophecy of his downfall, and each, in terror of it, struck first: one at an infant in a cradle, the other at an entire race. In striking, each forged the very enemy he feared. They are two masters of fear who never learned the lesson their own lives keep teaching, and each would sooner die than see himself in the other.

The Shadow She Defied w/ Dracaena Malfoy

To the Dark Lord, the Malfoy girl was never more than a convenient instrument — a tool to punish her father's failures, cheap and expendable, kept obedient by the simple leverage of everyone she loved. He never thought of her as a person, let alone a threat; her terror amused him, and her compliance he took entirely for granted. That a frightened child he'd long since broken might find the will to raise her wand against him was, to Voldemort, not even a possibility worth guarding against — which is precisely how it helped to undo him. He never grasped, to the very end, that the ones he deemed too small to matter were exactly the ones who would bring him down.
