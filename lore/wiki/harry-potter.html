Harry James Potter (born 31 July 1980) is a half-blood wizard of , the only son of James and Lily Potter. As an infant he survived the Killing Curse that cast at him on the night his parents were murdered, shielded by his mother’s sacrifice, and became famous across the wizarding world as the Boy Who Lived. He knew none of it. He grew up unwanted in a cupboard under the stairs of his aunt and uncle’s house, until a letter in emerald ink told him at eleven that he was a wizard.

At he found the first family of his life in and , and an enemy in , the pure-blood girl whose handshake he refused on the first day. Over seven years the war came back for him: the return of Voldemort, a prophecy that named him the Chosen One, a godfather lost through a veil, and a rival he slowly learned to see through, then to love. He walked into the Forbidden Forest to die so that others would not have to, came back, and destroyed the Dark Lord with a Disarming Charm and Dracaena’s hand over his own.

Physically, Harry is an ordinary young man, and a slight one. Peak-human Champions like Batman or Captain America would overpower him in close quarters, and anything at Spider-Man’s speed could close the distance before he finished a spell. What makes him dangerous is a wand arm trained in real fights: a fast, reliable Disarming Charm that strips an opponent’s resources in a single beat, a shield, a stunner, and the nerve to use them under pressure. His Patronus is the strongest of his generation, and the protections around him (his mother’s sacrifice, the Invisibility Cloak, the Elder Wand’s refusal to harm him) count for more than raw power.

Against city-level threats and beyond (the Hulk, Superman, any Super Saiyan) he has no answer beyond courage and allies. Within his own world he is an exceptional duelist for his age and a better fighter than , though she outstrips him in study and spellcraft. He escaped Voldemort again and again but never truly outdueled him: in the end he won with the Elder Wand’s choice and Dracaena beside him.

History

Worlds Apart

Family background

Harry’s father, James Potter, came from an old wizarding family; his mother, Lily Evans, was Muggle-born, the daughter of an ordinary family whose elder girl, Petunia, never forgave her sister for being magical. James and Lily met at Hogwarts, where James and his three closest friends (Sirius Black, Remus Lupin and Peter Pettigrew) called themselves the Marauders and mapped every secret of the castle. After school, James and Lily joined the Order of the Phoenix, Albus Dumbledore’s secret society against Lord Voldemort, and defied the Dark Lord three times.

A prophecy, half-overheard and carried to Voldemort, told of a boy born at the end of July to parents who had thrown him off three times, a boy who would have the power to defeat him. Voldemort chose Harry. The Potters went into hiding under the Fidelius Charm, and made Peter Pettigrew their Secret-Keeper in the belief that no one would suspect him. Peter sold them. On Halloween 1981 Voldemort came to their house in Godric’s Hollow, killed James, and then killed Lily when she would not stand aside from her son’s cot. When he turned his wand on the baby, the curse rebounded. Lily’s death had left a protection in Harry that Voldemort could not touch. The Dark Lord was torn from his body and fled as something less than a ghost, and Harry was left with a lightning-bolt scar and, unknown to anyone, a splinter of Voldemort’s soul lodged inside him.

Early life (1981–1991)

Dumbledore left the baby on the doorstep of his aunt and uncle, Petunia and Vernon Dursley, at number four, , because the blood Harry shared with Lily’s sister would keep her protection alive while he lived there. The Dursleys wanted nothing to do with magic and less to do with him. For ten years Harry slept in the cupboard under the stairs, wore the cast-offs of his bullying cousin Dudley, and was told his parents had died in a car crash. Strange things happened around him when he was frightened or angry, and he learned not to mention them. He grew up certain that he was ordinary, unwanted and alone.

The summer he turned eleven, letters addressed to him in emerald ink began arriving by the hundred, and every effort Vernon made to burn, hide or outrun them only summoned more. On a storm-battered night in a shack on a rock at sea, the sender came in person: Rubeus Hagrid, the Hogwarts gamekeeper, who told Harry the truth. He was a wizard, his parents had been murdered, and a school had been expecting him since the day he was born.

Hagrid took him into the hidden heart of the wizarding world. At Harry found a small fortune waiting in his parents’ vault. At Ollivanders a holly wand with a phoenix-feather core chose him, and the old wandmaker told him that the feather’s twin sat in the wand that had given him his scar. And at Madam Malkin’s, being fitted for school robes, he stood on the stool beside a pale, poised girl who talked about brooms and houses and which people were worth knowing. When she called Hagrid a strange creature, Harry heard himself telling her that maybe her father was wrong. She told him her father was never wrong. He left thinking she reminded him of his cousin ().

First year (1991–1992)

Main article:

The Weasley family showed him through the barrier to Platform Nine and Three-Quarters, and on the Hogwarts Express he fell into the first real friendship of his life, with the youngest Weasley boy, Ron. Their compartment did not stay quiet for long. The girl from the robe shop found it, introduced herself as Dracaena Malfoy, flanked by Crabbe and Goyle, and offered to teach him which wizarding families were worth knowing. She held out her hand. Harry, who had already decided he liked Ron and disliked the company she kept, told her he could figure it out on his own, and stood up beside Ron when her companions squared up to them (). The Sorting Hat weighed Slytherin for him, then put him in on its own judgment (). An exacting, insufferable girl named Hermione Granger went to , Dracaena to Slytherin, and Ron, last of all, to , the first Weasley in living memory sorted outside Gryffindor.

At the first-years’ flying lesson, Neville Longbottom fell from his broom and broke his wrist, and Dracaena snatched up his Remembrall and dared Harry into the air after it. Harry had never sat on a broom in his life. He flew as if he had been born to it, chased her down, and caught the Remembrall in a plunging dive. Professor McGonagall saw the catch, and instead of expelling him made him the youngest house Seeker in a century (). After a mountain troll was loosed in the castle on Halloween, he, Ron and Hermione were friends for good: a Gryffindor, a Hufflepuff and a Ravenclaw, with no common room to share and Hagrid’s hut as their meeting place. At his first Quidditch match, his broom turned savage beneath him and tried to throw him to his death; he kept his seat and caught the Snitch half by accident ().

That Christmas an unsigned parcel brought him his dead father’s Invisibility Cloak, and the Cloak brought him to the Mirror of Erised, where he sat night after night and watched his mother and father and a whole family he had never known look back at him out of the glass, until Dumbledore gently warned him away ().

Caught out of bounds after helping Hagrid smuggle out an illegal dragon, Harry served detention in alongside Hermione, Neville and Dracaena. Dracaena, terrified, clung to his robes, and Harry let her. When a hooded figure came out of the dark to drink a unicorn’s blood, his scar blazing, he put himself between it and her before she fled screaming into the trees, and stood his ground alone until Hagrid’s crossbow drove it off. Afterward she called him a mediocre bodyguard at best (). The centaur Firenze named the creature: the remnant of Voldemort, waiting for the Sorcerer’s Stone to restore him.

When no adult would believe them, the three went after the Stone themselves. Neville planted himself in front of the portrait hole to stop Harry, and Harry, out of time, put him gently aside with a binding spell and an apology (). Past Fluffy, a strangling plant, a storm of winged keys and a giant chessboard where Ron let himself be struck down, Harry went on alone. Waiting for him was Professor Quirrell, with Voldemort’s face on the back of his skull. The Mirror gave the Stone to Harry because he wanted to find it but not to use it, and when Quirrell seized him, Harry’s touch burned him: his mother’s love, still protecting him. He woke days later in the hospital wing. At the leaving feast Dumbledore’s last points, the final ones to Neville, honored all four of them, but Slytherin kept the House Cup it had earned, and Harry found he didn’t mind. On the train home Dracaena found him alone and, after rubbing the cup in his face, offered him her hand a second time. This time he took it.

Second year (1992–1993)

Main article:

A house-elf named Dobby appeared in his bedroom that summer to warn him not to return to Hogwarts, and ruined the Dursleys’ dinner party to force the point. A letter from the Ministry warned him he would be expelled for the house-elf’s spell. Ron and his brothers rescued him in a flying car and took him to , the first place that had ever felt like a home. At a book signing in Diagon Alley he met Lucius Malfoy for the first time, and watched a few cold words from him bring Dracaena back into line (). Barred from the platform, he and Ron chased the train in the flying car and crashed into the Whomping Willow.

The Chamber of Secrets was opened, and students began turning up petrified. Harry heard a murderous voice in the walls that no one else could hear. Seeker to Seeker against Dracaena, he was hunted the length of a match by a rogue Bludger that shattered his arm, and caught the Snitch one-handed anyway (). At the dueling club, when Dracaena’s conjured snake turned on the Hufflepuff Justin Finch-Fletchley, Harry called it off in Parseltongue without knowing he was doing it, and the school decided he was Slytherin’s heir. When Justin was found petrified soon after, Hufflepuff turned on Harry, and Ron stood with him against his own house. Disguised as Crabbe and Goyle with Hermione’s Polyjuice Potion, he and Ron questioned Dracaena in the Slytherin common room and learned she had nothing to do with it. What Harry saw behind her bluster was a lonely girl performing for companions who were not really friends ().

The trail led to a diary that wrote back: the memory of Tom Riddle, the boy who became Voldemort, who had been feeding on Ron’s sister Ginny all year. When Ginny was taken into the Chamber, Harry went down after her alone. Fawkes, Dumbledore’s phoenix, brought him the Sorting Hat, and from it Harry drew the sword of Gryffindor and drove it through the basilisk’s skull, with one of its fangs already buried in his arm. He stabbed the diary through with that same fang, and the phoenix’s tears saved his life. Afraid that he belonged in Slytherin after all, he heard from Dumbledore that the Hat could have put him there and chose Gryffindor, that only a true Gryffindor could have drawn the sword, and that he had chosen to go down into the Chamber. Afterward he tricked Lucius Malfoy into freeing Dobby. Hagrid, sent to Azkaban without a trial on the Minister’s word alone, came home.

Third year (1993–1994)

Main article:

Sirius Black had escaped from Azkaban, and the whispered word was that he was coming for Harry. Dementors were set around the school to catch him, and they affected Harry worse than anyone, because they dragged up the memory of his mother screaming. The new Defense teacher, Remus Lupin, taught him the Patronus Charm in private lessons. Black broke into the castle twice, first slashing the Gryffindor portrait and then reaching the Hufflepuff dormitories, where Ron woke to find him standing over his bed. When Dracaena’s taunting got Hagrid’s Hippogriff, Buckbeak, condemned to death by her father’s influence, Harry noticed something he did not expect: she was ashamed of it.

In Hogsmeade, invisible under his Cloak, Harry pelted her with mud and sent her fleeing in tears, and his satisfaction curdled into shame before she was out of sight. He went after her alone, dropped the Cloak and apologized. It was the first honest moment the two of them had ever shared (). Later, when Hermione struck Dracaena across the face and Ron howled with laughter, Harry did not laugh, and the argument that followed was the first his friends ever had with him about her (). It was not affection yet. It was the stubborn, inconvenient fairness that made him who he was.

On the night of Buckbeak’s execution the truth came out in the Shrieking Shack. Sirius was innocent; Peter Pettigrew, alive all these years as Ron’s pet rat, had betrayed the Potters and framed him. Pettigrew escaped. The Dementors came for Sirius at the lake, and Harry, failing, saw a silver stag drive them back and believed for a moment that his father had come. With Hermione’s Time-Turner, which had never once turned for her timetable but turned now to undo an injustice in progress, they went back, saved Buckbeak and Sirius both, and Harry understood that the stag across the water had been his own Patronus all along. He gained a godfather and lost him to the run in the same night.

Fourth year (1994–1995)

Main article:

The Goblet of Fire named Harry, who had not entered, as a fourth and underage champion in the Triwizard Tournament, and bound him to compete. Ron, caught between his own jealousy and a Hufflepuff house furious that its champion, Cedric Diggory, had been upstaged, refused to speak to him for weeks, and Dracaena’s POTTER STINKS badges spread across half the school (). Harry warned Cedric about the dragons of the first task, because it was only fair, and watching him face one ended Ron’s grudge.

Turned down for the Yule Ball by Chao Zheng, Harry was approached by Dracaena, who wrapped an invitation in a sneer, and he heard only the sneer and sent her away. That night he lay awake turning it over until it looked less like an attack than like someone who had been hurt (). When Hermione suggested that taking Malfoy to the ball might be the surest way to learn what Lucius was planning, Harry seized on the plan faster than he should have, because it gave him a reason to say out loud for going to her (). She said yes. When the champions opened the ball, the hall went silent at the sight of Harry Potter with Dracaena Malfoy on his arm, and for the length of one song he saw the girl beneath the performance and understood that the cruelty had always been the costume ().

For a few weeks they were quietly something. Then Dracaena, hidden under his Cloak, overheard Ron and Hermione speak of the ball as a plan, and went cold. In the third task, the Triwizard Cup was a Portkey. It carried Harry and Cedric Diggory to a graveyard, where Cedric was murdered on a word and Harry was bound and bled to give Voldemort a body again (). When their wands met, the twin cores locked in a cage of light, and Harry escaped with Cedric’s body. Lucius Malfoy had knelt in that circle. Dracaena came to Harry afterward carrying both the hurt and the terror, pushed his Cloak back into his hands, struck him across the face, and warned him to stay away from her before it killed him (). The Minister for Magic chose not to believe any of it.

Fifth year (1995–1996)

Main article:

Two Dementors attacked Harry and Dudley in a Muggle alley that summer, and the Ministry used his Patronus as an excuse to try to expel him. Dumbledore got him cleared. He spent the rest of the summer at with the Order of the Phoenix and his godfather, who was trapped in the house he had hated as a boy. Back at school, across the Great Hall on the first night, Dracaena’s eyes found his and held for a moment before she looked away, and Harry understood how far apart the war had already put them ().

The Ministry installed Dolores Umbridge at Hogwarts, and she made Harry write lines with a quill that cut them into the back of his own hand. Denied any real defense teaching, he founded Dumbledore’s Army and taught it himself. In Occlumency lessons with Snape he saw his own father bully the teenage Snape, and carried the shame of it for months. Dracaena joined Umbridge’s Inquisitorial Squad. One night in a corridor the two of them finally said what they meant: venom they did not mean and truths they had never spoken aloud. Harry was brutally honest, it broke her, and he walked away feeling he had won nothing (). He and Chao Zheng grew close in the Army, but a Valentine’s Day in Hogsmeade fizzled, because she was still grieving Cedric and he was still thinking of Dracaena, and they stayed friends. When the Army was exposed, it was because Chao had been forced to talk under Veritaserum, not because she betrayed them, and Harry said so.

Through the link in his scar, Voldemort sent Harry a vision of Sirius being tortured in the Department of Mysteries. Harry went, with his friends, into the trap, and never thought to open the two-way mirror Sirius had given him. In the battle that followed every Time-Turner in the Ministry was destroyed, and Sirius was struck by Bellatrix Lestrange’s curse and fell through the veil (). Voldemort was seen in the Ministry atrium, and the denial ended. Afterward Dumbledore told Harry the prophecy at last: that neither he nor Voldemort could live while the other survived (). Left Sirius’s house, name and servant, Harry did what Sirius never had: he set the old house-elf Kreacher free, though Kreacher had helped lure Sirius to his death, and the elf left that night for a life no one could predict ().

Sixth year (1996–1997)

Main article:

Dumbledore took Harry into his confidence that year, walking him through the memories of the boy Tom Riddle had been, and taught him that Voldemort had split his soul into Horcruxes, which fed his power as well as anchoring his life. The ring Dumbledore had destroyed that summer bore the same strange sign Harry had seen on Luna Lovegood’s necklace. On the train Dracaena found him alone and, for once, did not sneer: she warned him to keep away from her and everything that was coming, then froze him where he sat and left. Harry spent the year certain she was up to something, and right, and no one believed him. An old Potions book annotated by the Half-Blood Prince made him the best brewer in his year. Ron was nearly killed by a bottle of poisoned mead meant for Dumbledore, and Harry saved him with a bezoar (). Harry won Slughorn’s hidden memory of the young Riddle without any trick, by telling him the truth and whose son was asking.

He tracked Dracaena on the Marauder’s Map until he found her in a sixth-floor bathroom, sobbing to Moaning Myrtle that the Dark Lord would kill her and her parents. When she saw him she fired, and the duel that followed tore the room apart. When she started to say a word he recognized as the Cruciatus Curse, he used the Prince’s spell, Sectumsempra, not knowing what it did. It cut her open. He caught her before she hit the floor and held her as she bled, begging her to stay awake, and she apologized to him. Snape healed her, and Harry carried her to the hospital wing through a crowd that now believed he was a murderer (). That day he admitted to himself that he loved her, and he burned the Prince’s book ().

Dumbledore took him to a sea cave for a Horcrux and came back weakened, with a locket that turned out to be a fake. They returned to find the Dark Mark over the castle. Dracaena had let the Death Eaters in. Ada Khumalo was killed in the invasion by Goyle’s father. Harry, frozen and hidden on the Astronomy Tower, watched Dumbledore lay down his own wand, watched Dracaena let hers fall rather than kill him, and watched Snape kill him instead. In the chase Snape blocked every one of the Prince’s spells Harry threw at him, and revealed that he was the Half-Blood Prince. In the chase that followed he reached out his hand to her, and she refused it and went with the Death Eaters into the dark. Standing over Dumbledore’s body, Harry resolved to find and destroy every Horcrux, to end Voldemort, and to pull her off the wall she was trapped on if she would let him ().

The hunt and the Battle of Hogwarts (1997–1998)

Main article:

The Ministry fell to Voldemort from within, and Harry, Ron and Hermione did not go back to school. They went on the run to hunt the Horcruxes. With Kreacher gone, Harry called on Dobby, who tracked down the thief Mundungus Fletcher and with him the trail of the locket. They took it from Umbridge inside the Ministry itself, and could not destroy it. It drove Ron away for a time. At Godric’s Hollow Harry stood at his parents’ grave for the first time, was nearly killed by Voldemort’s snake, and lost his wand. Ron came back to pull him out of a frozen pool and destroy the locket with the sword of Gryffindor. The legend of the Deathly Hallows tangled with their quest, and Harry let Voldemort take the Elder Wand from Dumbledore’s tomb rather than turn aside from the Horcruxes.

Captured and taken to , the three were held while Bellatrix tortured Hermione on the drawing-room floor. Asked to identify Harry’s swollen face, Dracaena said she could not be sure, and in the fight she pressed her hawthorn wand into his hand. He fought the rest of the war with it. Dobby came for them, and was killed in the escape, and died free in Harry’s arms (). They broke into Gringotts with the goblin Griphook, who took back the goblin-made sword as his price, and escaped on a dragon. Then they returned to Hogwarts. In the Room of Requirement Harry hauled Dracaena out of Crabbe’s Fiendfyre, and the diadem burned with it.

Dying, Snape gave Harry his memories and an apology for the years he had spent punishing him, and Harry learned that he himself was a Horcrux and would have to let Voldemort kill him. He found Ron and Hermione one last time and said a goodbye he could not let them hear as one (). Then he walked into the Forbidden Forest with the Resurrection Stone, the shades of his parents, Sirius and Lupin beside him, and let Voldemort kill him with the Elder Wand. The curse destroyed the piece of Voldemort’s soul inside him. His mother’s protection gave way to a death he had chosen freely, and held his soul at the threshold. His heart stopped. Narcissa Malfoy, sent to check the body, found no heartbeat and said so. At the threshold Harry held Dumbledore to account for his secrets, and was offered rest with his family beyond it. He chose to go back, because Dracaena was still in the castle.

Voldemort displayed his body in the courtyard and drowned the defenders in his darkness. Dracaena, whom her Mark spared it, broke it with her first Patronus. Neville killed Nagini, the last Horcrux, and Dracaena held the weakened Voldemort until he cut her down; her father died shielding her. Then Harry stood up (). The last flare of his mother’s protection carried him through the Death Eaters’ curses, and then it was spent. Inside a ring of the last Death Eaters he fought Voldemort for a long time, Dracaena’s wand against the Elder Wand, and told him what he was now: a man who would die like any other. Voldemort cast the Killing Curse; Harry answered with a Disarming Charm. Ron held the ring’s gap, and Hermione closed the wound in Dracaena’s arm. As Harry began to give, Dracaena rose and closed her hand over his. Together they drove the curse back into Voldemort, and the Elder Wand flew into Harry’s hand. Lord Voldemort died. Harry caught Dracaena as she collapsed and carried her to the healers himself ().

Aftermath (1998)

He sat beside her bed until she woke. Ron and Hermione came with small gifts and their own long-overdue apologies, and when they went down to the celebration Harry kissed Dracaena on the forehead and left her breathless (). With the Elder Wand he mended his holly wand, gave Dracaena her hawthorn back and raised the fallen walls of Hogwarts. Then he snapped it in two. At her trial Katie Bell testified against her. He testified for her, with Ron, Hermione, Neville and many others, that she had acted under a sentence of death on her family; she was found guilty under duress, and pardoned. No one from the Ministry stood trial at all (). At the ceremony for those who had ended the war he honored her before the whole school, spoke for Slytherin, and then turned on the Ministry of Magic itself, naming its cowardice and cruelty and the bondage of the house-elves, and telling the room that winning the war was not the same as fixing what had allowed it (). It made some powerful people very angry.

On the last day of school, lying in the grass beside Dracaena, he told her he was proud of her and asked her to make a fresh start with him somewhere new, on his parents’ inheritance. She kissed him before he could finish. On the train out of Hogwarts, with Ron and Hermione across the compartment, he told her he loved her (, ). His reckoning with the Ministry continues in .

Physical appearance

Harry is thin and was small for his age as a boy, a legacy of the Dursleys’ neglect. He has his father’s untidy jet-black hair, which refuses to lie flat however it is cut, and his mother’s bright green eyes, behind round glasses that he has worn since childhood and that cracked on the bathroom floor in his sixth year. The thin, lightning-bolt-shaped scar on his forehead is the most famous mark in the wizarding world; people look for it before they look at him, and he has spent his life flattening his fringe over it. He carries other scars from the war: faint words on the back of his hand from Umbridge’s quill, and the mark of the basilisk’s fang on his arm.

Personality and traits

Harry’s defining trait is a stubborn, inconvenient fairness. He takes the side of whoever is being wronged, whether that is Neville, a condemned Hippogriff, a freed house-elf, or the girl who had made his life miserable for three years when his friends laughed at her being slapped. He does not need people to deserve it. That instinct made him refuse Dracaena’s hand on the first day, and the same instinct, years later, made him unable to watch her be hurt.

He is brave in the plain, unglamorous way of someone who acts before he is sure he can win. He goes after the Stone, into the Chamber, into the Ministry and finally into the Forest because someone has to and no one else will. That courage has a reckless edge: he rarely waits for adults he has learned not to trust, and the habit cost him Sirius. He has a temper, a sharp tongue when cornered, and a capacity for brutal honesty that can wound as badly as any curse.

Raised without love, he gives it fiercely and holds onto it hard. He wanted no fame and no power. He was suspicious of both all his life, and of the institutions that dealt in them. Watching a government deny Voldemort’s return, torture children and buy its innocence at Sirius’s expense left him with a lasting conviction that the people in charge are not always the good ones. He carries guilt heavily, for Cedric, for Sirius, and above all for what Sectumsempra did to Dracaena, and he has a gift for making other people braver than they believed they could be.

What finally beat Voldemort was the thing Voldemort never understood: Harry’s willingness to die for the people he loved. He walked into the Forest not as a Horcrux doomed to it but as a boy choosing it, the way his mother once stepped in front of a curse for him.

Magical abilities and skills

Dueling

Harry is an outstanding duelist for his age, shaped by real fights rather than the classroom. His signature is the Disarming Charm, Expelliarmus, which he has used against Death Eaters and against Voldemort himself, and he relies on a small, dependable arsenal: Stupefy, Impedimenta and the Shield Charm, Protego. He wins by nerve, speed and refusing to give ground rather than by cruelty or flash.

Defense Against the Dark Arts

Defense is the one subject where Harry outshone everyone. In his fifth year he founded Dumbledore’s Army and taught a roomful of students to fight when no adult would, and most of them stood on the walls at the Battle of Hogwarts.

Patronus Charm

Harry learned to produce a corporeal Patronus at thirteen, far younger than most wizards ever manage, and the same year his stag drove off a swarm of Dementors at the lake. It is the shape his father took as an Animagus. He used it again to save himself and Dudley in Little Whinging, and to teach it to the members of Dumbledore’s Army.

Flying

A natural flier from the first time he touched a broom, Harry became the youngest house Seeker in a century. He outflew a Hungarian Horntail in the first Triwizard task, and later rode a half-blind dragon out of Gringotts.

Parseltongue

Harry can speak to snakes, a gift that came with the fragment of Voldemort’s soul inside him. It was as much a burden as a weapon: it convinced the school he was Slytherin’s heir, and it let him open the Chamber of Secrets.

The link with Voldemort

Through his scar Harry felt Voldemort’s emotions and, at times, saw through his eyes. Occlumency lessons with Snape only made it worse, and Voldemort used the link to lure him to the Department of Mysteries. The link died with the soul fragment in the Forbidden Forest.

Lily’s Ward

Lily Potter’s death sealed an ancient protection on her son, known as Lily’s Ward or, formally, Ultima Custodia. It would not let dark magic take Harry’s life. It burned Quirrell, kept him safe at Privet Drive until he came of age, quieted the Dark Mark on Dracaena’s arm whenever she was near him, and in the Forbidden Forest held his soul at the threshold of death. It gave way to that death only because he had chosen it freely, as she had chosen hers. Its last flare carried him through the Death Eaters’ curses in the courtyard, and then it was spent. Since the war Harry has been as mortal as anyone.

The Elder Wand

Harry won the Elder Wand when his Disarming Charm tore it out of Voldemort’s hand. It is the most powerful wand ever made and nothing more; the legend that it cannot be beaten was the one Voldemort believed. Harry used it to mend his own wand and to repair Hogwarts, and then snapped it.

Possessions

  • Wand: holly, with a phoenix feather core, eleven inches, nice and supple. Its feather’s twin is in Voldemort’s wand. It was broken at Godric’s Hollow in 1998 and mended with the Elder Wand after the war.
  • Dracaena’s wand: hawthorn, which she pressed into his hand at Malfoy Manor. He fought the final duel with it, and gave it back to her afterward.
  • The Invisibility Cloak: his father’s, given to him at his first Christmas, and in truth one of the three Deathly Hallows. Dracaena took it from him for a few weeks in his fourth year, and overheard through it the conversation that broke them apart.
  • The Marauder’s Map: made by his father and his father’s friends, given to him by the Weasley twins in his third year. It shows every person within the castle’s walls.
  • The Resurrection Stone: another Hallow, left to him by Dumbledore, which he used to walk with the dead into the Forbidden Forest, and let fall there.
  • The Elder Wand: won from Voldemort in the final duel, used to repair Hogwarts, and snapped.
  • Brooms: a Nimbus Two Thousand from McGonagall in his first year, and a Firebolt from Sirius in his third.
  • The two-way mirror: Sirius’s gift, which might have told him his godfather was safe, and which he never opened in time.
  • Inheritance: the Potter vault at Gringotts, and, from Sirius, 12 Grimmauld Place. He means to use the first to start a new life with Dracaena.
  • Hedwig: his snowy owl, Hagrid’s gift for his eleventh birthday.

Relationships

Family

James and Lily Potter

Harry has no memory of his parents beyond the sound of his mother screaming that the Dementors dragged up. He has met them in the Mirror of Erised, in the shape of his own Patronus, in the wand-echo in the graveyard, and finally as shades walking beside him into the Forest. His mother’s sacrifice is the reason he is alive and the model for the choice he made at the end.

Sirius Black

His godfather, his father’s best friend, and for two years the closest thing to family Harry ever had: a home that was not the Dursleys’, promised for one evening and taken away again. Sirius died at the Department of Mysteries, in part because Harry went there. Harry inherited his house, his name and his servant, and freed Kreacher in his honor.

The Dursleys

His aunt Petunia, uncle Vernon and cousin Dudley raised him because they had to and resented every day of it. They gave him a cupboard, cast-off clothes and the certainty that he was nothing. He never went back to them after the war.

Dracaena Malfoy

The girl whose handshake he refused on the first day, his sharpest rival for three years, and the person he came to love most. She reminded him at first of Dudley. Over time he learned to tell her pettiness from real malice and noticed how rarely, when it counted, she chose the malice. He apologized to her in Hogsmeade, would not laugh when she was struck, and at the Yule Ball saw the girl beneath the performance. The war put them on opposite sides, and he spent two years wishing he could reach her. He nearly killed her with a spell he had not questioned, and admitted to himself that night that he loved her. He reached for her hand on the Tower and was refused; in the end it was her hand that closed over his in the final duel. He carried her to the healers, testified at her trial, honored her before the school, and on the last day asked her to build a new life with him. He has never stopped telling her she is better than everything she was raised to be.

Ron Weasley

His first friend, met on the train, and the brother he chose. Ron’s loyalty has broken twice, over the Goblet of Fire and over the locket, and both times he came back. He is the one who closes the distance first after their quarrels, and the one Harry trusts to stand beside him.

Hermione Granger

The cleverest witch of their age, whose friendship he and Ron earned the hard way. Hermione was the first to see what Dracaena felt for Harry, and the one who devised the Yule Ball plan. She stood with him through every year of the war and is the only reason many of their plans worked at all.

Rubeus Hagrid

The man who came for him on a rock in the sea and opened the door to the wizarding world. Harry trusts and defends him without hesitation, through Norbert, the Chamber of Secrets and Buckbeak’s trial.

Albus Dumbledore

His headmaster and mentor. Dumbledore kept his distance in Harry’s fifth year, a mistake he later admitted, and in his sixth took him fully into his confidence. Harry learned only after Snape’s death that Dumbledore had known all along that Harry would have to die, and had raised him toward it: a kindness and a cruelty that can no longer be told apart. At the threshold of death Harry made him answer for it, and Dumbledore did not excuse himself.

Severus Snape

The Potions master who loathed him on sight, and whom Harry suspected of nearly everything. Snape saved Dracaena’s life on the bathroom floor, killed Dumbledore on the Tower, and died giving Harry the truth: that he had loved Harry’s mother to the last and had worked against Voldemort all along. Harry laid into him for years of abuse when he learned Snape was the Half-Blood Prince (), and Snape’s last words were an apology for it.

Neville Longbottom

The boy who stood up to Harry himself in their first year, and who, at the end, killed Voldemort’s snake with the sword of Gryffindor. The prophecy might have named either of them. Harry has never doubted Neville’s courage.

Dobby and Kreacher

Harry freed Dobby in his second year and Kreacher at the end of his fifth. Dobby died saving him at Malfoy Manor, free, and was mourned as no house-elf had ever been. Harry named the bondage of the elves at the victory ceremony, and the movement that would one day fight for them took Dobby’s dream as its name.

Lord Voldemort

The wizard who murdered his parents and spent seventeen years trying to kill him. Their wands are brothers, their souls were bound together, and the prophecy said one must die at the hand of the other. Voldemort saw Harry as an obstacle and never understood him. Harry, in the end, saw Voldemort clearly: not a god, but a man who had carved up his own soul for a power he never understood.

Across the worlds

Harry’s Champion synergies point to bonds formed beyond his own world: with Batman, orphaned by violence like him but walking a different road through grief (Grief’s Two Roads); with Link, another hero of prophecy (Heroes of Prophecy); and with Spider-Man, who also carried far too much far too young (Young Shoulders). His synergy with Dracaena says the dark tore apart the life they built and scattered them across the multiverse, and that finding her again is the promise he means to keep above every other. These stories belong to Worlds Entwined.

Etymology

Harry is a plain, traditional English name, and Potter one of the most ordinary English surnames, an occupational name for a maker of pots: an unremarkable name for a boy who believed he was no one. His middle name, James, is his father’s.

Changes from Canon

  • His rival Draco Malfoy is reimagined as , and their rivalry becomes the central love story of his life. He ends the war with Dracaena rather than Ginny Weasley.
  • He apologizes to Dracaena in Hogsmeade after pelting her with mud, and will not laugh when Hermione slaps her.
  • He takes Dracaena to the Yule Ball. Cho Chang is reimagined as Chao Zheng, and was forced to betray Dumbledore’s Army under Veritaserum.
  • After Sectumsempra he holds Dracaena as she bleeds, and burns the Half-Blood Prince’s book himself.
  • He frees Kreacher at the end of his fifth year, and Ron and Hermione stand with Hermione’s elf movement, the Elf Liberation Alliance, rather than joking about it.
  • Time-Turners act for a pure purpose only, and every one in the Ministry is destroyed at the Department of Mysteries.
  • He truly dies in the forest, holds Dumbledore to account at the threshold, and chooses to return to save Dracaena. Narcissa tells no lie.
  • The Elder Wand has no special rules. He fights the final duel with Dracaena’s wand, and her hand over his turns Voldemort’s curse.
  • He publicly condemns the Ministry of Magic at the victory ceremony, and leaves Hogwarts to start a new life with Dracaena. There is no “Nineteen Years Later”.
