Behind Closed Doors

Harry Potter Memory · The Wizarding World

Harry, face-first, hit hard stone. His glasses crashed against an unknown floor as the pain struck at once, a burning sensation on his nose and what felt like scrapes across his cheeks. He let out a cry and lay on the ground for a moment, trying to get his bearings before trying anything. Coughing and weak, he held back tears as he slowly picked himself off the ground. Through his blurred vision, he saw no one nearby and nothing familiar. It certainly wasn’t Diagon Alley, he realized, and he muttered to himself that he’d never use Floo powder again.

Where am I?

He coughed as he hovered his hands above the floor to find his glasses. When he finally did, he tried to put them on, and they tumbled right back onto the floor. Confused, he lifted them up once more, trying his best to examine them with his flawed eyes.

“Bloody hell…” he whispered to himself as he looked at his glasses. The bridge had been completely destroyed, and they would no longer sit on his face. Letting out a huff, Harry held them against his eyes, resorting to using the lenses to look around.

The place was unlike anything he’d ever seen before. Everything looked… wrong. Very, very wrong. It appeared to be some type of shop, its dim lighting unwelcoming and dreary. Various cult-like masks lined the walls with expressions of pain and pleasure. A glass case sat on a nearby counter containing a withered hand on a cushion, some bloodstained cards and a (hopefully) glass eye. Bones sat beside the case, seemingly human, as if they’d been tossed aside. Spiked weapons hung shakily from the ceiling, like they could fall at any moment.

Harry, worried he may not be alone, searched the place carefully, nearing the front door by the counter to attempt an exit. He glanced out the window to see a dark, narrow street. It was mostly empty but for two silhouettes in the distance, coming closer. One of them, Harry recognized immediately…

What are the bloody chances?!

Harry, now alert, looked around. He had no idea where the shopkeeper was, let alone why she was coming here. Rushing back to the fireplace he had fallen out of, soot still covering his clothes, Harry noticed a (mostly) empty black cabinet. He darted inside, his breathing heavy as he tried to calm himself, equally bothered by the fact he had to hide next to some mysterious-looking jars. Through the crack of the cabinet door, Harry could see just a little in front of him, the counter in full view.

The bell of the door rang, and in came the familiar face. Dracaena Malfoy, and what looked like… her father? It must’ve been; the man was very obviously wealthy, adorned in an aristocratic black suit unlike any Harry had ever seen. Green lined the sides of his sleeves, matching the green of his daughter’s cloak. His hair, long and just as blond, fell past his shoulders, and his face, sharp and pale, held the same silver eyes. Wielding a cane with a snake’s head that he didn’t seem to need, the man strutted to the counter as Malfoy followed close behind him, holding his hand. He waited there for a moment, a serious expression on his face that looked permanently etched, while Malfoy, her eyes already on the glass case, waltzed up to it and pressed her hands against it, her face lighting up in wonder.

“Dracaena, what did I tell you?” the man said gently. “Please do not touch anything.” Malfoy took her hands off the glass and nodded at her father, looking a little disappointed. The man stood there another moment as Malfoy looked around curiously, then cleared his throat.

“Ahem. Borgin?” the man called, tapping his cane against the ground. These rich folk are such an impatient lot, Harry thought, as he put his eye (and his broken glasses) closer to the crack.

“Ah, yes! Mr. Malfoy, my apologies!” the man named Borgin cried from a distance, and Harry heard him shuffling around. “My apologies again, sir, I had not realized it was you! What can I do for you?” Seeing him in the light now, Harry made out a greasy-haired man of small stature, his face untrimmed. “I’ve got loads of new things, some of which just came in—“

“Not buying, Borgin. I have some items I must rid myself of.” Mr. Malfoy spoke coldly, taking a parchment from his pocket with careful precision. “The Ministry has been conducting more raids than usual lately. On the off-chance of any trouble, I have a list of items I wish to sell.” Borgin nodded, taking hold of the paper as he began reading. “Why, I doubt they’d give you any trouble, Mr. Malfoy! They wouldn’t dare.”

“You’d be surprised,” Mr. Malfoy muttered, glancing at Malfoy, who was now examining a skull on the floor. “Those fools continue to grow more and more meddlesome. There’s even talk of a new Muggle Protection Act, which that rat Arthur Weasley is likely behind. The damned fool loves meddling where he doesn’t belong.” Harry frowned at his words as he thought of the care the Weasley family had shown him, anger rising in his heart.

No wonder Malfoy can be so mean, he thought. Her father is insufferable.

“Regardless, the poisons in particular are my primary concern,” Mr. Malfoy said, pointing to a mark on the parchment. “Though I expect you’ll compensate me well for such an offer?” They began to talk business, and Harry’s focus moved to Malfoy.

As if she hadn’t taken her father’s words into consideration at all, Malfoy was already inspecting another item, a hangman’s rope that she flipped around in her hands for a moment. When she set it back down, her eyes moved to an opal necklace, and she held up the card beside it. Her eyes widened as she read it, and she put the card back down and stepped away from it in worry.

Harry felt a knot tighten in his stomach; everything she examined brought her closer and closer to his cabinet. At this rate, she’d find him in no time. Harry took a deep breath and retreated slightly towards the back of the cabinet. To his shock and fear, it let out a slight bump, and Malfoy’s head swung towards him at once.

Damn!

Malfoy walked up quickly, confusion crossing her face, as she got closer and closer to the crack of the cabinet.

Harry could see her face clearly, and he prayed she couldn’t see his. His heart beat so fast he could hear it in his ears, and he held his hand over his mouth as she reached for the handle.

“Always a pleasure to serve you, Mr. Malfoy. And your daughter has become quite beautiful, sir—“ Borgin said in the background. Malfoy was now mere inches away as Harry held his breath, seconds from being caught.

“Excuse me?!” Mr. Malfoy yelled, his anger sparking in an instant as he grabbed hold of Borgin’s collar. Malfoy, in a panic, turned away from Harry’s hiding spot and towards her father. “You dare speak of my daughter in such a way, you fiendish cretin?!” Mr. Malfoy went on, his voice powerful and commanding. Malfoy dashed over to her father as Harry breathed a sigh of relief and put his eye back to the crack, more careful now to remain hidden, wiping the sweat from his forehead.

“I-I didn’t mean it like that, sir, please, I meant only respect—“ Borgin begged, waving his hands in protest. Mr. Malfoy drew his wand from the head of his cane and held it to the man’s throat, gritting his teeth like he was seconds from casting a spell.

What a maniac!

“Please, Father, stop!” Malfoy cried, yanking at his suit. Mr. Malfoy looked down, and at the sight of his daughter he seemed to calm himself. He slowly drew back his wand and let go of Borgin, who began to gasp for air. The sight caught Harry a little off guard; he was surprised she cared at all what happened to Borgin.

“You’d do best to be mindful of your words, especially when you’re speaking of my daughter,” Mr. Malfoy muttered, stroking Malfoy’s hair as she stood blinking with worry. “N-no, it was my fault, sir,” Borgin wheezed, his face red and sweating. “C-can I interest you in anything else while you’re here?”

Malfoy pointed at the hand in the glass case. “Can I have that? It looks interesting,” she asked, as Mr. Malfoy glanced at it in confusion. Borgin clapped his hands together. “Ah, the Hand of Glory! A wise choice, young Miss Malfoy. Should you lay a candle in the hand, it will light for you and only you, leaving the others in the dark. A thief’s dearest companion!” Borgin chuckled, his speech still shaky from the brief skirmish. Mr. Malfoy scowled. “My daughter will amount to more than a simple thief.” Borgin immediately backpedaled, his face shifting back to panic. “W-why, yes, of course! I apologize, sir, I didn’t mean to imply otherwise. Your daughter is destined for greatness, a true Slytherin at heart!”

“That she is, indeed… if she keeps her grades up,” Mr. Malfoy said quietly, as Malfoy looked up at him with a pout. “Can I please have it, Father? I don’t want to use it to steal at all, I promise!” she said, looking at the hand in awe. Harry couldn’t help but find it a little funny, and he smiled to himself.

“Like I said,” Mr. Malfoy began, “perhaps I’d consider it if your grades were higher.” Malfoy stomped her foot and crossed her arms. “But it’s not fair, Father! The teachers give special treatment to that foolish Granger!” she cried. Mr. Malfoy shook his head. “That’s no excuse to be inferior to a Mudblood. Her exam scores were higher than yours too, were they not? A Muggle-born should not be doing any better than you,” he said sternly.

Mudblood…? Harry had never heard the word before. What he was certain of, however, was that Mr. Malfoy was rotten. The Muggle-born comment made him think of Hermione, and his quiet anger at the man grew.

The comment seemed to upset Malfoy a little, and she went silent. Her gaze fell to the floor, an expression on her face Harry couldn’t quite place. When her father noticed, his demeanor changed, and he took a deep breath before he continued. “You’re already getting yourself a broom, young lady. Surely you don’t need this too?” He smiled genuinely at her, a look so unnatural on him that it creeped Harry out. Malfoy, unrelenting, looked up at her father. “But Potter already has a broom! And he even got a spot on their team!”

“Yes, I know, princess. You’ve told me, over and over. What did I tell you about speaking of that Potter boy?” Mr. Malfoy sighed, then blinked at the hand. “How much for the Hand of Glory?” Borgin, caught off guard, stuttered. “O-oh, well, for you, we can make it free! Anything for young Miss Malfoy.” Harry could practically see the sweat beading on his forehead; he clearly didn’t want to anger Mr. Malfoy again. Mr. Malfoy smirked and nodded towards it. “Then let it be so.”

In an instant, Malfoy grinned a wide grin and hugged her father’s side. “Oh, thank you, Father! Thank you, thank you, thank you!” She let out a squeal of delight that reminded Harry of when she was first sorted into Slytherin. Borgin fetched the hand from the case and handed it to her, and she held it in awe as her father smiled. “For this, you had better make sure those grades are high this year.” Malfoy nodded rapidly, flipping it around in her hands.

What kind of child wants something like that? Harry wondered. She… she truly is an enigma.

“Thank you kindly for your business, Mr. Malfoy. It is always a pleasure,” Borgin said, rubbing his hands together with a smile. Mr. Malfoy nodded coldly, his tone reverting at once to its usual chill, and took his daughter’s hand again. “Be at the Manor tomorrow by noon. Do not keep me waiting. Understood?” Borgin simply nodded as Mr. Malfoy turned and walked out, the excited Malfoy in tow. As soon as the door shut behind them, Borgin let out a long sigh and shuffled, mumbling, into the back of the shop once more.

As soon as Harry thought it was safe (and once he was certain that neither Malfoy would still be near), he burst out of the cabinet and ran out the door, clutching the lenses awkwardly to his face.

The street was no friendlier than the shop. Harry slowed his pace, afraid to draw attention to himself, and did his best to ignore the few wizards staring at him from a distance.

Even so, his mind kept wandering back to the shop. He tried to reconcile the girl who had seemed so excited to be friends with him on the train with the girl he had just watched: hugging her father’s side, begging for a dead man’s hand, going quiet at a word Harry didn’t know. She acted entirely differently with her father than he had ever seen her act, and there was a warmth between them he hadn’t expected from such a seemingly cruel man. Her father had nearly cursed a man for her. Harry didn’t know what to make of any of it.

“Arry!”

A familiar voice boomed down the alley, and Harry looked up to see Hagrid storming over. He grabbed Harry by the arm, sternly but carefully. “Boy, what the ell are yer doing ere?” Keeping his voice down now, he led Harry along a strange path between the shops, winding through one passage after another.

“I… I got lost using Floo powder. I’m sorry,” Harry said, turning to Hagrid, whose brow was set with concern and frustration. “I didn’t mean to, I swear. But… thank you, for showing up and helping me.”

Hagrid’s expression softened as he looked down at him, sunlight starting to emerge ahead of them. “It’s… it’s alright, Arry. I’m just glad yer safe. That spot is no place for a youngling like yer. Tell me yer’ll never go there again.”

“I don’t plan on it,” Harry sighed, still holding the lenses up to his face. Hagrid raised an eyebrow at them. “They hit the stone when I fell. Hit my nose too.”

“Blimey, Arry.” Hagrid shook his head. “And why didn’t yer answer any of my letters?”

“Well… I never got them. The Dursleys already hated it when I got anything, but a house-elf seemed to be keeping them from me, too.” Harry’s face fell as he thought of all the letters he’d likely never seen. Hagrid groaned, his face darkening. “Those blasted Muggles. I ought to ask Dumbledore why we left yer with such an awful sort.”

Before Harry could reply, the path beneath them turned to the marble of Gringotts. They were back in Diagon Alley.

“Harry! Harry!” Hermione was running towards him at full speed, and Harry grinned. “Hermione!” She crashed into him with a hug. “How’ve you been? I sent you letters, but… and what happened to your glasses?”

“Long story,” Harry sighed. Before he could begin it, a cluster of ginger hair came rushing over: Mr. Weasley, out of breath, with Fred, George and Ron behind him.

“We were looking all over for you! Where’d you wind up?” Ron asked, his voice laced with worry.

“Knockturn Alley,” Hagrid answered for him, patting Harry on the back.

“Are you kidding? That place rules!” Fred said, as George nodded in agreement. “Anything eventful, Harry?”

Harry considered it for a moment. He decided not to mention Malfoy, and shook his head as he wiped down a lens. “No. And I didn’t mean to end up there, I swear.”

Then Mrs. Weasley came hurrying over with Ginny by her side, and dragged him into a hug. “Oh, Harry! Harry, oh Lord! You’re okay!”

“It’s okay, it’s okay!” Harry laughed. “I’m okay.”

It’s nice to be cared about for once, he thought, smiling to himself.

When she finally let him go, Mr. Weasley held out a hand for the glasses, and with a flick of his wand they were whole again. Harry thanked him, and Hagrid, with a wave of one giant hand, headed off, promising to see them all at Hogwarts.

“Well, now that we’re all together again,” Mr. Weasley said, “let’s get shopping, shall we?”

Harry fell in beside Ron and Hermione once more. He let everything that had happened drift behind him, and calmed himself for what would, hopefully, turn out to be a normal day.
