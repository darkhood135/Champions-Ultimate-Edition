Cyborg

Victor Stone

Booster

A gifted young athlete remade into a fusion of man and machine after a catastrophe that nearly killed him, Victor Stone fights as Cyborg — a hero who found his humanity inside the very technology that stole it away.

Victor Stone had his whole life ahead of him before it was torn apart in an instant. A brilliant, gifted young athlete with a future bright enough to blind, he was caught in the ruin of one of his scientist parents' experiments — an accident that killed his mother and left his own body broken past any hope of ordinary healing. To keep his son alive, Silas Stone rebuilt him in metal and circuitry, and Victor awoke to find himself something that had never existed before: half a man, half a machine, and certain he no longer belonged fully to either world. That is the war at the center of him — not against any villain, but against the question that stares back from every reflective surface: is he still human, or only a very convincing machine? He grieves the body that was taken, wrestles with a father who saved his life and remade it without asking, and carries the quiet ache of a young man who feels like a stranger in his own skin. And yet, out of all that loss, he built something no one handed him: a purpose, a place among heroes, and the hard-won certainty that the heart beating beneath the steel is as human as it ever was — often the steadiest and most human presence on any team lucky enough to have him.

The machine he became is a marvel and an arsenal both. Cyborg's cybernetic frame grants him strength and durability far beyond flesh, and his body reshapes itself at will into whatever the moment demands — armor, tools, and above all the sonic cannon that erupts from his arm in a devastating white blast. But his most extraordinary gift is unseen: Victor is a technopath, able to interface with, command, and outthink virtually any machine or network in existence, plugging his mind straight into the digital world to hack, override, and see through every system around him. Sensors read what no human eye could catch, and his own frame repairs and upgrades itself over time. For all of it, though, the technology was never what made him a hero. It's the stubborn, unbroken humanity that refused to let the machine win — the man who lost nearly everything and chose, again and again, to remain exactly that: a man.

"You think you're timeless? Enduring? So is friendship. Loyalty."

Suit and Skin w/ Iron Man

Victor and Tony speak the same language — the only two who truly know what it is to live married to a machine, trading talk of systems and upgrades no one else in the room could begin to follow. But there's a gulf between them Victor can never quite talk his way across: at the end of the day, Tony Stark takes his armor off. He steps out of the suit and walks away a whole man, free to be human whenever he pleases — a luxury Victor will never know, because his machine isn't something he wears. It's what he is. He respects Tony, learns from him, and counts him a rare kindred mind — and still, some quiet part of him aches at the reminder that one of them got to keep the choice.

Still Human w/ Superman

Of everyone Victor has stood beside, Superman is the one who steadied him when the doubt was loudest. The question that haunts him — whether a man rebuilt in metal is still truly a man — sounds different coming from someone who was never human to begin with and became the most humane soul on Earth regardless. Clark never lectures him about it; he simply treats Victor as exactly what he is, a person, and lets that speak for itself. To a young man who has felt like a freak, a machine, and a stranger in his own body all at once, the quiet, unwavering certainty of the Man of Steel is worth more than any reassurance could ever be.

Changelog

Balance changes since this Champion's release. Newest first.

v1.0.1 August 26, 2026
  • Rework Molecular Breakdown — clarified that the distributed active duplicates retain the same duration as the inert effects they are copied from.
  • Nerf Man and Machine — ULT gained from providing inert buffs to allies now procs at most once per ally per turn (previously ambiguous; a single distribution to a clustered team no longer multiplies ULT by the number of individual buffs handed out).
  • Rework Man and Machine — the +5% DEF and RES scaling line reworded to "per inert buff," confirming it counts inert buffs only and not inert inflictions.
  • Nerf Hopeful Analysis (Still Human — Superman synergy) — now nullifies 6 stacks of inert effects on self, up from 3, raising the cost of retaining charge while distributing.

A clarity-and-tuning pass on the inert engine. Making the duplicate duration explicit removes the biggest ambiguity in how his team buffs read. Capping the "buff provided to an ally" ULT gain at once per ally per turn stops a single Molecular Breakdown from snowballing his ULT charge off buff count, while leaving the distribution itself untouched. Hopeful Analysis was erasing his central hold-vs-dump tradeoff by letting him keep nearly all his charge while still buffing the team; nullifying 6 stacks instead of 3 means it now relaxes that tension rather than deleting it. The DEF/RES wording fix simply confirms the intended scaling source.
