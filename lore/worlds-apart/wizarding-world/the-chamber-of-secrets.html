The Chamber of Secrets is the second story arc of in . It covers ’s second year at , when an old legend comes true: the Chamber of Secrets is opened, students are found petrified, and suspicion falls on Harry himself. Behind it is a diary planted by and the memory of the boy who became .

Story

Privet Drive

The summer after his first year was a return to the narrow, unwanted life Harry Potter had been so glad to leave. His friends never wrote, and he began to wonder whether Hogwarts wanted him back at all, until a trembling, wide-eyed house-elf named Dobby appeared in his bedroom. Something terrible was being planned at the school, Dobby told him, and Harry must not go back. He had been stealing Harry’s letters to make him believe no one cared. When Harry refused, Dobby wrecked the Dursleys’ dinner party with a floating cake to force the point. A letter from the Ministry of Magic arrived within minutes, warning Harry that any more magic would see him expelled. Harry hadn’t cast a spell. Vernon put bars on his window.

The Burrow

He was rescued by friends rather than by magic. Ron and the twins, Fred and George, arrived in their father’s enchanted car in the dead of night, tore the bars off the window and flew him to the Burrow, the Weasleys’ crooked, cluttered, gloriously ordinary home. It was the first place that had ever felt to Harry like a family. Molly Weasley shouted at her sons for the car and fed Harry until he could barely move. Arthur Weasley, who worked at the Ministry looking after bewitched Muggle objects and plainly adored them, wanted to know everything about plugs. Nobody at the Burrow seemed to mind the yellow-and-black scarf on Ron’s bedpost half as much as Ron did. And the youngest Weasley, Ginny, about to start at Hogwarts herself, went scarlet and fled whenever Harry walked into a room.

Flourish and Blotts

The new year’s shopping brought everyone to Diagon Alley, where the bookshop Flourish and Blotts was mobbed for a signing by the celebrity author Gilderoy Lockhart, who spotted Harry in the crowd and dragged him into the photographs. Dracaena was there too, with her father. Whatever had passed between her and Harry on the train home, she acted as though it never had. She needled him and Ron, began to ease off when the rest of the Weasleys closed ranks around their brother, and then Lucius Malfoy swept in, and a few cold words from him put her claws back out. It was Harry’s first sight of Lucius: elegant, contemptuous, hateful on the instant. In the ease with which one word from him brought his daughter back into line, Harry read nothing warmer than control, and would go on reading it that way for a long time. Lucius and Arthur came to blows among the shelves. In the scuffle, no one saw Lucius slip a small black diary into Ginny’s cauldron.

The Flying Car

On the first of September the barrier to the hidden platform sealed itself in front of Harry and Ron. They made the reckless choice to chase the train in the flying car, and crashed it into the vicious tree that guarded the castle grounds. Ron’s wand snapped in the wreck and backfired for the rest of the year, and the car fled into the Forbidden Forest. They escaped expulsion by a whisker. The next morning a Howler from Molly screamed Ron’s disgrace across the whole Great Hall, and the Hufflepuff table, which had barely got used to a Weasley in its ranks, listened to every word.

Ginny was sorted into Gryffindor, the house Ron had been sure would be his. So was a small, starstruck first-year named Colin Creevey, who followed Harry everywhere with a camera.

Mudblood

Lockhart proved a vain fraud from his first lesson, when he loosed a cage of Cornish pixies on the class and fled; Hermione defended him for weeks anyway. Dracaena, meanwhile, had become Slytherin’s Seeker after her father bought the whole team new brooms. When the Slytherins swaggered onto the pitch to flaunt them, Hermione cut through the display: Gryffindor’s players had earned their places rather than paid for them. Dracaena glanced at her watching housemates, felt them waiting for her to put Hermione in her place, and after a hesitation that cost her visibly, forced out the ugliest word she knew. It had never really been hers. It was her father’s word, and her house’s, and the shame of it curdled in her even as she said it. Hermione went very still. Ron tried to curse Dracaena for it, and the spell rebounded off his broken wand and left him heaving up slugs; Dracaena fled in tears. In Hagrid’s hut, with Ron still retching into a basin, Hagrid explained what the word meant and told Hermione there wasn’t a spell she couldn’t do. Harry, who had now looked Lucius Malfoy in the eye, found the whole scene unsettled him more than it satisfied him.

Halloween

On Halloween Harry and his friends went to the five-hundredth deathday party of Nearly Headless Nick, the Gryffindor ghost. On the way back Harry heard a cold, murderous voice moving inside the walls, a voice no one else could hear. They followed it to a corridor where the caretaker’s cat hung stiff and petrified beneath a message daubed on the stone: the Chamber of Secrets had been opened, and the enemies of its heir should beware. The three of them were found standing over it.

The legend behind the message came out slowly. A thousand years before, Salazar Slytherin, one of the four founders of the school, had believed magic should be kept from children of non-magical families. He was said to have built a hidden chamber within the castle and sealed a monster inside, to be loosed one day by his true heir and finish the purge he never could.

The Bludger

The dread sat oddly beside the ordinary rhythms of school, among them the season’s first Quidditch match, Gryffindor against Slytherin, Harry and Dracaena matched Seeker to Seeker. A Bludger bewitched to hunt Harry alone battered him the length of the match and shattered his arm. For a moment, seeing the pain he was in, Dracaena faltered, and her taunts came out uncertain, almost soft, before her pride hardened them back into a mockery she half hated herself for. Harry caught the Snitch one-handed, then endured Lockhart’s attempt to mend the arm, which removed the bones from it entirely. That night, in the hospital wing, Dobby confessed that the sealed barrier and the Bludger had both been his, desperate attempts to send Harry home. A few beds away, Colin Creevey lay petrified, his camera melted in his hands.

The Dueling Club

In the fearful weeks that followed, Lockhart convened a dueling club, and Harry was set against Dracaena. She conjured a serpent, and it turned on a Hufflepuff boy, Justin Finch-Fletchley. Harry called it off without thinking, by speaking to it in the hissing tongue of snakes, and the hall recoiled. To everyone watching, Harry had set it on Justin. Dracaena didn’t crow. She found herself frightened for him: sure he had not done the terrible things himself, and afraid that something dark had sunk its hooks into a boy she was realizing, against all sense, she rather liked as he was.

Days later Justin was found petrified beside the blackened, frozen ghost of Nearly Headless Nick, and Harry was found standing over them both. Hufflepuff turned on him. Ernie Macmillan said in front of the whole house what most of them were thinking, that Potter was the heir of Slytherin and Justin had been next on his list. Ron told his housemates they were wrong, and kept telling them, at a table where he was already the odd one out. Neville, who had stood in Harry’s way once and had no intention of standing in it again, went on sitting next to him in lessons when half of Gryffindor wouldn’t. From that day Harry walked the corridors feared and shunned.

Polyjuice

Determined to find the real culprit, Hermione brewed a forbidden potion in the bathroom no one used, the haunt of a weeping ghost called Moaning Myrtle. Over Christmas it let Harry and Ron take the shapes of Crabbe and Goyle and slip into the Slytherin common room to question Dracaena. She gave herself away as no one’s mastermind: she didn’t know who the heir was and wished she did, and offered only a resigned, callous hope that if the monster took anyone, it might as well be the Granger girl. What Harry saw behind the bluster was a lonely girl performing for companions who weren’t really friends. They came away sure of one thing: whoever had opened the Chamber, it wasn’t Dracaena Malfoy. Hermione’s own dose, brewed with a hair from a cat, left her in the hospital wing for weeks, covered in fur.

The Diary

In Myrtle’s flooded bathroom Harry found a blank old diary that someone had tried to flush away. When he wrote in it, it wrote back. It belonged to Tom Riddle, a student from fifty years before, and it drew Harry into a memory of the Chamber’s last opening, when Riddle had caught the culprit: a young Hagrid and a monstrous pet spider. Harry’s suspicions turned, painfully, toward his friend. Weeks later someone tore apart the Gryffindor boys’ dormitory, and the diary was gone.

Then the attacks struck close. Hermione was found petrified beside a Ravenclaw prefect, Penelope Clearwater, a small mirror in her hand and a torn library page crushed in her fist. Percy Weasley turned grey at the news, and his brothers learned why: Penelope was the girlfriend he had kept secret all year. With the school on the brink of closing, the Minister for Magic, Cornelius Fudge, came for Hagrid, on nothing more than the old suspicion from fifty years before. There was no trial. Fudge said himself that he had no real evidence, only a need to be seen doing something, and Hagrid was taken to Azkaban that same night. Lucius Malfoy came with him, bearing an order from the school governors, whom he had threatened one by one, removing Dumbledore from the castle.

The Spiders

Hagrid had left a last clue on his way out of the hut: follow the spiders. Harry and Ron followed them deep into the Forbidden Forest, Ron white with terror the whole way, and found the ancient, enormous Aragog. Hagrid had been innocent all along, the spider told them. The thing in the Chamber was the one creature spiders feared, and a girl had died in a bathroom the last time it was loosed. Then Aragog gave his children leave to eat them. The Weasleys’ car, gone wild in the forest, came crashing through the trees and carried them out.

Ginny

In the hospital wing they prised the page from Hermione’s fist. Her research had cracked it. The monster was a basilisk, a giant serpent whose gaze killed outright, moving unseen through the castle’s pipes, and every victim had met it only in reflection: Mrs. Norris in a puddle, Colin through his camera, Justin through Nick, Hermione and Penelope in a mirror. The girl who had died was Moaning Myrtle, and the Chamber’s entrance lay in her bathroom. But before they could tell anyone, the worst message of all appeared on the wall. Ginny had been taken down into the Chamber itself.

She had spent the whole year pouring her heart into the diary: her shyness, her loneliness, her brothers who had their own friends, and the famous boy she couldn’t speak to. Ron, at another table in another house, hadn’t been close enough to see her fading. No one else had looked either. The diary had fed on her, blurred her memory, and turned every suspicion away from a quiet first-year.

Harry and Ron dragged Lockhart down with them, and at the entrance he showed himself for what he was. He had never done a single thing in his books. He had stolen other wizards’ deeds and wiped their memories. His last cowardly attempt to wipe theirs backfired off Ron’s broken wand, erased his own mind instead, and brought the tunnel down between the two boys. Harry went on into the Chamber alone.

The Chamber

There he found Ginny cold and fading, and the memory of Tom Riddle grown almost solid on the life he had drained from her. Riddle revealed himself at last as the boy who would become Lord Voldemort, his own name rearranged into the title he would one day make the world afraid to speak. Ginny had not been entirely his. Some buried part of her had fought him all year, and that resistance had pulled the serpent’s strikes a fraction wide every time, so that each victim was frozen rather than killed. Riddle loosed the basilisk. Help came on wings: Dumbledore’s phoenix, Fawkes, brought the old Sorting Hat and blinded the serpent, and from the Hat Harry drew a gleaming goblin-made sword. He drove it through the monster’s skull, with one of its venomous fangs already buried in his arm. With that same fang he stabbed the diary through, and Riddle, screaming, unravelled into nothing as Ginny woke. The phoenix’s tears drew the venom out of Harry’s arm before it could kill him.

Aftermath

In Dumbledore’s office, restored to the school by the governors’ panic, Harry asked the question that had followed him all year. The Hat had wanted Slytherin for him; he spoke to snakes; Riddle had said they were alike. Did he belong in Slytherin after all? Dumbledore told him that some of Voldemort’s own powers had passed into Harry the night he got his scar. The Hat could have sent him to Slytherin, and had chosen Gryffindor; only a true Gryffindor could have drawn that sword from it. What made a person who they were, the headmaster said, was not what they could do but what they chose to do with it, and Harry had chosen to go down into the Chamber.

Lucius Malfoy arrived in a fury with Dobby cowering at his heels, and the pieces came clear. It was Lucius who had slipped the diary into Ginny’s things, meaning to reopen the old terror and ruin Arthur Weasley in the doing. Harry, with a little cunning, slipped a sock inside the ruined diary and handed it back. Lucius tossed it aside, Dobby caught it, and Dobby was free. Lucius was stripped of his seat on the board of governors. The petrified were revived, Hermione among them. Hagrid came home from Azkaban, and no one in the Ministry said they were sorry. Hufflepuff, which had spent half the year shunning Ron for standing by Harry, apologized to them both, Ernie Macmillan first.

For Dracaena there was only the sour taste of her father brought low in public, by Potter, once more. Lucius came home from the castle in a cold fury that the whole house felt, except her. And the news that Dobby was free, which should have meant nothing to her, left her oddly glad. She had been the only one in the house who ever treated him kindly. And though she would never have admitted it, she was left with a whole year of small moments she could not square with the enemy her father insisted Harry was.

Changes from Canon

  • With sorted into , Justin Finch-Fletchley is his housemate. When Hufflepuff turns on Harry after the dueling club, Ron stands with Harry against his own house.
  • Ron wasn’t in Ginny’s house, and so wasn’t close enough to see the diary draining her.
  • Hagrid is sent to Azkaban without a trial, on Fudge’s own admission that he has no real evidence. It is the first injustice in the Ministry’s long record in this continuity.
  • The basilisk’s victims survive because they meet its gaze only in reflection, and because Ginny’s buried resistance pulls its strikes wide.
  • Because the Sorting Hat chose Gryffindor for Harry on its own, Dumbledore’s answer to Harry’s fear that he belongs in Slytherin rests on the Hat’s choice, the sword, and Harry’s choice to go into the Chamber.
  • The serpent at the dueling club is ’s, and she comes away frightened for Harry rather than triumphant ().
  • The sword of Gryffindor is described as goblin-made, in keeping with this continuity’s goblins.
