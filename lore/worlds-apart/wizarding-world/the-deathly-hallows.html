The Deathly Hallows is the seventh story arc of in . It covers the year , and spend hunting the last of Voldemort’s while the Dark Lord rules Britain through a fallen Ministry, and lives under his roof. It ends in the Battle of Hogwarts, where Harry dies and returns, and he and Dracaena defeat Voldemort together.

Story

The Dark Lord’s House

When the Dementors deserted Azkaban for the Dark Lord, Lucius Malfoy came home a broken man, and found his house was no longer his. Voldemort had made Malfoy Manor his headquarters. He held his councils at the Malfoys’ own table, took Lucius’s wand from him in front of his followers, and kept Dracaena seated among them, branded and silent, so that her parents would never forget what their daughter’s life was worth. At one such council he murdered Charity Burbage, the Muggle Studies professor, over the table and fed her to his snake, and Snape, the hero of the Tower, told him the night Harry Potter would leave his aunt’s house.

The Seven Potters

Harry’s mother’s protection on Privet Drive would end on his seventeenth birthday, and the Order of the Phoenix meant to have him gone before then. The Dursleys left first, under guard. At the door Dudley, awkward and sincere, told his cousin that he didn’t think he was a waste of space, and shook his hand.

Six of Harry’s friends drank Polyjuice Potion and became six more Harrys, each flown out beside an Order member, so that the Death Eaters waiting in the sky would not know which one to chase. They chased all of them. Hedwig was killed in her cage. Mad-Eye Moody died when Mundungus Fletcher panicked and fled from beside him. George Weasley lost an ear to a curse from Snape’s wand. Voldemort himself came for the real Harry on Hagrid’s motorbike, carrying Lucius’s borrowed wand to get around the wands that would not fight each other, and Harry’s own wand spat golden fire on its own and shattered it. Harry reached the Burrow alive. Voldemort went back to the Manor wanting a wand that nothing could stand against.

The Wedding

Bill Weasley married Fleur Delacour at the Burrow at the end of the summer. Before it, the new Minister, Rufus Scrimgeour, came in person to hand over what Dumbledore had left them in his will: a Deluminator for Ron, a children’s book of wizarding tales for Hermione, and for Harry the Snitch he had caught in his first match. Dumbledore had left Harry the sword of Gryffindor too, and Scrimgeour would not give it up. At the wedding, Luna’s father Xenophilius Lovegood wore a pendant carved with the same sign Luna had worn and the Gaunt ring had borne, and Viktor Krum, a guest, wanted to fight him for it: it was the mark Grindelwald’s followers had carved into the walls of his school.

Then a silver lynx crashed into the dancing and spoke in Kingsley Shacklebolt’s voice. The Ministry had fallen. Scrimgeour was dead. They were coming. The Ministry fell from within, to a Minister under the Imperius Curse, and kept its desks, its laws and its seal. Abroad, nothing seemed to have happened at all. Hermione had already sent her parents to Australia believing they had no daughter. She Disapparated with Harry and Ron in the middle of the panic, and when two Death Eaters found them in a Muggle café that same night, Ron wanted to kill them. Harry would not let him. Hermione wiped their memories and they left them asleep in the booth.

Grimmauld Place

They hid in Sirius’s house. On the door of a bedroom on the top floor was a small plaque bearing the name of Regulus Arcturus Black, Sirius’s younger brother, who had joined the Death Eaters as a boy and died at eighteen: R.A.B., who had taken the real locket from the cave. Hermione remembered the heavy locket none of them could open when they cleared the house two summers before. It was gone.

Kreacher, who might have known where, had left the night Harry freed him, and no one knew where he had gone. There was only one elf Harry could ask. He called for Dobby, and Dobby came. Three days later Dobby dragged Mundungus Fletcher into the kitchen by the ankle. Mundungus had stolen the locket from the house after Sirius died and lost it in Diagon Alley to Dolores Umbridge, who took it as a bribe. How Regulus had found the locket, how he had died, and how it had come home, nobody living could tell them.

Lupin came to the house one night wanting to come with them, and leave his pregnant wife behind. Harry told him his child needed a father more than they needed him. Lupin went home to Tonks.

The Ministry

Umbridge sat now at the head of the Muggle-born Registration Commission, which tried witches and wizards for having stolen their magic. The three of them went into the Ministry in the bodies of three of its employees, under the new statue in the atrium of Muggles crushed beneath a throne. In a courtroom Harry found Umbridge sentencing a frightened woman named Mary Cattermole, the locket on Umbridge’s chest. He stunned her, took it, and they freed the prisoners waiting to be tried, but a Death Eater caught Hermione’s arm as they Disapparated, and Grimmauld Place was lost to them. Ron was splinched in the escape.

The Locket

They could not destroy it. They had no sword and no fang, and the locket fed on whoever wore it. Through the autumn they moved from wood to hillside to riverbank, living in a tent on whatever they could find, while the radio named the dead and Hermione read and reread the book of tales. The locket found the weakest place in each of them. In Ron it found the boy who had not made Gryffindor, the sixth Weasley son with nothing his own, frightened for his sister at Hogwarts and sure that Harry and Hermione would be better off without him. One night in the rain Ron said so, and left.

At Christmas Harry and Hermione went to Godric’s Hollow. Harry stood at his parents’ grave for the first time. In the same churchyard was an older stone, for an Ignotus Peverell, cut with the sign from Xenophilius’s pendant. Old Bathilda Bagshot led Harry upstairs, and she was not Bathilda. She was Voldemort’s snake, waiting inside her. They escaped by a breath, and Hermione’s curse, rebounding in the struggle, broke Harry’s holly wand in two.

Rita Skeeter’s new book on Dumbledore was everywhere that winter. It was cruel and it was not all lies. As a young man Dumbledore had been the closest friend of Gellert Grindelwald, and had dreamed with him of wizards ruling Muggles for their own good, until a quarrel left his sister Ariana dead. He had never told Harry any of it. Harry, holding a broken wand in a frozen tent, could not decide whether he had ever really known the man.

The Silver Doe

A silver doe led Harry through the snow one night to a frozen pool in the Forest of Dean. The sword of Gryffindor lay at the bottom. He went in after it, and the locket around his neck tightened to drown him. Ron pulled him out. The Deluminator had brought him back, carrying the sound of Hermione saying his name. He had been trying to find them for weeks.

Harry opened the locket and gave Ron the sword. What came out of it spoke in Ron’s worst fears: his mother’s favorite was someone else, the girl he loved wanted someone else, and he was the Weasley who couldn’t even make Gryffindor, too ordinary for his own family and too weak to be anyone’s friend. Ron drove the sword through it. He had chosen his friend and he had come back, and when Hermione had finished being furious with him, she forgave him too.

The Tale of the Three Brothers

They went to Xenophilius Lovegood to ask about the sign. He showed them it was the mark of the , from a tale in Hermione’s book. Three brothers cheated Death, who gave them gifts: a wand that could not be beaten, a stone that called back the dead, and a cloak that hid its wearer even from Death himself. The eldest brother boasted of his unbeatable wand and was murdered in his sleep for it. The second called back the woman he loved and could not bear what came back. The youngest hid beneath his cloak until he was old, and greeted Death as a friend.

Harry already owned the cloak, and had since his first Christmas at Hogwarts. The stone had been set in the Gaunt ring. And Voldemort, he was sure, was hunting the wand. Xenophilius had kept them talking because the Death Eaters had taken Luna off the Hogwarts Express at Christmas, and he had called them in to trade the three of them for her. They escaped as his house exploded.

That spring, in a vision, Harry watched Voldemort find Grindelwald in the prison tower he had built for his enemies and kill him there. Then he saw Voldemort open Dumbledore’s white tomb and take from the dead man’s hands. Harry let him have it. Dumbledore had sent them after the Horcruxes, not the Hallows.

Hogwarts Under Snape

That year Snape was headmaster of Hogwarts, the Death Eaters Alecto and Amycus Carrow taught the Dark Arts and Muggle Studies, and attendance was compulsory for every child of the right blood. Amycus taught the Cruciatus Curse by having students practice it on those serving detention, and Crabbe and Goyle took to it gladly. Dracaena kept her head down, did the least she could, and never once turned it on a student.

Neville led the resistance. With Ginny and Luna he re-formed , painted its name on the castle walls at night, and took the Carrows’ beatings for every student he stood in front of, until he had to go into hiding in the Room of Requirement. By spring the Room held dozens of students from Gryffindor, Ravenclaw and Hufflepuff, living there, fed through a passage from the Hog’s Head.

At Christmas the Death Eaters feasted at the Manor. Over the wine, Goyle’s father boasted of the Tower night, and of the half-blood girl he had killed in the corridor: his boy had told him exactly where she would be, and Crabbe’s boy had told his son. Dracaena sat at the table and heard how Ada had died, and who had arranged it.

Malfoy Manor

Voldemort had laid a curse on his own name, so that anyone who spoke it could be found. Harry spoke it at Easter, and Snatchers caught all three of them. Hermione stung Harry’s face out of shape before they were taken, and they were dragged to Malfoy Manor, where Dracaena was home for the holidays.

Bellatrix saw the sword of Gryffindor among their things. It should have been in her vault at Gringotts, and her terror of what the Dark Lord would do if it had been stolen from her was greater than her terror of anything else. She sent the boys to the cellar and tortured Hermione on the drawing-room floor to learn where they had found it. Dracaena was made to stand and watch.

Then they held the swollen, disguised boy up before her and demanded she name him as Harry Potter. One word would have ended the war. The Mark on her arm, which had burned at every summons for a year, was quiet. She looked at him and said she could not be sure.

In the cellar were Luna, the wandmaker Ollivander, Dean Thomas and the goblin Griphook, who told Bellatrix the sword was a fake. Harry called for Dobby, and Dobby came. When Wormtail came down to see what the noise was, Harry reminded him he owed him his life, and Wormtail hesitated, and the silver hand Voldemort had given him closed on his own throat. In the fight in the drawing room Dracaena pressed her hawthorn wand into Harry’s hand. Dobby dropped the chandelier and took them all away, and Bellatrix’s knife followed him through the air. Dobby died in Harry’s arms on the sand outside Shell Cottage, a free elf.

Harry dug his grave by hand, without magic. The Elf Liberation Alliance would later be known by another name, Dobby’s Dream.

At the Manor Dracaena told them Potter had torn the wand from her, and no one doubted it. That night her mother gave Dracaena her own wand and asked her nothing at all.

Gringotts

At Shell Cottage, Ollivander told Harry that the Elder Wand was a wand like any other: the most powerful ever made, but its legend had always been larger than it was. Bellatrix’s terror told Harry the rest. Something of Voldemort’s was in her vault. Griphook agreed to get them in for one price, the sword, the work of goblin hands that wizards had carried for a thousand years as if it were their own.

Hermione went into Gringotts as Bellatrix. Griphook dealt with his own people himself. In the vault, where every treasure they touched multiplied and burned, they found Helga Hufflepuff’s cup. Then Griphook took the sword and raised the alarm, as any goblin would have, and the three of them broke out of the bank on the back of the blind dragon that had guarded it, setting it free.

The theft told Voldemort that Harry knew his secret, and in his rage Harry saw where the last of them were hidden. One was at Hogwarts.

The Return to Hogwarts

They came through the Hog’s Head, where Dumbledore’s brother Aberforth told them about Ariana and asked Harry why he still trusted the man, and Neville led them up the passage into the Room of Requirement to a roar of welcome. Within the hour Harry was in Ravenclaw Tower with Hermione and Luna, and Hermione had won from the house ghost, the Grey Lady, the secret of her mother’s lost diadem. Ron, alone, went down into the Chamber of Secrets, opened it in a fair imitation of Harry’s Parseltongue, and destroyed Helga Hufflepuff’s cup himself with a basilisk fang.

McGonagall drove Snape from the school. Then Voldemort’s voice filled every room in the castle, offering to spare them all for Harry Potter. Pansy Parkinson told everyone to hand him over. The houses gave their answer instead. The underage were sent away through the Hog’s Head, but the rest of the school stayed, all four houses on the walls together: Ron at the head of the Hufflepuffs and Hermione the Ravenclaws, the Gryffindors behind McGonagall, and more Slytherins than anyone would have believed. The Order came, and Hogsmeade, and Percy Weasley, who apologized to his family in the middle of a corridor and was forgiven.

The Room of Requirement

Dracaena already knew where the thing was. For a year, mending the Cabinet in the Room of Hidden Things, she had felt the dark coming off a tarnished diadem on an old bust, cold with the same magic that burned in her Mark. Bellatrix had boasted at Easter that the Dark Lord trusted her with a treasure as he trusted no one else, and kept others where no one would ever think to look. Dracaena went to destroy it, and Crabbe and Goyle followed her in.

They meant to stop her and they meant to make her pay, and she knew now what they had done to Ada. She beat Goyle back and would not kill him. Crabbe reached for a fire he could not hold. The Fiendfyre swallowed the room and both of them, and the diadem with them, and in the heart of it Dracaena glimpsed an old mirror showing her herself and Harry before the flames took it. Harry, Ron and Hermione had come for the diadem as well. Harry pulled her out of the fire. No one was left alive who could tell the Dark Lord what she had done.

When they were clear, Ron said the kitchen elves should be warned to get out before they died for wizards too. Hermione kissed him.

The Battle of Hogwarts

The Death Eaters broke the walls. Fred Weasley died laughing at Percy’s joke as the wall they stood behind came down. Lupin and Tonks died on the grounds, leaving a son, Teddy, born that spring, to whom Harry was godfather. Colin Creevey had sneaked back in to fight and died too. Dracaena slipped away from the fighting to find her parents in the dark before the night took them, and could not find them. They were in the forest, at the Dark Lord’s side.

At midnight Voldemort called off his followers and gave the castle an hour to give up Harry Potter. He had learned that night from his goblins that the sword in Bellatrix’s vault was a forgery, and he knew who had sent it there. He summoned Snape to the Shrieking Shack and had the snake kill him. Harry, hidden in the tunnel, reached Snape as he was dying. Snape gave him his memories, and the only apology he ever made: for the boy he had spent six years punishing for another man’s face.

In the Pensieve Harry saw it all. Snape had loved Lily Evans from boyhood until he died. He had begged Dumbledore to save her, and served Dumbledore ever since, from inside the Death Eaters. The ring’s curse had been killing Dumbledore, and he had ordered his own death at Snape’s hand. And on the night Voldemort killed Lily, a fragment of his soul had lodged in her son. Harry was a Horcrux. To end Voldemort, he had to let Voldemort kill him. Dumbledore had known it for years and told no one, and had raised him toward it.

The Forest

Harry did not run from it. Under the Cloak he found Ron and Hermione, and said goodbye without letting them hear it, and told Neville that the snake had to die. Then he walked into the forest. The Snitch opened at his lips, and inside was the stone from the Gaunt ring. He turned it, and his mother and father, Sirius and Lupin walked beside him to the clearing. There he let it fall.

Voldemort raised the Elder Wand and killed him. Lily’s protection would not let dark magic take her son’s life, but it gave way to a death he had freely chosen, as hers had been chosen. The curse destroyed the fragment of Voldemort’s soul inside him, and Voldemort fell as well. Harry’s heart stopped. His soul was held at the threshold.

Voldemort sent Narcissa to see whether the boy was dead. She knelt over him, found no heartbeat and no breath, and said so. Hagrid, bound and weeping, was made to carry the body back to the castle.

In a place full of white light that looked like King’s Cross station, Harry found Dumbledore waiting. Harry made him answer for all of it: the secrets, Ariana and Grindelwald, the Hallows he had hidden behind riddles, the boy he had raised for slaughter. Dumbledore did not excuse himself. He said he was sorry, and that the choice was Harry’s now. Beyond the light was his family, and rest. Harry chose to go back. Dracaena was still in the castle.

The Darkness

Voldemort laid Harry’s body on the stones before the castle and told them their hero was dead. Then he let the whole weight of his power go. It cost him more than he had left to spend, and he spent it anyway. The dark of him poured over the courtyard into the souls of everyone fighting, until the strongest of them could not stand or lift a wand, and the defenders went down where they stood. Only those who bore his Mark were spared it. Among the fallen defenders Dracaena was the only one left on her feet.

Voldemort saw her and called her forward to laugh at her: the girl who could not kill an old man, the heir of a disgraced house, standing among his enemies. Her Mark burned as he called her, and went quiet as she looked at Harry’s body on the stones. She raised her wand and fired. He swatted the spell aside and asked her what she thought she was doing. She told him he had taken Ada, and Harry, and the whole of her life, and she would rather die on her feet than kneel to him one more hour. As the killing light gathered, she thought of Ada, and of Harry, and cast her first Patronus: a silver peacock that tore out of her mother’s wand and drove the dark back off the courtyard. All across the grounds the fallen breathed again, and stood.

The Last Stand

Neville stood first. Voldemort set the Sorting Hat on his head and set it on fire, and Neville drew the sword of Gryffindor out of the flames. The goblin-made sword came to the Hat as it always had for whoever needed it, and with one stroke Neville cut off the snake’s head. It was the last of the Horcruxes, and the change in Voldemort was plain to see. He was no god now, only a man.

The battle came roaring back. The centaurs came out of the forest, Grawp waded in, Slughorn returned with the families of Hogsmeade behind him, and the kitchen elves charged out of the castle with carving knives, crying Dobby’s name. On the stairs Goyle’s father died at Ron’s hand. Voldemort fought McGonagall, Kingsley and Slughorn at once, weakened, and still beat all three.

Dracaena stood in front of him while they got up again. She was outmatched and knew it. She held him a few seconds, and a few more, until he opened her arm from shoulder to wrist with a slash of the Elder Wand, the same arm a curse had once laid open in a washroom, and she went down in her own blood. He would not finish her himself. He told Bellatrix to do it. Lucius Malfoy, who had no wand left, threw himself into the curse and died to keep it from her. Bellatrix did not live out the minute. Molly Weasley killed her.

The Duel

Then Harry stood up.

The Death Eaters threw everything they had at him, and the last of his mother’s protection flared and carried him through it. Their curses died before they touched him. When it had gone, it was gone for good, and Harry was as mortal as anyone.

The last of the elite Death Eaters closed in a ring around their master. Harry walked into it. Inside the ring he and Voldemort fought, Harry with Dracaena’s hawthorn wand against the Elder Wand, for a long time, while outside it the battle went on: Dolohov fell to Flitwick, and Yaxley to George and Lee Jordan, and one by one the ring thinned. When it began to break, Ron and the Hufflepuffs behind him held the gap, so that nothing reached Harry’s back. On the stones nearby Hermione had found Dracaena bleeding, the girl who had watched her tortured at the Manor, and was closing the wound in her arm. Neither duelist could finish the other. Harry told him what he was now: every piece of his soul destroyed, a man who would die like any other, holding a wand from a tale whose eldest brother was murdered in his sleep.

Voldemort cast the Killing Curse. Harry cast the spell that had always been his, a disarming charm. The two beams met and locked, and Harry began to give. Then a hand closed over his hand and his wand. Dracaena was up off the stones, the wound in her arm closed, and with both of them behind it the charm drove Voldemort’s curse back down the beam into him. The Elder Wand spun out of his fingers into Harry’s hand. Lord Voldemort fell dead, and did not move again.

After the Battle

Dracaena went down before he hit the ground. Harry caught her and carried her through the crowd that came for him weeping and laughing, and did not put her down until Madam Pomfrey had her. On every Death Eater’s arm the Mark faded, and on Dracaena’s it was left as a scar.

In the Great Hall the Weasleys gathered around Fred. Ron held his mother.

Harry found Dracaena a bed in the hospital wing and sat with her until she woke. What they said to each other took a long time. In the middle of it Ron and Hermione came in with small gifts and said their own overdue things to a girl they had hated for six years. When they rose to go down to the celebration she told Harry to go with them, because she needed to mourn first. He kissed her forehead and went.

With the Elder Wand Harry mended his own holly wand and gave Dracaena her hawthorn back. He used it to raise the fallen towers and walls of Hogwarts, and then he snapped it in two. The rest of the castle was rebuilt by hand in the days that followed, with students and staff working side by side.

The Last Weeks

Term went on for the few weeks that were left of it. The lessons were hardly lessons. Teachers threw parties, cancelled examinations and let the school mourn and rest together. Professor Binns, who had not noticed that there had been a battle, set an essay on the goblin rebellions all the same, to Ron’s and Dracaena’s loud disgust. Dracaena spent those weeks starting over with Ron and Hermione, warily on all sides, and the four of them studied together in the long afternoons.

Narcissa Malfoy had vanished from the battle, and the Ministry wanted her. Dracaena was put on trial before the Wizengamot for what she had done. Katie Bell, who had spent months in St Mungo’s after touching the cursed necklace, testified against her. Neville and a dozen others spoke for her, and Hermione testified for her. Then Ron testified for her, knowing the mead that had nearly killed him had been hers, and many in the court who had come to see her punished changed their minds. The court found her guilty, but under duress, and pardoned her. The court that had once sent Sirius Black to Azkaban without a trial had given Dracaena Malfoy a hearing. She gave up the Malfoy fortune herself, as reparations to the families the Registration Commission had robbed.

The Ministry put her on trial, and no one from the Ministry stood trial at all. The people who had denied the Dark Lord’s return, hunted the Muggle-borns and kept the elves in chains went back to their desks. When the school held a ceremony for those who had ended the war and asked Harry to speak, he used his speech. He honored Dracaena in front of all of them and spoke for Slytherin. Then he turned on the Ministry of Magic, naming its cruelties, and Dobby, and the elves still in bondage. The hall rose for his toast, all but a handful by the doors.

The Last Day

Before she left, Dracaena went alone to Ada’s grave and said what she had never been allowed to say while her friend lived. Ada had hated the chaining of the elves as much as any cruelty, and Dracaena promised to carry that on.

On the last morning a letter came from her mother, alive and in hiding, with nothing in it about any of it, only love.

That afternoon Harry found her lying in the grass by the lake and told her he was proud of her. Neither of them had anywhere to go back to, and he asked her to make a home with him. She kissed him, and they decided to leave together, and Ron came stomping across the field to tell them the train was leaving. The four of them took one compartment on the Hogwarts Express, and it pulled away from the castle with all of them on it.

The Ministry had answered for nothing. That reckoning was still to come.

Changes from Canon

  • Harry truly dies in the forest. Lily’s Ward lets him go only because he chose his death freely, then holds his soul at the threshold. Its last flare carries him through the Death Eaters’ curses, and after that he is fully mortal.
  • At the threshold Harry holds Dumbledore to account for his secrets, and chooses to return to save Dracaena.
  • Narcissa finds no heartbeat and reports it truthfully. She does not lie.
  • has no special rules. It is the most powerful wand ever made, and nothing more. Voldemort kills Snape because he has worked out Snape’s betrayal from the forged sword, not to master the wand.
  • Each Horcrux destroyed weakens Voldemort. His darkness over the courtyard is his last costly exertion, and it spares only those who bear his Mark.
  • Dracaena refuses to identify Harry at the Manor, and gives him her hawthorn wand. He fights the final duel with it.
  • She learns at the Manor that Crabbe and Goyle arranged ’s death, and finds the diadem herself. Both of them die in the Fiendfyre.
  • Voldemort calls Dracaena forward to mock her, not to thank her. Her first Patronus breaks his darkness.
  • The final battle is spread out: Nagini dies, Voldemort beats three masters at once, Dracaena holds him and is cut down, Lucius dies for her and Molly kills Bellatrix. Only then does Harry return, and his duel with Voldemort takes place inside a ring of the last Death Eaters. Ron holds the ring’s gap, and Hermione heals Dracaena so she can rise for the final spell.
  • Harry’s disarming charm wins the beam-lock because Dracaena’s hand is over his.
  • The Elder Wand repairs Hogwarts before Harry snaps it.
  • Kreacher is gone, so Dobby finds Mundungus, and Regulus Black’s story stays untold.
  • Each founder’s relic falls to its own house: Ron, a Hufflepuff, destroys Hufflepuff’s cup, and Hermione, a Ravenclaw, finds Ravenclaw’s diadem.
  • Harry never uses an Unforgivable Curse. Griphook handles the goblins at Gringotts himself.
  • Katie Bell testifies against Dracaena, and Ron’s testimony after hers turns the court. Dracaena is found guilty under duress and pardoned. Narcissa goes into hiding from the Ministry, and Dracaena gives up the Malfoy fortune as reparations.
  • There is no epilogue. Harry and Dracaena leave Hogwarts together.

Trivia

  • Dracaena casts her Patronus with her mother’s wand, and fights beside Harry while he holds her own.
  • Neville’s stand in the courtyard mirrors his first stand in the portrait hole, six years before.
  • Professor Binns is the only teacher on record to set real work in the weeks after the battle.
