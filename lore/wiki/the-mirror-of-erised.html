The Mirror of Erised was an ancient mirror kept at , which showed whoever stood before it the deepest and most desperate desire of their heart. It showed nothing true and nothing to come, and gave nothing that it showed, yet more than one viewer wasted away in front of it. Its maker is unknown., “The Mirror of Erised”

At Christmas 1991 the eleven-year-old found it and saw in it the family he had never known. then made it the last and truest lock on the Philosopher’s Stone, and it gave the Stone to Harry and not to Voldemort., “Quirrell” Afterward it was put away in the Room of Hidden Things, where Harry found it again in his fourth year and where saw herself in it beside him., “The Yule Ball” It burned there in the Fiendfyre of 1998., “The Room of Requirement”

Description

The Mirror stood as high as the ceiling of the room that held it, in an ornate gold frame resting on two clawed feet. Carved across the top of the frame ran a line that looked like no language at all: Erised stra ehru oyt ube cafru oyt on wohsi. Read backward, it says what the Mirror does: I show not your face but your heart’s desire.

To anyone looking at it from the side it was only a fine old mirror. The vision belonged to whoever stood directly before it: when took Harry’s place in front of the glass, the Potters were gone, and he saw only himself.

Properties

What it shows

The Mirror showed its viewer as they most wished to be, or beside whatever they most wished to have, as though it were simply true. It read the heart rather than the face, so it could not be fooled: no one could choose what it would show them, or hide from it what they wanted. More than once it showed people a desire they had not yet admitted to themselves., “What is needed, and what is meant”

What it showed could change, but only when the viewer’s heart did. For more than four years it showed Harry his family whenever he looked, until his desire changed without his choosing it.

The happiest man

Dumbledore explained the Mirror to Harry on the last of his nights before it. The happiest man alive, he said, could use it like an ordinary mirror: he would look into it and see himself exactly as he was.

Danger

The Mirror gave neither knowledge nor truth, and it could not make what it showed real. People had wasted away in front of it all the same, entranced by what they saw, and some had been driven mad, unsure whether what it showed them was real or even possible. Dumbledore warned Harry not to go looking for it again, and told him it did not do to dwell on dreams and forget to live. Then he had it moved.

Limits

The Mirror only showed. It could not foretell the future, though people often mistook it for a window onto what would be. It held no power over the one looking into it, and asked nothing of them. Whatever harm it did, its viewers did to themselves.

The Mirror and the Stone

Main article:

The Philosopher’s Stone had no will of its own and served whoever held it, so whatever guarded it had to judge for it. The teachers’ trials beneath the trapdoor could be passed by anyone clever, brave and determined enough. The Mirror could not., “Through the Trapdoor”

Dumbledore hid the Stone inside it and laid on the glass a working of the old magic, built on what the Mirror already was. Only someone who wanted to find the Stone, and not to use it, could take it out. Anyone else would see themselves making gold, or drinking the Elixir of Life, or putting the Stone in their master’s hand, and the glass would keep it. It was the one protection that could not be passed by skill, because it asked nothing of the thief’s skill. It asked only what they wanted.

History

Origin

No record names the Mirror’s maker or says how it came to Hogwarts. Its sight is a working of , the oldest kind, which answers to what is meant rather than what is said, and it is older than anyone at the school could account for. How long it stood in the castle before 1991 is not recorded.

Christmas 1991

Harry spent his first Christmas at Hogwarts, and among his presents was his dead father’s Invisibility Cloak. Wandering under it one night, he hid from the caretaker in an empty classroom and found the Mirror standing there. In the glass, around his own reflection, stood his mother and father and a whole family he had never known, smiling and waving at him.

He went back the next night and brought Ron, who saw no Potters at all. Ron saw himself alone, in Gryffindor scarlet, the house the Sorting Hat had not given him, holding the House Cup and standing at last above his brothers. Harry went back a third night alone, and Dumbledore was waiting for him. He told Harry what the Mirror was, and that it would be moved to a new home. If Harry ever came across it again, he would be ready for it.

Beneath the trapdoor (1992)

Main article:

The Mirror’s new home was the last chamber beneath the third-floor trapdoor, with the Stone inside it. In June 1992 , carrying Voldemort on the back of his skull, reached it first. He stood before it for some time and saw himself presenting the Stone to his master, and the glass would not give it up.

Harry came in after him, and Quirrell set him in front of the Mirror to look. Harry, who wanted only to keep the Stone from them, saw his reflection take the Stone from its pocket, wink and put it back, and felt its sudden weight in his own. The Mirror had given it to him. Quirrell seized him, and burned at his touch., “Quirrell (1992)”

The Room of Hidden Things (1992–1998)

After the Stone was destroyed, Dumbledore put the Mirror away where the castle keeps what is hidden: the , in its form as the Room of Hidden Things, among a thousand years of lost and forgotten objects.

Harry found it there in his fourth year, in the warm weeks after the Yule Ball. Wandering under the Cloak and wishing he could see his parents again, he came upon a door that had never been there before, and behind it stood the Mirror. He took it for a lucky find, and did not understand what the room was until explained it to him a year later., “Dumbledore’s Army” He began to visit it at night, and the visits became a habit of walking the empty corridors that outlasted them.

One evening Dracaena Malfoy caught him slipping out of the Cloak on his way to it, and came along. Half laughing, she looked into the glass herself, and saw only herself and Harry, and no one else at all. She left with his Cloak, a small theft and a joke between them. More than a year later, with the two of them on opposite sides, she still could not put it out of her mind, and still wondered whether he was truly what she wanted most.

Harry kept visiting into his fifth year, when Dracaena, on patrol for the Inquisitorial Squad, caught him in a corridor and, rather than report him, asked if he was on his way to the mirror. Not long afterward, when she had defied Umbridge for him and then driven him away on purpose, the Mirror stopped showing Harry his family. It showed Dracaena standing beside him instead., “Resistance” He stopped going. It hurt to look, and there was a war coming. He told himself the change had come from seeing his father bully a teenage Snape earlier that year, as though learning what James had been had loosened his wish for his parents. He would not admit to himself what the Mirror had shown him until his sixth year, though he thought about it often., “Christmas”

Destruction (1998)

On the night of the Battle of Hogwarts, Dracaena went into the Room of Hidden Things to destroy the diadem Voldemort had hidden there, and and followed her. Crabbe called up a he could not hold, and it swept through everything the room had kept for a thousand years. In the heart of the blaze Dracaena glimpsed an old mirror showing her herself and Harry, and then the flames took it., “The Room of Hidden Things (1998)” Harry pulled her out of the fire.

The Room of Requirement itself survived, being the castle’s own magic, but nothing it had hidden did. No other mirror like it is known.

Etymology

“Erised” is “desire” written backward, as a mirror would show it. The inscription on the frame is the same trick at greater length: read from the last letter to the first, with the spaces moved, Erised stra ehru oyt ube cafru oyt on wohsi becomes I show not your face but your heart’s desire.

Changes from Canon

  • The Mirror’s sight is a working of ancient magic, and Dumbledore’s lock on the Stone was built on it.
  • Ron, sorted into Hufflepuff, sees himself in Gryffindor scarlet with the House Cup, rather than as Head Boy and Quidditch captain.
  • Canon never says what became of the Mirror after the first book. Here it is hidden in the Room of Hidden Things, where Harry finds it again in his fourth year, and it is destroyed in the Fiendfyre of 1998.
  • Harry’s and Dracaena’s visions after the first book are new.

Trivia

  • Quirrell and Harry stood before the Mirror within minutes of each other, wanting the same Stone. It kept it from the one who meant to use it and gave it to the one who only meant to keep it safe.
  • Harry’s habit of walking the castle at night began with his visits to the Mirror, and outlasted them.
  • Dumbledore told Harry he saw himself holding a pair of thick woollen socks. It was most likely not the truth. What he wanted most was his family whole again, his sister Ariana among them, the family he answered for at the threshold in 1998., “The Forest”
  • Ron never wore the scarlet the Mirror showed him. By the end of his fourth year he was sure Hufflepuff was where he belonged, and in his fifth he won it the Quidditch Cup.
  • Harry blamed the change in his vision on his father. Learning what James had been made a convenient reason, and it let him go on telling himself, for another year, that the girl in the glass meant nothing.
  • The last thing the Mirror ever showed anyone was Dracaena’s heart’s desire: herself and Harry, and nothing else, exactly as in her fourth year. By then it was Harry’s too.
