Hold Me Close

Dracaena Malfoy Memory · The Wizarding World

The air was crisp on the final day at Hogwarts as the sun crept over the horizon. It wasn’t long before seventh-year students would say goodbye to this school forever, everything that happened there left behind in another era.

For Dracaena, that couldn’t be further from the truth. Even with Harry, Ron and Hermione’s forgiveness, it didn’t change those she harmed and what she couldn’t take back. She still blamed herself for Ada; while she may not have been the one to do it, she stood side by side with her killer. The pain lingered no matter how much time had passed. Even as they left Hogwarts, she’d carry it with her forever.

She blamed herself for her father’s death; believing that, if she simply fought better, it could have been prevented. She blamed herself for Dumbledore. For Snape. For everyone she’d ever idolized and betrayed. She blamed herself the night Harry rejected her, the night Harry used that curse on her, the night…

Remember what he said, Dracaena.

Harry Potter was the one that made her realize something else. That, ultimately, it wasn’t too late to change. To be better. To love, to care. He’d tell her always, ever since they defeated Voldemort, that those moments didn’t define her, that she can’t blame herself for everything. She could never bring herself to fully believe him but, if only for him, she’d try.

It was there, lying in the grass, that she pondered these things. Her past, her present. Her future was more uncertain than ever; her family had been torn apart in more ways than one; a lineage torn to shreds before her eyes and she wasn’t sure how to feel. As thoughts of her father began to creep up again, her eyes on the sky above, Harry’s glasses emerged from the top of her vision.

“Hey.” He said simply. “Hey.” She responded back.

It all became so… easy, now, with Harry. Simple, in a way that cheered up her day every time. Everyone else still looked at her with some level of hesitation and fear. Not Harry. Never Harry. In truth, she was overjoyed it was him, of all people, that stuck with her.

“Deep in thought?” Harry asked, tilting his head a little while she remained facing him on the ground. “I guess so.” She responded, preparing to sit up as Harry put his hand out to stop her. “Stay there. We can just relax for a while, we’ve got plenty of time.” Dracaena nodded, laying back down as her blond hair settled amongst the grass. He sat down next to her before, likewise, laying down and facing the sky.

They sat there in silence for a long time. Neither had any idea how much time had passed when Harry opened his mouth again. “I’m proud of you, Dracaena.”

“What?” She glanced over in surprise as Harry remained staring at the sky, a little flicker of light sparking in her heart. “I said, I’m proud of you.” Harry turned his face towards her as she glanced over at him. She couldn’t help but be a little mesmerized as he stared into her eyes, their warmth overtaking her.

“I… how? For what?” She contested, stuttering it out in a strange rhythm. “I… I haven’t done anything—” “Yes, you did. I never thought I’d see the day that you and Ron would be on good terms, let alone you and Hermione!” He laughed a little. “But, in all seriousness, you’ve… you’ve grown a lot. And I’m glad to see you’re not letting his shadow overtake you anymore.” His expression got a little emotional. “I… If I’m being truthful, I was concerned about you since… well… and…” Stuttering over his words, Dracaena giggled. He blushed slightly and laughed a little himself as he finally got out the words. “I was worried at the chance you’d let it consume you. Everything that happened. And despite it all… you’ve been strong… radiant, even.” His glance held now, looking at her with level of warmth she didn’t know was even possible as her heart nearly leaped into her stomach. “I… I just wanted to say that, with our year wrapping up… it’s been great getting to truly know you, Dracaena Malfoy.” His honest, hopeful smile crossed his face again; the one she couldn’t forget.

Her face became warm as they maintained their shared gaze, a deep wanting coming to the forefront of her mind. “And you’re a stubborn miracle, Harry Potter.” He chuckled a little at that, his messy hair shaking with his movement. She couldn’t help but analyze every part of him now that she was privileged to do so.

“I… actually had something else to say.” He began, his eyes glancing away from her as he looked to the trees ahead. Puzzled, she waited for him to continue. “I… I was wondering… neither of us quite have anywhere to go. The Dursleys… well, I can’t go back to them. And, you… things have changed for you, now. So, I was wondering…” His words were a little nervous, his eyes glancing to her and then back again, Dracaena’s eyes widening as she began to pick up what he was saying.

“I… I was thinking we could find someplace to go… together. I have inheritance money from my parents and… it could give us both a fresh start—” Harry stopped his speech as he glanced at Dracaena again. She wasn’t smiling, but her excitement must have been abundantly obvious as his face began to blush in a similar way.

Dracaena had no words. She knew he was genuine; he never lied to her, he never would. His purity always shone like the sun, the source of light in her heart since the start. She breathed in shakily, the tears beginning to form a little in her eyes.

Before her laid the boy she was destined to hate; the one her parents, her house, her favorite teachers all pointed her away from. Yet, despite everything, here he was with a request she never thought she’d hear. She never would have thought in a million years that he’d be here with her now, enjoying time together like the friends they were never meant to be, dreaming of a future in a shared home.

But for Dracaena, she longed for more than that. She never expected it, of course; she’d done so much evil, both to Harry and his friends, but she could hope and dream in spite of it. That night he kissed her forehead was as fresh in her mind as it was the night it happened, and she found herself longing for another moment quite like that. Maybe it was an in-the-moment mistake, a—

No. Dracaena would take it into her own hands, now.

Before he even got the chance to continue, she leaped onto him, so close now their faces were but inches away. “D-Dracaena…?!” He began to say, his face shocked in a way that made her even more confident. In an instant, her lips crashed against his like they were always meant to be aligned, desperate and needing. His face lit as she closed her eyes, savoring the moment like it’d never happen again. His hands, which previously idled at her side, wrapped around her torso, clutching her close with intensity, like he couldn’t bear to let her go. She gasped a little at the contact, her tears of joy spilling onto his face as they embraced. When she finally parted, her face shone with tears as Harry looked up at her. He looked just as overwhelmed, huffing with heavy breaths as he looked her in the eyes. She waited, anticipated, hoped. “J-Just tell me you love me, Potter!! Just say it, you… idiot!!” She spoke, baring her fangs like she used to do all those years ago. He only smiled back at her, tears forming at the edge of his own eyes, as he finally opened his mouth to speak.

“Does this answer it?” In an instant, he’d yanked her aside, their positions switching now as she stared up at him. Her heart beat faster than ever, as she yanked his tie down for more.

Their kiss continued, and evolved into something more as they opened to each other. She couldn’t be happier, she’d never been happier. For a moment, she thought of her father, his disapproval, but it was cast out just as quickly. They’d defeated Voldemort, they’d helped rebuild the castle. They’d lived through it all, and they’d continue to live in a way they never had before. She ran her hand through his hair, tugging on it with force, running her other hand beneath his robe before—

“Ay! Lovebirds!” Ron shouted, his footsteps coming ever closer. In an instant, Harry had parted his lips from hers, saliva trailing from them both as he wiped his mouth in a hurry. Dracaena, pissed, looked up at the redhead. “R-Ron, hey! What’s up?” Harry began, his face still blushed and his glasses off-balance. They’d gotten a little too carried away, she noticed, seeing his robe misaligned. Hermione was with Ron too, covering her mouth as she hid a giggle. “Clearly a lot’s up with you, mate.” Ron looked between Harry and her, shaking his head a little. “I mean, it was pretty obvious but like…. Here? In the open field?” Harry stuttered out a rebuttal as he got to his feet. “Ron, we weren’t going to—”

“Yeah, yeah, I get it. Anyhow, it’s time to board. You ready?” Dracaena’s eyes were still locked on Ron with an angry expression as he looked back at her with amusement.

Even if we’re friends now, Dracaena thought, you’ll never cease to get in my way.

Harry held his hand out and Dracaena took it, getting to her feet and brushing the grass off of her outfit. Her makeup was probably a mess now, she realized, but this was their last day. And now? There was no one left to impress but Harry (and he’d already seemed quite impressed as is).

“Let’s go then.” Hermione spoke a little frantically, checking the time. “We’ve got to hurry or we’ll be late! It took ages to find you two!” Ron simply put his hand on Hermione’s shoulder as she glared at him. “It’ll be alright, hon. Come on, you two.”

Dracaena glanced at Harry, calmed down with her face still flush, as she nodded. Harry nodded in return, their gaze redirecting to Ron again. “Let’s go, then.” Harry said, winking in Dracaena’s direction as she giggled a little.

When they finally arrived at the station, after gathering their belongings in quite the long walk, they boarded the train with minutes to spare. Of course, the four of them went into the same compartment. Where else would they go but together? As they sat down, Hermione and Ron on one end and her and Harry on the other, she’d never felt more at peace. Her new life seemed like it’d be quite wonderful.

She thought back to her first-year self; the out-of-place snobby girl with no direction. If she could only speak to herself now… she’d… she’d tell her everything Harry told her.

It’s okay. It’ll all be okay. I’m here for you.

She’d tell her that there’d be pain. That there’d be hardship. That she’d need to endure a lot and struggle a lot for the sake of those she loved. That she’d wind up pushing those she loved most away to keep them safe… but that clinging to them was always the key. Appreciating every moment, loving every part of them.

Ada. Snape. Dumbledore. Her father. The many who died in their final battle. Her eyes shut in grief before opening with reignited purpose. They’d live for those who couldn’t. And she knew, deep down, that if Ada were here now… she’d be so proud. Dracaena smiled.

Hermione and Ron looked out the window, Hermione’s head leaned against his shoulder as he leaned against her. Their eyes looked at Hogwarts; their old home, the place they’d grown in more ways than one. Harry, likewise, looked at Hogwarts with nostalgia flowing through him. Dracaena looked at Hogwarts and saw it in a different light; a flawed school, years of hardship. But she couldn’t deny that it’d ultimately raised her, and those she met transformed her.

Dracaena leaned her head against Harry’s shoulder as he wrapped his arm around her. He whispered into her ear, teasingly. “So… earlier… that was a yes, right?” She only scoffed before pecking him on the cheek, a light blush on his face again. “Of course, you idiot.” His face lit with his usual smirk before he uttered three words she had waited for, for oh so long. “I love you.”

“I love you too.” Dracaena responded in an instant, flowing as naturally as breathing. Ron groaned as Hermione smacked him on the leg, mouthing something to him as he began a rebuttal. Dracaena tuned them out as she and Harry met their lips again.

Dracaena pictured their future, their marriage, their children. As her mind began to shut those thoughts down, she only brought them to the forefront again. She’d never crush her dreams again, she’d never let others drive her ever again. She was her own person now, and she’d chosen who she wanted.

She longed for it to never end, as the train began to move.
