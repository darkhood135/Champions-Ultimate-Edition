Loki

Loki Laufeyson

Summoner

The adopted son of Odin and eternal trickster of Asgard, Loki Laufeyson schemes without end for the throne, the vengeance, and the recognition he believes the God of Mischief is owed.

Loki was born unwanted and has spent an immortal lifetime making the world pay for it. A runt of the Frost Giants, cast aside to die and taken up by Odin as a spoil of war, he was raised in the golden halls of Asgard as a brother to Thor — and never for a single day allowed to forget that he did not belong there. A giant among gods, a schemer among warriors, a shadow beside a favored son whose every triumph deepened his own sense of exile, he learned early that he would never win the throne or his father's love by strength or valor, and so he chose the only weapons left to him: cunning, deception, and a silver tongue that has toppled kingdoms. That is the heart of him — not malice for its own sake, but a bottomless, aching need to be seen, twisted into endless mischief and grand betrayal. His hatred for Thor is real, and so, maddeningly, is his love; he can neither defeat his brother nor abandon him, forever circling the one bond he cannot cut. He is a villain who was, in some cruel measure, made into one — and who has embraced the role with a flourish, because if the world insists on casting him as the trickster, he will be the finest trickster it has ever feared.

His power lies not in his fists but in his mind and his magic. Among the mightiest sorcerers in all the Nine Realms, Loki bends reality through illusion and enchantment — conjuring phantoms indistinguishable from truth, slipping between places in an instant, and reshaping his very form to wear any face, any voice, any guise he pleases. Beneath the sorcery runs the resilience and long life of his Asgardian upbringing, but it has always been the least of his weapons. His true art is manipulation: the whisper that turns ally against ally, the plan folded inside a plan inside a lie, the scheme already three moves ahead by the time anyone realizes a game is being played at all. The most dangerous thing about Loki is never the spell you can see — it is that you can never be certain which of his words is the truth, and by the time you are, the trap has already closed around you.

"Everything is part of my plan."

Love and Loathing w/ Thor

For all his scheming, everything Loki does traces back to Thor. He covets the throne his brother was promised, resents the golden favor Thor was given and he never was, and has plotted the thunder god's ruin more times than either could count — and yet, in the wreckage of every scheme, he can never quite bring himself to see it through. He tells himself he hates Thor, and part of him truly does; but the brother he grew up beside is the only soul who ever treated him as kin, and Loki has never learned how to stop needing that. Love and loathing, braided together too tightly to ever pull apart — the one knot the God of Lies has never managed to talk his way out of.

The Liar and the Lasso w/ Wonder Woman

Loki has unwound kings and gods alike with nothing but a well-placed word — and in Diana of Themyscira he has met the one opponent his gift slides right off of. She is truth made flesh, armed with a lasso that drags honesty from any throat it binds: everything the God of Lies has spent an eternity making himself the opposite of. He finds her maddening and fascinating in equal measure, a mind he cannot cloud and a will he cannot bend, forcing him to be genuinely clever rather than merely deceitful. To Loki there is no sport in fooling the foolish — Diana is the rare mark worth the effort, precisely because he knows she will see straight through him.

Father of the Avengers w/ Iron Man

It is the single greatest irony of Loki's long life, and Stark will never let him forget it: the mightiest heroes of Midgard first banded together for the sole purpose of thwarting one of his schemes. He meddled with Thor, and in doing so authored the very team that has foiled him at every turn since — a legacy he never wanted and can never disown. Tony wears that history like a smirk, needling the trickster about the family he accidentally founded, and it galls Loki all the more because he cannot argue the point. Of all who oppose him, the armored futurist is the one who reminds him, gratingly, that his cleverest gambit was also his most catastrophic mistake.
