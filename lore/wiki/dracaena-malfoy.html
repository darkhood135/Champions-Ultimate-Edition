Dracaena Lucius Malfoy (born 5 June 1980) is a pure-blood witch of , the only child of Lucius and Narcissa Malfoy and heir to the ancient House of Malfoy. Raised at Malfoy Manor on a diet of wealth, privilege and blood-purity, she was sorted into in 1991. She spent her years at as the sharp-tongued rival of , performing a contempt she was never sure she felt.

The return of trapped her between the family she loved and the conscience she could not silence. Made to pay for her father’s failures with an impossible task, she let the Death Eaters into Hogwarts but could not bring herself to kill . She lost her closest friend, , to the door she had opened. At the Battle of Hogwarts she turned her wand on the Dark Lord in front of both armies, conjured her first Patronus, and stood with Harry when the Elder Wand, which had chosen them both, refused to strike them down. Cleared at trial, she left Hogwarts at Harry’s side.

Physically, Dracaena is an ordinary teenage witch. Peak-human Champions like Batman or Captain America would overpower her in a moment, and anything at Spider-Man’s level could end a fight before she raised her wand. Her danger is magical. Wizarding spells ignore the usual scale: one well-aimed Stunner, Disarming Charm or curse can drop anyone with human-level durability, her duelist’s precision lets her land them, and her Occlumency guards her mind against telepathy.

Against city-level threats and beyond (the Hulk, Superman, a Super Saiyan), she has no answer except cunning and allies. Within her own world she is a strong duelist for her age, well short of Dumbledore or Voldemort, though she held the Dark Lord off for the seconds that mattered.

History

Worlds Apart

Family background

The Malfoys are among the oldest and wealthiest wizarding families in Britain. The line traces back to Armand Malfoy, who crossed from France with William the Conqueror and was granted land in Wiltshire for services the family has never cared to describe. On that land the Malfoys built their Manor, and over the centuries they enlarged it, along with their fortune and their influence. They married only within the pure-blood families, kept close to whoever held power in the Ministry, and wore their contempt for Muggles and Muggle-borns as a mark of breeding.

Dracaena’s grandfather, Abraxas, raised her father in that creed. Lucius took it further: in his youth he followed Lord Voldemort as a Death Eater. When the Dark Lord fell in 1981 he bought his way back to respectability, claiming he had acted under the Imperius Curse. Her mother came from another proud line, the House of Black. Narcissa’s elder sister Bellatrix was the most fanatical of the Dark Lord’s servants; her other sister, Andromeda, had married a Muggle-born and been struck from the family for it. Dracaena was the single heir both houses had to show for their names.

Early life (1980–1991)

Dracaena was born on 5 June 1980 and grew up at Malfoy Manor as an only child, surrounded by peacocks on the lawns, portraits of ancestors on the walls, and a house-elf, Dobby, whom her father treated with casual cruelty. She wanted for nothing that money could buy. Her parents’ exacting upbringing drilled magic and manners into her long before Hogwarts, and she arrived there with both well ahead of her age.

What she learned best was the family creed. The Malfoys taught their daughter that blood was worth more than character, which people were worth knowing, and that a Malfoy never let anyone see her uncertain. Lucius and Narcissa were cold, contemptuous people to almost everyone they met, but not to her. Her father’s love was real, private and fierce, and it was bound up with his expectations, so that pleasing him and becoming what he believed in came to feel like the same thing. She grew up reciting his opinions as though they were her own, and learned early to perform the contempt she heard at home, long before she knew whether she meant it.

First year (1991–1992)

Main article:

The summer before her first year, being fitted for robes at Madam Malkin’s in Diagon Alley, Dracaena talked at the boy on the next stool: about brooms and houses and which sorts of people were worth knowing. She never asked his name. She learned it on the Hogwarts Express, where she found Harry Potter sharing a compartment with Ron Weasley and introduced herself with the pride of a name she expected to mean something. Flanked by Vincent Crabbe and Gregory Goyle, the heavy, silent sons of her father’s associates, she offered to teach him which wizarding families were worth more than others, and held out her hand. He refused it. No one had ever refused her, and her pride cracked into something more wounded than she would let anyone see (). The Sorting Hat placed her in Slytherin, exactly as her family had always assumed.

She spent the year needling the boy who had turned her down, as much for his attention as for sport. During the first-years’ flying lesson she snatched ’s Remembrall and dared Harry into the air after it, then watched, gaping for half a second, as he caught it in a dive that won him a place as Gryffindor’s Seeker. That evening she challenged him to a midnight duel she never meant to attend. Caught out of bounds on the same night as Harry, Hermione and Neville, she shared their punishment: a detention in . When a hooded figure came out of the dark to drink a unicorn’s blood, Harry put himself between it and her before she bolted into the trees (). Afterward she told him he had been a mediocre bodyguard at best. At the leaving feast Slytherin lost the House Cup to Gryffindor on the last points awarded, and her face soured most of all.

Second year (1992–1993)

Main article:

At a book signing in Diagon Alley, Dracaena needled Harry and Ron and had begun to ease off when the rest of the Weasleys closed ranks around their brother. Then her father swept in and, with a few cold words, pressed her to sharpen her claws again. In the scuffle between Lucius and Arthur Weasley, no one saw Lucius slip an old black diary into Ginny Weasley’s cauldron.

That autumn Lucius bought the whole Slytherin team new Nimbus Two Thousand and Ones, and Dracaena’s place as Seeker came with them. When Hermione Granger pointed out on the pitch that Gryffindor’s players had earned their places rather than bought them, Dracaena felt her housemates waiting for her to put the girl in her place. After a hesitation that cost her visibly, she forced out the ugliest word she knew. It was her father’s word, recited rather than believed, and the shame of it curdled in her even as she said it (). Ron Weasley’s attempt to curse her backfired through his broken wand, and she fled in tears.

The rest of the year kept giving her moments she could not fit into the story her father told her. Facing Harry Seeker to Seeker, she faltered when a rogue Bludger shattered his arm, her taunts coming out almost soft before she hardened them again. She told herself she was the better player and did not quite believe it (). At the short-lived dueling club she conjured a serpent to menace him, and when he spoke to it in Parseltongue and the school recoiled, she found herself frightened not of him but for him (). When the Chamber of Secrets was finally closed, her father was stripped of his seat on the school’s board of governors for the diary he had planted, and Dobby was freed through a trick of Harry’s. Dracaena was left with the sour taste of her father brought low by Potter once again.

Third year (1993–1994)

Main article:

Showing off in ’s first Care of Magical Creatures lesson, Dracaena insulted the Hippogriff Buckbeak to its face, and it opened her arm. The wound was shallow; her reaction was pure theatre. She had never meant the creature any harm, but Lucius used her injury, and the Ministry’s creature-disposal office, to have Buckbeak sentenced to death. She spent the weeks that followed visibly wretched, knowing better than anyone that the beast had done nothing a beast shouldn’t (). It was the first time she was openly ashamed of what her family’s name was doing in her name.

She was still capable of pettiness. With Crabbe and Goyle she disguised herself as a Dementor to spook Harry off his broom, and was blasted flat by his Patronus Charm for it. But the rivalry was changing shape. In Hogsmeade an invisible Harry pelted her with mud and sent her fleeing in tears, then followed her alone, dropped his cloak and apologized. It was the first honest moment the two of them had ever shared, and neither knew what to do with it. Later, with Buckbeak’s appeal lost, Hermione struck her across the face, and Ron howled with laughter. Harry did not laugh, and the argument it caused between him and his friends was the first they ever had about her.

Fourth year (1994–1995)

Main article:

The summer opened with Death Eaters in masks rampaging through the campsite at the Quidditch World Cup, her father among them. At Hogwarts, the Triwizard Tournament named Harry its fourth, underage champion. Dracaena’s POTTER STINKS badges bloomed across half the castle. To make so many she needed a clever hand, and so she turned for the first time to , a Slytherin she had shared a house with for years and never once noticed (). Ada was unbothered, unimpressed and quietly kind, and saw through Dracaena’s performance faster than anyone ever had. The use became the first real friendship of Dracaena’s life. When Dracaena worked out that Ada was a half-blood, she said something small and ugly out of reflex. Ada shrugged it off as ridiculous, Dracaena heard herself and apologized, and the two swore to keep the friendship secret from her father ().

It was Ada who named what Dracaena would not: that she had a crush on the Potter boy (). Pushed to ask him to the Yule Ball, Dracaena wrapped the invitation in a sneer, telling him she supposed she’d let him take her if he was so desperate (). Harry, freshly turned down by Chao Zheng, heard only the blade and sent her away. She wept alone that night, as much at the armored girl she showed the world as at him. Then Harry came to her with an apology and an invitation of his own, and at the ball, for the length of one song, every wall she had built came down ().

It ended beneath an invisibility cloak, where she overheard Ron and Hermione speak of the invitation as a plan to learn what her father was planning. She fled, certain she had been played from the first dance, and went cold. At the end of the year the Dark Lord rose again in a graveyard, with Lucius kneeling in his circle and a boy, Cedric Diggory, dead. Dracaena came to Harry carrying both the betrayal and the terror. She struck him across the face, warned him to stay away from her and her family before it killed him, and walked to the other side of the war.

Fifth year (1995–1996)

Main article:

Resigned after the graveyard to becoming what her family needed, Dracaena joined Dolores Umbridge’s Inquisitorial Squad. Ada, who had never once raised her voice at her, was furious, and the argument that followed was brutal. Dracaena stayed in the Squad regardless and drifted back to Crabbe and Goyle, because being with them was safer than being known. The truth she could not tell Ada was that she had not chosen the Squad so much as been caught between her father and the thing in the graveyard, with no road out.

The loneliest night of her life came when she and Harry clashed in a corridor and, for once, said what they meant. Harry was brutally honest, and it pierced her because she could not deny a word of it (). Yet the truth did its slow work. When Umbridge’s cruelty passed what even her fear could stomach, Dracaena defied the woman openly, in front of the Squad and the school, and very nearly paid for it (). It was the first time she had ever stood in the open against power. Afterward she went back to Ada and made it right, and this time she let the whole house see that the Malfoy girl’s truest friend was a half-blood (). Crabbe and Goyle, who had always assumed her theirs, never forgave it. At the year’s end her father was captured at the Department of Mysteries and sent to Azkaban.

Sixth year (1996–1997)

Main article:

The Dark Lord did not forgive failure, and he punished the Malfoys through their daughter. Brought before him that summer, Dracaena was given a task no sixteen-year-old could be meant to survive: mend the broken Vanishing Cabinet in the Room of Requirement so the Death Eaters could enter Hogwarts, and murder Albus Dumbledore herself, with her parents’ lives forfeit if she failed (). Out of love, Narcissa went to and bound him with an Unbreakable Vow to protect her daughter and finish the task if she could not. Over that same summer her aunt Bellatrix taught her Occlumency, so that no one at Hogwarts could read what she was hiding.

Her attempts were as desperate and botched as a frightened girl’s would be. A cursed necklace meant for Dumbledore nearly killed Katie Bell; a bottle of poisoned mead nearly killed Ron Weasley. She came apart at the seams, grey and sleepless, and pushed Ada away to keep her clear of it. Her one comfort was a letter from her mother with nothing in it but love (). Harry spent the year certain she was up to something. Everyone else put it down to lovesickness over the Yule Ball, and when he finally cornered her in a washroom, his desperate, unknown curse laid her open and nearly killed her. Snape healed her. Harry, devastated, visited her in the hospital wing, and the two exchanged apologies neither could say properly ().

The Cabinet was mended, and Dracaena let the Death Eaters in (). In the fighting, Ada was killed by one of the men she had admitted, and Dracaena was made to walk past her body at the killer’s side and say nothing. On top of the Astronomy Tower she found Dumbledore wandless and waiting. He spoke to her gently, and she could not add one more sin to the pile. She did not disarm him: she simply let her own wand fall. Snape, bound by the Vow, killed Dumbledore in her place. As the Death Eaters fled, Harry reached out his hand to her. She refused it with a grief that had no bottom, because her parents’ lives still hung on her staying, and went with them into the dark.

Seventh year and the Battle of Hogwarts (1997–1998)

Main article:

When Harry, Ron and Hermione were captured and brought to Malfoy Manor, Dracaena was made to watch Bellatrix torture Hermione on the drawing-room floor, sick at the sight of a girl she now knew to be worth ten of her. Asked to identify the disguised, swollen-faced boy as Harry Potter, she said she could not be sure. It was a quiet failure to damn him, and she had been practicing it for years. The prisoners escaped with the help of Dobby, who died in the attempt.

During the Battle of Hogwarts she went to the Room of Requirement and found Crabbe and Goyle, who she had learned had engineered Ada’s death in revenge for the friendship she had shown the school (). She beat Goyle back but would not kill him. Crabbe’s cursed Fiendfyre, too large for him to control, consumed them both, and in the heart of the blaze she glimpsed, in an old mirror, herself and Harry and nothing else, before Harry hauled her out of the flames. Disoriented, unwilling to raise her wand against classmates, she slipped away to find her parents.

When Voldemort displayed Harry’s apparently dead body in the courtyard (), he called her forward, almost tenderly, to take her place among the victors. She came forward, stopped, and turned her wand on him. Swatting the spell aside, he asked what she imagined she was doing. She told him he had taken Ada, and Harry, and her whole life, and that she would rather die on her feet than kneel to him for another hour. As the killing light gathered she thought of Ada and of Harry, and conjured her first Patronus, a great silver peacock that drove back the dark across the grounds. Hopelessly outmatched, she held him for precious seconds before he opened her arm from shoulder to wrist and she fell to her knees. He called on Bellatrix to finish her. Lucius threw himself into the path of the curse and died to save her (), and Molly Weasley killed Bellatrix where she stood.

In the final duel, as Harry’s Disarming Charm met Voldemort’s Killing Curse, Dracaena rose one last time and closed her hand over his. The Elder Wand, which had chosen them both for what they were beneath everything they had done, refused to kill its own: it turned the curse back on its master, and Lord Voldemort died. Dracaena collapsed, and Harry carried her through the courtyard to the healers himself.

Aftermath (1998)

Dracaena mourned her father, who had died in the only unguarded act of his life, and Ada, who had died for the crime of being loved by the wrong girl. In the weeks that followed she found something she had never had: an ordinary peace and a true circle of friends in Harry, Ron and Hermione. She was tried for what she had been made to do. Harry, Ron, Hermione, Neville and many others testified that she had acted under a sentence of death on her family, and that her turning had bought the living the time they needed. She was cleared. At the ceremony for the war’s victors, Harry honored her before the whole school.

On the last day of school, lying in the grass with the castle emptying around them, Harry told her he was proud of her and asked her to make a fresh start with him somewhere new. She kissed him before he could finish (). That morning a letter had reached her in her mother’s hand: Narcissa was alive, in hiding, and wrote of nothing but love (). Before she left, Dracaena went alone to Ada’s grave and said at last everything she had never been allowed to say. She promised to be better for having been loved by her, and to carry forward Ada’s hatred of the chaining of house-elves ().

Physical appearance

Dracaena is slight and pale, with long platinum-blonde hair worn loose and a sharp, fine-boned face. As a girl she carried herself with a rehearsed hauteur, chin raised and arms crossed, every movement calculated to look unbothered. Through her sixth year the strain showed plainly: she grew grey, thin and sleepless, and flinched at shadows. She bears the scars of the war: the curse Harry laid open in the washroom, and the wound Voldemort cut from shoulder to wrist at the Battle of Hogwarts. By the end of the war the hauteur had softened, though the sharp tongue and the habit of baring her teeth when flustered remain.

Personality and traits

Proud, quick-witted and sharp as a drawn blade, Dracaena is at heart a performer who spent most of her life performing the wrong part. She learned young that a Malfoy must never look uncertain, and that belonging, and her father’s approval, required the contempt she heard at home. She parroted it so well that almost everyone mistook it for her. The cruelty she showed at school was real in its effects and seldom real in its source. More often than not it was recited (her father’s word, her house’s opinion) and followed by shame.

Beneath the costume ran a conscience she could scarcely admit to and never fully silence. She is genuinely dramatic and genuinely frightened of pain and blood, and when afraid she armors herself in mockery. For years she could only reach for what she wanted sideways, wrapping a kindness or a confession in a sneer so that refusal would not wound her. When it did, she wept alone rather than be seen. Her deepest fear was not of Voldemort but of being exactly what her family wanted her to be. The night after the Yule Ball refusal, she cried not at Harry but at the hateful girl she showed the world.

Her defining weakness was cowardice born of love. Almost every terrible thing she did in the war, she did because her parents’ lives hung on it. Her defining strength turned out to be the same love, aimed elsewhere. She would not kill a defenseless man even to save her family, and when everything had been taken from her she stood up in front of both armies rather than kneel. She is capable of real shame and, more rarely and at great cost, of real apology. After the war she struggles to forgive herself, blaming herself for Ada, for her father, for Dumbledore and for every bystander she harmed. Harry insists those moments do not define her. She cannot quite believe him, but for him she tries.

She remains a true daughter of Slytherin: ambitious, cunning, resourceful and fiercely protective of the few people she lets in. What changed over the course of the war was not her nature but what it was for.

Magical abilities and skills

Dueling

A gifted and precise duelist, Dracaena fights through resourcefulness and composure rather than raw power. She outthinks opponents she cannot overpower and refuses to yield an inch. Against Voldemort himself she was hopelessly outmatched, but she still held him for the seconds that mattered.

Spellcraft

Raised under exacting expectations, Dracaena arrived at Hogwarts with a command of magic ahead of most of her year. Her spellcraft is deep and disciplined: she conjured a serpent at the dueling club in her second year, and in her sixth she repaired a broken Vanishing Cabinet that had baffled others.

Occlumency

Taught by her aunt Bellatrix in the summer before her sixth year, Dracaena became a capable Occlumens. Throughout that year she kept her mind, and her mission, closed even to the teachers watching her most closely.

Flying

Slytherin’s Seeker from her second year, she is a skilled flier. She was never quite Harry’s equal in the air, a fact she resented and never fully admitted.

Patronus Charm

For most of her life Dracaena could not cast a corporeal Patronus; she had nothing pure enough to hold onto. She conjured one for the first time in the Battle of Hogwarts, thinking of Ada and of Harry, as the Killing Curse gathered against her. It took the shape of a great silver peacock, the old emblem of the Manor’s lawns made luminous, and its light drove the Dark Lord’s weight off the fallen defenders across the grounds.

The Elder Wand

The Elder Wand chooses its wielders by strength of character, not by conquest. Unknown to everyone, Voldemort included, it had chosen both Dracaena and Harry. She never needed to hold it. When her hand closed over Harry’s in the final duel, the wand recognized its own, refused to complete the Killing Curse against them, and turned it back on the Dark Lord.

Possessions

  • Wand: hawthorn, with a unicorn hair core, ten inches and reasonably springy. Hawthorn is said to suit a conflicted nature, or a witch passing through a period of turmoil.
  • Nimbus Two Thousand and One: bought for her, along with the rest of the Slytherin team, by her father in her second year.
  • Her mother’s letters: one sent in the depths of her sixth year and one from hiding after the war, both with nothing in them but love.

Relationships

Family

Lucius Malfoy

Cruel, contemptuous and ambitious toward the whole world, Lucius was gentle only with his daughter. He taught her the creed she performed for years, and for a long time Harry mistook his hold over her for nothing warmer than control. His love was real, but it came bound to expectation, so that she could not easily tell pleasing him from becoming him. His failure at the Department of Mysteries put her in the Dark Lord’s power. His last act undid it: at the Battle of Hogwarts he threw himself into his own side’s curse to keep it from reaching her, the only unguarded act of his life. She mourns him, and still wonders whether she could have saved him if she had fought better.

Narcissa Malfoy

Proud and cold to the world like her husband, and like him tender only with her daughter. Narcissa made the Unbreakable Vow with Snape so that Dracaena would not have to finish the task herself. In the Forbidden Forest she lied to the Dark Lord’s face that Harry was dead, as the fastest way back into the castle to find her. Her letters bookend Dracaena’s war, and after it she lives in hiding, free in her own way at last.

Bellatrix Lestrange

Her mother’s sister, the most devoted of the Death Eaters. Bellatrix taught Dracaena Occlumency and tortured Hermione in front of her at the Manor. When Dracaena turned on the Dark Lord, Bellatrix was the one called to make a public lesson of the traitor-child. Molly Weasley killed her before she could.

The Black family

Through her mother, Dracaena is related to much of the old House of Black, including her mother’s cousin Sirius Black and her aunt Andromeda, struck from the family for marrying a Muggle-born.

Harry Potter

The boy her family loathed above all others, and the one person she could never bring herself to truly hate. Their rivalry began with a refused handshake and was fed for years by pride on her side and principle on his. It was softened by small, unwilling kindnesses: her fear for him at the dueling club, his apology in Hogsmeade, the laugh he would not share. It nearly became love at the Yule Ball before a misunderstanding and the war tore it apart. For two years they stood on opposite sides, each wishing they could mend what the other could not. It survived his worst spell and her worst year. In the end it was Harry she thought of when she cast her first Patronus, Harry’s hand she closed hers over in the final duel, and Harry who carried her to the healers. On the last day of school they chose to make a new life together. He is the first person who ever looked past the costume to the girl underneath, and he has never stopped insisting she is worth more than her past.

Ada Khumalo

The first real friend Dracaena ever had, and the person who changed her most. Dracaena approached Ada only because she was useful. Ada, a half-blood Slytherin who found the whole idea of blood-purity faintly ridiculous, saw through her faster than anyone and loved her plainly anyway. It was Ada who named her crush on Harry, Ada who refused to let the Inquisitorial Squad pass without a fight, and Ada whom Dracaena finally claimed in front of the whole house. Dracaena never told her the truth about her sixth year, to keep her clear of it. Ada was killed by a Death Eater who came through the door Dracaena had opened, on a plan engineered by Crabbe and Goyle. Her death was Dracaena’s lowest point, and the grief behind her final defiance. At Ada’s grave she promised to carry Ada’s cause forward.

Hermione Granger

For years the target of Dracaena’s worst words, including the slur she forced out in her second year. In their third year Hermione answered with a slap Dracaena had earned. Hermione was also the first to see what Dracaena felt for Harry, and in the end she was among the first to forgive her. Watching her tortured at the Manor, Dracaena understood that the girl she had wronged was worth ten of her. After the war they became friends, something neither would have believed possible.

Ron Weasley

A blood-traitor in her father’s eyes and an easy target in hers, Ron returned her contempt with interest for six years. His part in the Yule Ball plan broke her heart in fourth year. After the war the two settled onto good terms, much to Harry’s delight.

Crabbe and Goyle

Her shadows from their first day, chosen for her by their fathers’ allegiances, and never her friends. Their casual nastiness she echoed more than she led. When she chose a half-blood openly over them they never forgave it, and they engineered Ada’s death in revenge. She faced them in the Room of Requirement, would not kill Goyle, and watched both die in Crabbe’s own fire.

Severus Snape

Her head of house and, through her mother’s Vow, her protector in her sixth year. He healed her after the washroom duel. On the Tower he finished the task she could not, and in doing so spared her a murder.

Albus Dumbledore

The man she was ordered to kill. Every one of her attempts on his life went wrong. When she finally stood before him on the Tower, wandless and failing, he spoke to her with a gentleness she had not expected, and she found she could not do it.

Lord Voldemort

The Dark Lord used Dracaena as a lever against her family and a punishment for her father’s failure. To him she was a tool to be thanked, then discarded. She defied him in front of both armies, held him at bay for a handful of seconds she was never meant to survive, and stood beside Harry when his own curse destroyed him.

Etymology

Dracaena comes from the Greek drakaina, “she-dragon,” the feminine counterpart of Draco. It is also the name of a genus of plants known as dragon trees. Malfoy is often read as the Old French mal foi, “bad faith.” Her middle name, Lucius, is her father’s.

Changes from Canon

  • A reimagining of Draco Malfoy as Dracaena Malfoy. Her family background and early life follow Draco’s closely.
  • Her rivalry with Harry becomes a romance: it nearly ignites at the Yule Ball, is torn apart by the war, and is sealed on the last day of school.

Trivia

  • Dracaena shares her birthday, 5 June 1980, with her canon counterpart, which makes her almost two months older than Harry.
  • Her Champion voice line, “My father will hear about this!”, is her canon counterpart’s best-known threat. After the war it reads very differently.
  • Her Patronus, a peacock, matches the white peacocks that roam the grounds of Malfoy Manor.
  • Of all wand cores, unicorn hair is the hardest to turn to the Dark Arts.
