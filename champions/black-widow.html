Black Widow

Natasha Romanoff

Striker

Molded by the Soviet Red Room into its deadliest weapon until she broke free to fight for the very world she was sent to deceive, Natasha Romanoff stands among the Avengers as the Black Widow — the most dangerous spy on Earth.

Natasha Romanoff was never given the chance to become anyone; she was built. Orphaned in the burning ruins of Stalingrad as a child, Natalia Romanova was delivered into the Red Room, where girls were trained against one another until only the best were left standing, and where even her memories became tools of her handlers — a ballerina’s life she never truly lived, written over the one she had. She was taught that she was a mission and nothing more, and for years she believed it, until an assignment against Tony Stark brought her face to face with the heroes she had been sent to destroy. Clint Barton saw something in her worth reaching for, and her defection became the first decision that was ever entirely her own. She has served the Avengers and S.H.I.E.L.D. ever since, though never as the bright, banner-waving kind of hero. She takes on the work no one else will, in the places no one else will go, and she has never once asked to be forgiven for who she used to be. Behind the dry wit and the cool, unreadable composure is a woman fiercely and quietly loyal to the few she calls family, sworn that no other girl will be broken the way she was. She doesn’t ask anyone to call her a hero. She just keeps doing the work, paying back what she owes one debt at a time.

Her body bears the Red Room’s craftsmanship as surely as her mind does. A Soviet answer to the Super-Soldier serum honed her strength, speed, agility and endurance to the edge of human limits, quickened her healing and held back the march of age, keeping her decades younger than the history she carries. On that frame rests a lifetime of training few on Earth can match: a dancer’s grace turned to lethal acrobatics, mastery of a dozen fighting styles, deadly marksmanship, and fluency in more languages than most people could name. Bracelets at her wrists house the Widow’s Bite, stingers that loose crackling bolts of electricity, alongside a grappling Widow’s Line that carries her across rooftops and through any gap in a defense. Yet her sharpest weapon has always been the mind behind them: her gift for reading everyone in a room, becoming whoever she needs to be, and finding the crack in every lock, alibi and lie. The Red Room set out to make the perfect weapon. It never imagined the day she would choose her own side.

"We are not heroes. We are tools. And tools get used."

What the Red Room Took w/ The Winter Soldier

Before he was a ghost story and before she was the Black Widow, James Barnes was the man the Red Room assigned to teach Natalia Romanova how to fight. Somewhere between the lessons they fell in love, the one thing in that place that was never on anyone’s orders. When their handlers found out, they took it the way they took everything else: they wiped it from both of them and sent them into the world as strangers. Natasha spent decades not knowing what she’d lost. When it finally came back, it came back in pieces, to two people who had each become someone else in the meantime. She doesn’t pretend they can pick up where they left off. But Bucky is the only person alive who knew her inside the Red Room and loved her anyway, and she has no intention of letting anyone take that from her twice.

The First Assignment w/ Iron Man

Tony Stark was the mark that brought Natasha to America. She was a Soviet spy sent to steal his secrets and sabotage his work, and she very nearly managed it. Since then she has studied the man more closely than almost anyone alive, closely enough to know that the arrogance is real, the genius is real, and the guilt beneath both runs deeper than he will ever say aloud. Tony has never entirely stopped glancing over his shoulder when she’s in the room. He jokes about it constantly, which is how he admits it. She respects him for that. He trusted her with his eyes open, knowing exactly what she was capable of, and in a life built on people trusting her blindly, she has come to value that kind of trust far more. Of every mission she ever ran against him, losing was the best thing that ever happened to her.

Straight Answers w/ Captain America

Natasha has a lie ready for everyone she meets, and Steve Rogers is one of the few people she no longer bothers to use one on. He is the most transparent man she has ever known, a soldier who says what he means and means what he says. To someone raised to treat every truth as a matter of circumstance, he was first a puzzle, then a provocation, and finally a relief. She pushes him on his idealism, his stubbornness, and the gap between the world he believes in and the one they actually work in, because someone has to, and he listens because she’s usually not wrong. He pushes back just as hard, holding her to the better instincts she insists she doesn’t have. Neither of them has ever quite won the argument. Both of them are better for having it.

Before the Widow w/ Wolverine

Logan remembers a little girl in Madripoor in 1941, an orphan he and Steve Rogers pulled from the hands of the men hunting her and returned to the one guardian sworn to protect her. Natasha doesn’t remember it at all. The Red Room saw to that, the same way Weapon X carved holes in Logan’s own past, which makes him one of the last living witnesses to a childhood she can’t reach. Neither of them is the sentimental type. They have fought beside each other, against each other and around each other for decades, trading barbs the way old soldiers do. But every so often he’ll mention something about the kid she used to be. Natasha trusts almost no one’s version of her life, least of all her own, and she finds that she believes his.

No Masks Left w/ Batgirl

Natasha speaks dozens of languages and can lie fluently in every one of them. Cassandra Cain was raised with no language but the body’s, and she reads it so perfectly that no lie survives her gaze. For the first time in her life, Natasha found herself across from someone she simply could not deceive. It unsettled her more than any enemy had in years, until she realized how restful it was. They were forged the same way, little girls trained into killers by people who saw nothing in them but a weapon. Cass rejected it with her whole heart and never wavered, and Natasha, who took far longer to find her way out, regards her with something close to awe. She keeps a quiet watch over the younger woman all the same. She swore that no one would ever do to another girl what was done to her, and Cass is the reminder of why that promise is worth keeping.

The Side She Was Born To w/ Dracaena Malfoy

Natasha recognized the performance the moment they met: the cultivated sneer, the cold poise, the cruelty worn like a uniform someone else had picked out. She wore the same one for years. Dracaena was raised to serve monsters for the sake of her family, and Natasha to serve a regime that never once asked her name. Both learned early that the mask was safer than the face beneath it, and both eventually turned their backs on the only side they had ever known. What Natasha offers is the part no one warns you about: defecting is not the end of it, the habits of the mask outlast the cause, and you will spend years being looked at sideways by the very people you came over to stand beside. She doesn’t soften any of it. She simply makes sure Dracaena never has to wonder whether someone in the room understands.
