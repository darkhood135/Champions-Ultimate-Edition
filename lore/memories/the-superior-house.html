The Superior House

Dracaena Malfoy Memory · The Wizarding World

Torchlight lit the room as the various Heads of House sat at the High Table, students at their respective tables, a nervous yet excited atmosphere filling the hall. Minerva McGonagall of Gryffindor, Pomona Sprout of Hufflepuff, Filius Flitwick of Ravenclaw and, of course, Dracaena’s idol: Severus Snape of Slytherin. Tonight was the night they’d find out who won the House Cup. The students in the Great Hall, especially those in Slytherin, sat in eager silence as Dumbledore walked up to the podium. Dracaena was no different, excitedly hopping up and down in her seat as she awaited the news. After all, she already knew Slytherin had won. Their hourglass in the entrance hall had dominated over the others the entire year, and the antics of Potter, Longbottom and Granger had only doomed Gryffindor and Ravenclaw to failure.

472 points, she reminded herself.

“Another year has passed! How glorious to see you all on this fine night.” He nodded with a cheerful smile. “And quite the year it has been, hm? I hope you’ve all had your fill of knowledge for quite a while.” The hall echoed with agreement. “Indeed, indeed, I thought so. I’d be quite pleased with myself if I were you, for all your hard work and effort! Especially you first years! Oh, the first year is not an easy one, that’s for sure. But you’ve all made it here tonight to celebrate regardless! A round of applause, if you may, for yourselves.” The hall clapped, teachers and students alike. Dracaena glanced around, giggling to herself as she noticed that the other houses didn’t have nearly the same level of excitement.

“Anyhow, children, I will not continue to lecture you with my babbling old talk. In my old age, I do tend to ramble! But I know you’re all looking forward to eating and maybe even… the results of the House Cup?” Slytherin began to holler and shout, Dracaena the loudest, as Dumbledore chuckled to himself, giving them their moment as the rest of the hall sat in silence and annoyance. When they settled down, Dumbledore cleared his throat.

“However, I want you to remember, children, that the winner of the cup does not determine your place in our school. You will forever be welcome and appreciated at Hogwarts, and I’m quite pleased to have such a gifted lot of younglings in attendance. Every single one of you, every single house, has accomplished great things! Still, we do, indeed, have a victor. But before that… I have a few extra points I must award.”

Dracaena’s heart sank a little, but she remained optimistic.

Extra points? What does he mean?

Those in the hall glanced at each other and muttered, confusion adorning their faces. It seemed as if even the teachers had no idea what he was referring to. That worried her a little, but the worry eased when she saw Professor Snape looking as confident as ever.

“Firstly, to Ron Weasley of Hufflepuff.” Dumbledore spoke as Dracaena gasped, glancing over at the Hufflepuff table. Everyone, likewise, looked at Weasley in shock. “For the best game of chess that Hogwarts has ever seen, and for loyalty unmatched, 50 points to Hufflepuff.” Their table erupted in excitement as a few of the students crowded around Weasley, huddling and hollering, his face blushing red with a slight smile. Dracaena couldn’t believe that the failed Gryffindor had actually accomplished something of his own.

It makes no difference anyway, she reminded herself, they’re too far behind.

“Next,” Dumbledore began, “to Hermione Granger of Ravenclaw. For cool logic and a sharp mind in the face of fire, 50 points to Ravenclaw.” Dracaena nearly groaned as she looked over at Granger, her expression caught in shock as her housemates cheered her on. She hid her face as though embarrassed, her ears reddening behind her hands.

That should put them at… 426. Still beneath us.

“Next, to Neville Longbottom of Gryffindor.” The words had barely left Dumbledore’s mouth before the grumbling across the Slytherin table began, Dracaena herself utterly baffled. Even Gryffindor looked puzzled, but Dracaena noticed one boy who appeared quite happy for Longbottom, putting a hand on his shoulder as he smiled. Longbottom’s eyes went wide in shock, his face turning pale, his mouth opening into a wide grin.

Potter. How typical of you.

“You’re telling me that coward earned them points?” Goyle groaned quietly. Dracaena only shook her head in worry, unsure where this was heading. “For standing up to a friend to do what’s right, 50 points to Gryffindor.” When this line left Dumbledore’s mouth, Gryffindor began to pile onto Longbottom in sheer excitement.

Why are they…? This won’t make them win. They’re still only at 412.

“Finally, to Harry Potter of Gryffind—” The hall was deafening before Dumbledore could even finish, Slytherin booing and Gryffindor cheering as everyone joined in the ruckus. Dracaena booed the boy as best she could, hoping her voice carried above the rest. She glanced over to see Weasley and Granger cheering with glee, an excitement that left a sour taste in her mouth.

No, they can’t do this, they can’t! But… of course he’d…! Everyone knows he’s from Gryffindor, he’s going to rig it! He’s going to—

“Ahem,” Dumbledore started, and the noise died down. “For outstanding courage, and for heroism nigh unmatched…” The hall went quiet. This number would determine everything.

“50 points to Gryffindor!” Instant relief washed over Dracaena as it all began again, the cheering and the booing.

He… didn’t.

She couldn’t help but notice Professor Snape clapping slowly, his lip curving into a subtle smile. Dumbledore waited, a slight frustration on his face as he eyed those who were booing (mostly from Slytherin), before opening his mouth again.

“Now, now then. Settle down. That’s all for the bonus points tonight. And that means… Slytherin wins the House Cup!” At this, Slytherin erupted once more, realization dawning on them. Dracaena was the first out of her seat, hands in the air, and as the others saw her, they followed in turn. Slytherin pride radiated through the hall with an energy unseen before, hollers and screeches galore. Dumbledore simply shook his head and smiled lightly, clapping his hands as Slytherin banners fell from the ceiling.

Professor Snape held a smile of pure satisfaction, nearly unable to contain his glee. Dracaena glanced over at the other Heads of House as well, curious to see their reactions. McGonagall looked furious, Sprout looked mildly annoyed, and Flitwick appeared bored. Excited, Dracaena ran to Snape’s side as the Heads of House all went up to shake his hand, donning fake smiles easily seen through. She grabbed at Snape’s cloak as McGonagall turned away, stomping lightly. “Professor Snape, Professor Snape, we did it!” Snape, initially annoyed, softened as he glanced down to see Dracaena. “Indeed, the superior house wins again. Well done, Malfoy.” Dracaena smiled wide and pumped her fist in the air, sprinting back to Crabbe and Goyle. She couldn’t wait to rub it in everyone’s faces. She turned her head to see that look on Potter’s face when—

Puzzled, she paused her celebrating. He… he didn’t look mad. He looked… happy. Content.

What…?

She followed his gaze and saw him looking between Granger and Weasley, the three of them exchanging glances and waves from afar. They… they didn’t look too upset, either. Something about that made her feel… sad. Mean, even. Unsure how to proceed, she sat puzzled by her own emotions as Crabbe opened his mouth.

“What do those losers have to celebrate?” Crabbe snickered, his eyes on the Slytherin banners now lining the hall. “You’d think that they’d won the cup.” Goyle laughed alongside him as Dracaena looked at them in confusion. “Y-yeah… how… weird,” Dracaena muttered, faking a laugh as she kept her eyes on Potter.

He… he truly is an enigma.

“Come on, Malfoy, don’t let those losers get you down! We still won, and they lost miserably even with those cheap points!” Goyle urged, patting Dracaena on the back as she jumped in surprise. She thought for a moment and decided, ultimately, that he was right. “Yeah… yeah, you’re right, this is our day!” And just like that, Dracaena’s energy was back. Why did she care what Potter had to think, anyway? Their victory was more important than whatever sentimental stuff he was thinking!

They feasted like there was no tomorrow, as she tried to pretend none of it bothered her.
