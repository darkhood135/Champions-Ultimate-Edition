Memory

Uncomfortable Resemblance

Harry Potter Memory · The Wizarding World

Harry walked into Madam Malkin’s shop, his nerves rising without Hagrid by his side. The place itself was mystical, lined wall-to-wall with fabric and various robes. It put him at ease instantly, which helped when Malkin walked over, clutching her cane in hand.

“You’re here for Hogwarts, dear?” She said, and as Harry opened his mouth to talk, she continued. “Follow me, we’ve got another student being fitted as we speak.” Malkin walked Harry to the back of the shop, grabbing a stool for him and prepping him for fitting. As Harry stood on the stool, he took a moment to glance at the girl being fitted adjacent to him; a blond, pretty girl with green eyes and a small stature. As they began the pinning and robe adjustments, Harry felt like he needed to make small talk. “Hello.” Harry said, not sure how to proceed.

“Hi.” The girl responded, eyes not even looking towards him. “You’re heading to Hogwarts too?”

“Yeah.” Harry responded, and before he even got another word out, she continued her speech. “My father’s busy buying me books and my mother’s looking at wands for me. I’m hoping they can get me a racing broom! Technically those aren’t allowed for first years but…” The girl smirked. “I’m sure I can find a way to get one!”

“How?” Harry asked, assuming the best intentions. “Well, I’ll smuggle one in of course. My father’s pretty important, you know!”

It’s like being back home, Harry thought, realizing the type of person he was speaking to.

There was awkward silence for a few moments as, still, the girl never even so much as glanced his way. Then, she opened her mouth again with an odd question. “What house do you think you’ll be in?”

Harry thought for a moment, trying to recall all of the information he’d been jam-packed with over the past twenty-four hours. “Not sure. Guess I’ll just find out when it happens.” Malfoy let out a light scoff.

“Well, surely you hope for at least one of them! I want to be a Slytherin, like my father. What house were your parents?” Harry, unsure how to respond without getting too deep into his past, opted to give an easy answer. “Not sure.” The girl shook her head lightly, taking a deep breath. “You don’t have very interesting responses, do you?”

“I guess not.” Harry responded. “I just don’t know much about this place yet.”

“Oh,” the girl responded, “well, you could probably find someone to guide you around, or whatever.” Harry, uncomfortable from this girl’s attitude, opted to stay silent for a few minutes.

The next time he glanced over at her, she was still staring ahead, eyes focused on something. Figuring that maybe there was something of interest, Harry redirected his eyes there only to spot Hagrid through the window. Confused, he glanced back at the girl and asked. “What are you looking at?”

“Oh. That strange guy outside, of course.” The girl said, with no hesitation or care. “I’ve heard he’s a Hogwarts employee. But I kind of find myself wondering… is he, like, a magical creature of some kind? He looks very weird.”

That judgment struck Harry immediately, bringing him back to Dudley’s attitudes and mannerisms.

Maybe this whole experience won’t be quite as different as I thought.

Harry took another glance at Hagrid when she said this, finding himself wanting to defend Hagrid’s honor, as though in an argument with his cousin. “He’s just larger than most people. But he’s great when you get to know him.” The girl let out a light chuckle. “Yeah, I’m sure. Father says he’s not worth my time.”

“Well, maybe your father might be wrong.” Harry blurted out, not thinking before he spoke. That same instinct had gotten him in trouble with the Dursleys plenty. The girl, now frustrated, narrowed her eyebrows before she finally glanced his way. “My father’s never wrong! You just don’t know him like I do.” She exclaimed, crossing her arms as the woman working on her robe grumbled. “If you knew him, you’d know that I’m a Malfoy, and the Malfoys are the purest, greatest bloodline. Surely you’ve heard of us?”

Harry blinked, trying to recall. “…No, I don’t think so.”

“That’s too bad. But soon you’ll know all about us! I’ll be the most popular girl in school!” Malfoy flipped her hair with pride, flashing a smile. “But if you want my friendship, you have to earn that from me, of course. It’s a multi-step process.”

This person can’t be real.

Malfoy’s process was finished up, and as she stepped off the stool she finally gave Harry a proper glance, her eyes narrowing on his face a little.

“You do look somewhat familiar. Well, see you in Hogwarts I suppose.” Malfoy said, carelessly waving her hand in a weak goodbye as she walked out of the store with a swagger that matched her personality.

Harry, hearing Malkin let out a sigh behind him, glanced over. “You’ll find out soon, dear boy… those Malfoys are quite the characters.”

“She reminds me of my cousin.” Harry muttered. “It doesn’t get much worse than that.”
