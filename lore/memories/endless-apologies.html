Memory

Endless Apologies

Harry Potter Memory · The Wizarding World

“You’ve got to get over it, Harry.”

Everyone was always quick to assume Harry’s obsession with Malfoy was romantic; perhaps driven by the night they shared a dance together at the Yule Ball. The crowds of all houses, mouths agape, shocked at the sudden unity between the two biggest icons of Slytherin and Gryffindor. That night, amidst others, would haunt Harry since it happened.

It would be one thing if Dracaena was any other girl. But she wasn’t; she was a Malfoy. From the start, it seemed, she was destined to follow in her family’s footsteps. Live out cruelty, embrace the vile, wicked ways of the average Slytherin. Most would leave it at that and believe her to be a spoiled, bratty child. Harry certainly did once. When he met her, and all throughout their first few years, she only proved herself insufferable. But now was different; he’d seen the glimpses of guilt and sorrow amidst her actions, a wanting to belong where she felt she couldn’t, a true warmth for him that shook him to his core.

It was one dance. It means nothing.

Regardless, it wasn’t her warmth that drew him this year, but the strange absence of her in the halls. The inactivity where she once used to be, and more importantly, the hint that she may have more to do with her father’s allegiances than she let on. She warned him consistently to stay away, to leave her be. Harry couldn’t, both for Hogwarts and for her. If there was even a chance she was connected to the assassination attempt that almost killed Ron, he had to do something. He knew she must have a connection to Voldemort and he prayed it was unwilling.

Like a routine, Harry pulled out the Marauder’s Map, searching for Dracaena’s name against the parchment. He’d tried this many times, the name flickering in and out and popping up in spots he wouldn’t expect. But today, he got lucky.

Dracaena’s name flashed in the sixth-floor bathroom. He inhaled sharply as he shoved it back into his robe, sprinting up the stairs despite the strange glances he received. He had no idea when she’d up and disappear again; time was of the essence.

For a minute, he nearly stopped himself and walked back. What if she was just using the bathroom? But he couldn’t take that chance; she’d been hidden away from the map’s gaze for so long that she must’ve been up to something. As stupid as it sounded, even to himself, he knew the simple answer couldn’t be true. As he reached the sixth floor, he crept towards the bathrooms casually, scanning to see if he’d spot her leaving. As he reached the doors, separated by gender, he hesitated; he didn’t belong in there, especially in such a public location. As he weighed his options, he heard the faint noise of crying on the other side of the door. That shut his worries up completely as he put his ear against it, straining for whatever he could make out amidst the normal sounds of running water. The first voice he heard was, shockingly, Moaning Myrtle.

“Tell me what’s wrong, Dracaena. I’m sure we can figure it out…” Her voice tried to soothe, speaking with a cadence not unlike a professor or parent.

“It’s not like that, Myrtle! I can’t tell you! I can’t just…” The sound of sniffling. “He’ll kill me and… they’ll kill my parents.”

What?

Harry began to crack open the door, keeping his ears focused on what he was hearing.

“Who, Dracaena? Please, just—”

“I can’t, Myrtle! I have to do this alone. Please, just… just leave me alone.” Her sobs became even more frantic, unceasing. It pained Harry to hear, part of him itching to comfort her and—

She’s working for Voldemort.

That thought made him open the door. There she was, Dracaena, cupping her face in her hands as she leaned over the sink, her makeup running down her face. She looked utterly defeated, her robe thrown carelessly to the side of the room. When Myrtle began to speak again (“Please, Dracaena, tell me”), Malfoy looked up at the mirror, double-taking as she caught a glimpse of Harry. Immediately, she stopped cold, her eyes widening slightly as they held eye contact with him. Harry didn’t move a muscle, waiting to see her next action, as he debated his choice of words. He hadn’t yet thought this throu—

Malfoy, spinning around in an instant, aimed her wand and fired. Harry reacted just in time, darting to the side, as the door splintered. “Wait, wait! Stop!” Myrtle shouted, as Harry fired back in turn. Each of them dodged around the room, blasting various parts of the bathroom with their inaccuracies. “Wait, wait, Malfoy, wait,” Harry tried to tell her, trying to calm her down so he could catch his breath and simply talk to her, but Malfoy had other plans. “Everte Statum!” she shouted. Harry tried to dodge as it struck him right in the chest, sending him flying into the side of the room. Myrtle’s cries and pleas continued as he hit the wall, letting out a shout of pain, slamming onto the ground with a thud. He felt crippled as he tried to get up, his legs weak as Malfoy readied yet another spell. “Malfoy, please, just let me—” Harry pleaded, his instincts taking over now as her wand fired again. It struck the wall behind him as Harry barely scraped by it. “Malfoy, just listen to me! Please!” It was no use. Her tears ran wild, panic in her expression that showed how desperate she was. Her mouth opened to utter another. “Cru—” She stopped herself short, but Harry knew the spell before she even finished her sentence. If she’d fired that…

Adrenaline filled his veins as, in an instant, Harry was brought back to earlier that day; the spell “for enemies”, Sectumsempra. If there was any time to use it, it was now. With his whole chest, Harry spoke the spell he believed would be his savior, utterly terrified at what she’d fire next. “Sectumsempra!”

Malfoy’s eyes widened as Harry spoke it, regret and panic mounting her face. In an instant, her body was sliced clean; once, twice, thrice. Her clothes torn to shreds and her blood leaking like a faucet, she fell to her knees.

Harry couldn’t believe his eyes at first. He stared at her for a moment, unable to come to terms with what lay before him. As the horror finally grasped him, terror and anguish struck him with an iron fist. He dashed to Malfoy’s bloodied side and caught her before she hit the cold, hard floor, holding her back as he cradled her. “Malfoy, fuck, I didn’t mean—” Her eyes were already out of focus, her breathing rattled as she let out wheezes of agony. The slashes coated Harry’s robe in red as he tried to cover her wounds the best he could. It had all happened so fast; their fight, the realization, the spell. He was about to lose her; he was about to kill her.

“Dracaena, Dracaena please, stay awake, please. Please! Please, just stay awake!” Harry tried to bring to mind any healing magic, anything at all that could help him, as Myrtle screamed “Murder!” as loud as she could. Harry wouldn’t even refute it; like he hadn’t in years, Harry’s tears streaked down his face in utter grief, puddling off of his cheeks and onto Malfoy as he clung to her tightly. “SOMEONE! SOMEONE HELP, PLEASE!!” Harry cried, hysterical. He clutched her closer, hugging what would soon be her corpse, as her shaky breath landed on his neck. He couldn’t lose her now, he couldn’t.

Please, God, please—

“Ha… Harry… s-sorry…” her voice uttered, barely audible amidst her broken state. Harry looked down at her as he shook, his words failing him as he stared her in the eyes. She didn’t appear angry, she didn’t even look scared. She just looked sad, defeated. Like she’d already surrendered. He was utterly destroyed that, even in the state she was in, she sought to apologize to him.

“No, Malfoy, don’t die on me yet, please, I still need you—” His words, like fuel, kept her eyes open as her consciousness began to fade. He hated himself; what he’d become, how he’d failed her. She let a weak smile creep to her lips as her eyes began to shut.

“No, no, no, no, please! Dracaena, please hold on a little longer! I’m so sorry, I’m so sorry, I’m so sorry…” he clung tighter and tighter, like that would stop what came next. Before he could utter more apologies, Professor Snape burst through the door. Myrtle continued to scream as Harry’s eyes darted up to him, his glasses tumbling off his face and hitting the floor. Harry’s teeth gritted as he hyperventilated. “Snape, please, God, please help her!! Please, professor!!” Snape, aghast at the sight, looked at Potter with an unfamiliar expression as he sprinted next to Malfoy. Harry had never seen him so emotionally struck; he didn’t even seem mad at first, but that didn’t last long. His gaze narrowed before tipping into pure rage and focus.

“Move, Potter!!” Harry let go of her as she turned to face Snape with her eyes, blood now pooling at the edges of her mouth. Snape began to chant, drawing his wand over Malfoy’s injuries, as he uttered the countercurse. A small part of Harry held onto hope, a glimmer amidst the sea of blood.

Please, Snape, please, you can hate me forever, just please don’t let her die.

As Harry watched on, teeth chattering and his face a mess, he saw the wounds begin to seal themselves shut. Malfoy’s raspy breathing began to ease slightly as she started to weep with the little energy she had. Her eyes filled with life once more as Harry’s anguish began to morph into relief, his cries transitioning into hesitant, overjoyed laughter.

“It’s done.” Snape finally breathed again, looking red in the face as though exhausted.

Malfoy’s cuts were mended, patches of scarred skin where her injuries were just a moment ago. He lifted Malfoy’s head up as she began to pass out, her energy utterly depleted. Harry opened his mouth to speak as Snape gave him a glare. “She’s going to be okay, Potter. I made sure of it.” Harry could only weakly nod as Snape questioned Malfoy whether she could walk. Exhausted, she didn’t even get a word out before her eyes shut and her breathing began to steady.

Snape sharply turned to Harry, shouting with fury. “Come on then, Potter! Help me bring her to the hospital wing!” He picked up Harry’s glasses and shoved them onto his face with force, nearly pushing his head against the wall. Harry didn’t care, couldn’t care. He picked up Malfoy with warmth, grabbing her under her back and thighs as he prepared for his long walk beside Snape.

They stepped outside of the bathroom, Snape and Harry with the unconscious Dracaena in his arms, where a crowd had already built up all around them. Snape grabbed Harry by the collar like a prisoner; his classmates around him gawked and covered their mouths. Harry Potter was an attempted murderer. He glanced over and spotted Ron and Hermione at the front of the crowd, their faces concerned and pleading for answers. Harry had no idea when they’d get those answers. He just knew that, whatever came for him next, was deserved. Next was Crabbe and Goyle, held back by their Slytherin friends and Snape’s comments as they nearly killed him then and there. Then, there was Ada; her tears flowed down her face as she looked away, cupping her mouth with her hand.

The walk of shame was lit only by the hope that was Dracaena’s breathing. Though still a little shaky, Harry had never heard something so beautiful. He continued to cry the entire way, his glasses now marked with a single crack from the bathroom he dropped them in. A resolve stuck in him, then. That potions book needed to exist no longer. It’d only caused him trouble and misery, some of which was too brutal to ever be needed by anyone ever again. He vowed, then, to burn the book; but first, his priority swung to Malfoy. He’d stay the night with her in the ward if need be until she awoke; and if she wanted him to leave, so be it.

Though Harry had learned Malfoy served Voldemort, it was a hollow victory. And that alignment, he was certain from the bottom of his heart, wasn’t who she truly was. Never again would he question it; concern himself with the notion that maybe she was as he thought all along. No. Malfoy was a victim. And Harry would prove it, even if it meant his own reputation had to plummet first.

As they neared the hospital wing, Snape’s hand still on his neck, Harry took another glance at Malfoy. Her face shone in the lights of the hall, radiance adorned on her face as Harry acknowledged her beauty once more. Even in the state she was, she remained—

Selfish. Selfish, selfish, selfish.

Harry hated himself, but he no longer could hide it. He had something deep in his heart for her. He could pretend all he wanted, but in the face of something so simple… he knew he loved her. He could only hope that she’d forgive him; and that, in time, they’d be on the same side of their war.
