Anya

Anya Musume

Healer

The gentle nurse of the freighter Tulpar, failed by everyone who should have protected her and lost to the dark, Anya has been pulled back from death into a second life she was never meant to have.

Anya was the one who took care of everyone else, and no one took care of her. As the Tulpar's nurse she was its quiet, steady heart — the soul who bound the wounds and soothed the fears of a crew that would, in the end, fail her utterly. Isolated aboard a ship with no one to turn to, she was wronged and betrayed in the cruelest way a person can be, abandoned by the very people sworn to keep her safe, and left to carry that terror alone until it consumed her. She died frightened, and she died failed, and for a long time that was all her story was permitted to be. But it is no longer where it ends. Torn back from death and cast into a splintering multiverse, Anya has been handed the one thing her life never once offered her — another chance at simply being, and with it the slow, uncertain, frightening work of becoming something more than the victim the Tulpar made of her. What defines her is the thing that all of it could not kill: a caregiver's heart that fear, betrayal, and death itself never managed to put out.

Even at her most afraid, Anya's first instinct was always to reach for someone else — to steady a shaking hand, to close a wound, to keep another person breathing when she could barely breathe herself. That instinct, and the training beneath it, is what she carries onto a battlefield she never asked to stand on. Where the others around her fight to bring ruin, she fights to hold the line of the living, mending the hurt and drawing the fallen back from the edge with quiet, practiced hands. She is no warrior and has no wish to be one; her strength is the rarer and harder kind — the fierce compassion of someone who knows exactly what it is to be helpless and afraid, and who refuses, now, to let anyone beside her feel that way alone. Resurrected into chaos, the woman no one ever protected has become a protector in her own right — and in reclaiming the power to keep others safe, she begins, at long last, to reclaim herself.

"Our worst moments don't make us monsters."

Forgiveness, in Time w/ Curly

Once, Anya had been glad of Curly — had trusted him, warmed to him, felt the first flicker of something that might have been more, on a ship where he was supposed to be the one she was safe with. That is precisely why his failure cut the way it did: the man who looked away was not a stranger but someone she'd let close, and no betrayal wounds like one from a person you believed was on your side. Seeing him again splits her clean in two. The hurt of it hasn't dissolved because he's broken now — and he is broken, hollowed by a guilt she can read in every step he can't steady and every glance he can't hold. But Anya was never able to stop caring for people, not even the ones who failed her, and least of all this one. Slowly, painfully, through a hurt that may never fully close, she finds she cannot hate the wreck of the man in front of her — and begins to wonder whether forgiveness, if she allows it, might set her free every bit as much as it would him.

Angels Who Escaped Hell w/ Dagger

Anya knows the look in Tandy Bowen's eyes, because she has worn it herself — the particular steadiness of someone who learned to give comfort precisely because they went so long without ever receiving any. Both women walked through their own private hell and came out the far side not hardened but somehow kinder, each choosing to become the light for others that no one had thought to be for them. In Dagger, Anya finds the rare peer who understands that healing is not weakness but the hardest kind of strength — that tending another's wounds can be a way of surviving your own. Two angels who clawed their way out of the dark, they mend the world side by side, and, quietly, each other.

Deniers of the Dark w/ Batgirl

Cassandra Cain and Anya were each handed a darkness they never asked for — one raised as a weapon and denied even the words to name it, the other wronged and failed by everyone who should have shielded her — and each made the same quiet, defiant choice: to refuse to become what that darkness wanted of them. Neither pretends the wound isn't there; they simply will not let it hold the pen. In Cassandra, Anya recognizes a kindred defiance — a woman who reclaimed a self that others tried to define for her, and turned the very pain of it toward protecting others from the same. They speak little of what was done to them. They don't have to. Each is, to the other, living proof that the dark does not get the final word.
