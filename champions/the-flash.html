The Flash

Barry Allen

Striker

Struck by a bolt of lightning that bound him to the Speed Force, forensic scientist Barry Allen guards Central City as the Flash — the Scarlet Speedster and the fastest man alive.

Barry Allen was chasing the truth long before he could outrun anything. He was only a boy when his mother was murdered in their home and his father was convicted of a crime he did not commit, and the grief of that night set him on a single path: to become the kind of man who finds answers when no one else will look. He grew into a forensic scientist for the Central City Police Department — patient, meticulous, and famously late to everything — until a lightning strike shattered a shelf of chemicals over him in his lab and connected him to the Speed Force, the energy that drives all motion through creation. He has carried the mantle of the Flash ever since with an optimism that his losses should have broken and never did. That hope has cost him dearly. Once, given the power to undo the worst day of his life, he ran back through time to save his mother and returned to find the world fractured around his choice, and letting her go again was the hardest thing he has ever done. It left him with the conviction that defines him still: the past is not his to rewrite, only the people in front of him to save. And so he gives them everything — a hero who looks for the best in everyone he meets, friend and enemy alike, and has never stopped believing he’ll find it.

The Speed Force makes him the fastest man alive, and speed is only where it begins. Barry can cross a continent in a heartbeat and land a thousand blows before an opponent finishes a breath; his mind and senses race alongside his body, so that to him a speeding bullet hangs in the air like something to be plucked away. He can vibrate his molecules fast enough to pass through solid walls, heals in hours from wounds that would cripple others for months, and at his limits can outpace time itself, running into the past, the future, and the worlds that lie beside his own. Paired with a scientist’s mind that turns every fight into a problem to be solved, it makes him one of the most dangerous heroes in any universe. But the speed was never what made Barry Allen the Flash. It’s that he spends every second of it on someone else — the man who’s late for everything except the moment someone needs him.

"Life is locomotion…if you're not moving, you're not living. But there comes a time when you've got to stop running away from things… and you've got to start running towards something, you've got to forge ahead. Keep moving. Even if your path isn't lit…trust that you'll find your way."

The Brave and the Bold w/ Green Lantern

Hal Jordan is the closest thing Barry has to a brother, and on paper the two should never have gotten along. One is a buttoned-up police scientist who has never once been on time; the other is a cocky test pilot who has never met a rule he couldn’t break, and each has spent years telling the other exactly how wrong he’s living. But beneath the bickering runs the easiest trust either man has ever known. Hal makes Barry braver, Barry makes Hal think twice, and whether the fight is a rogue loose in Central City or a war between the stars, each knows without asking that the other is already on his way. Barry may be late to everything else in his life, but never to Hal, and with one running on willpower and the other on lightning, there is very little in any universe that can keep up with them.

A Father's Letter w/ Batman

Barry once lived through a world where young Bruce Wayne died in that alley instead of his parents. In that world, a grieving Thomas Wayne took up the cowl and made it something far crueler. When Barry ran the timeline back into place, he carried home the one thing that other Batman pressed into his hand: a letter from a father to the son he never got to watch grow up. It’s a debt Bruce has never known how to repay, and a kinship Barry never expected. Both men were made by a murdered parent, and both chose to answer that grief by chasing the truth. Batman respects few minds the way he respects Barry’s, a forensic scientist who can read a crime scene nearly as well as he can. Barry, for his part, is one of the very few who can still see, beneath the cowl, the man Thomas Wayne hoped his son would be.

Race to the Finish w/ Superman

Barry and Clark have raced each other more times than either cares to count — around the world, across the solar system, once all the way to the end of the universe — and they still can’t agree on who won. It may be the friendliest rivalry in either man’s life: no grudges, no stakes beyond bragging rights, just two of the kindest heroes alive grinning at each other across the starting line like a pair of boys. Beneath it runs a deep and simple respect. Barry knows exactly what Superman means to the world, and Clark knows that Barry’s courage has never once depended on how fast he can run. Not that either of them will ever turn down one more race.

Running from What Was w/ The Summoner

Barry knows better than anyone what happens when one person decides they know how reality ought to go. He broke the timeline once to save his mother and woke into a world torn apart by that single act of love. So when he looks at the Summoner, a stranger whose fate seems tangled with every world at once, he sees a temptation he has already lived through and only barely survived. He doesn’t distrust them; Barry has never been able to distrust anyone who means well. But he worries, openly and often, and he asks the questions no one else thinks to: who decides what each world gets to keep, and who carries the weight when that choice turns out to be wrong? The Summoner listens. Barry can only hope they never have to learn the answer the way he did.
