The Sorcerer’s Stone is the first story arc of in . It covers ’s first year at : the discovery that he is a wizard, a friendship that crossed three of the school’s four houses, his first clash with , and his first confrontation with the remnant of over the Sorcerer’s Stone.

Story

Privet Drive

Before he was a wizard, Harry Potter was a secret the world had agreed to keep from him. Orphaned as an infant when the dark wizard who had terrorized magical Britain murdered his parents and then, inexplicably, broke himself against the baby he had meant to kill next, Harry was left on a doorstep and handed to an aunt and uncle, Petunia and Vernon Dursley, who wanted nothing to do with magic and less to do with him. For ten years he slept in the cupboard under the stairs, wore his cousin Dudley’s castoffs, and was assured that the strange things that happened around him were figments best left unmentioned. He grew up certain he was ordinary, unwanted, and alone.

That certainty cracked the summer he turned eleven, when letters addressed to him in emerald ink began arriving by the hundred, and Vernon’s furious efforts to burn, hide, and outrun them only summoned more. On a storm-battered night in a shack on a rock at sea, the sender came in person: Rubeus Hagrid, the Hogwarts gamekeeper, a mountain of a man with a tender heart and a tongue he could never quite govern. He told Harry the truth his family had spent a decade smothering. He was a wizard, his parents had been murdered rather than killed in any car crash, and a school called Hogwarts had been expecting him since the day he was born.

Diagon Alley

Hagrid led him into the hidden heart of the wizarding world, a crooked alley behind a grimy London pub. At Gringotts, the goblin-run bank buried deep beneath the city, Harry found a small fortune waiting in his parents’ vault, and watched Hagrid collect a small, grubby package from a second, high-security vault on Dumbledore’s orders. That vault was broken into the same night and found already empty. Hagrid bought him a snowy owl, Hedwig, for the birthday no one had ever marked. At Ollivander’s a holly wand chose him, and the old wandmaker told him an unsettling thing: the phoenix feather at its core had a twin, and the twin sat in the wand that had given him his scar.

At Madam Malkin’s, being measured for school robes, Harry stood beside a pale, poised girl his own age who talked about brooms, about houses, and about which sorts of people were worth knowing, in the easy way of a child reciting her parents’ opinions. They parted without trading names. Harry knew only that she reminded him, unpleasantly, of Dudley.

The Hogwarts Express

A month later a warm, chaotic family of redheads showed him through the barrier at King’s Cross: Molly Weasley shepherding her brood, the twins Fred and George already deep in mischief, the prim prefect Percy, and the youngest, Ginny, too young for school and unable to stop staring at the famous boy. On the train Harry fell into the first real friendship of his life, with Ron Weasley, the youngest of the brothers, who had freckles, a hand-me-down everything, and no notion that Harry Potter would ever choose to sit with him. They split a mountain of sweets from the trolley, and Harry’s first Chocolate Frog card bore the face of Albus Dumbledore, famed for defeating the dark wizard Grindelwald and for his work with the alchemist Nicolas Flamel. Harry pocketed it and forgot it. A bushy-haired girl named Hermione Granger put her head in to ask after a lost toad, already knowing every spell in the set books and making sure they knew it too.

Then the girl from the robe shop found the compartment. She was Dracaena Malfoy, she said, with the pride of a name she expected to mean something, and she came flanked by Crabbe and Goyle, two heavy, silent boys whose nastiness she echoed more than she led. Some wizarding families were worth more than others, she told Harry, and she could teach him to tell which. She put out her hand. Harry, who had already decided he liked Ron and disliked the company she kept, refused it, and her pride cracked into something sharper and more wounded before she turned on her heel and was gone.

The Sorting

At the castle a battered, talking hat sorted each first-year into one of four houses: brave Gryffindor, loyal Hufflepuff, clever Ravenclaw, and cunning Slytherin. Hermione went to Ravenclaw almost the moment it touched her. Neville Longbottom, a round-faced boy who had spent the whole journey hunting for his toad, went to Gryffindor. Dracaena went to Slytherin before the brim had settled, exactly as her family had always assumed.

The Hat took longer over Harry. It spoke to him in a voice only he could hear: there was ambition in him, a thirst to prove himself, and Slytherin could set him on the road to greatness. Harry sat frozen beneath it, too stunned to argue either way. Then the Hat made up its own mind and called out Gryffindor to the whole hall, and Harry went to the table where Minerva McGonagall, stern and fair and not a woman to cross, would be his head of house.

Ron came last. Every Weasley in living memory had gone to Gryffindor, and he walked up to the stool pale with the certainty that he would follow them. The Hat called Hufflepuff. The Gryffindor table went quiet, and Harry watched his friend cross the hall to the yellow-and-black table as if the floor had dropped away beneath him, until Fred and George were on their feet, the first in the room to cheer for him anyway.

The Seeker

The months that followed taught Harry how little he knew and how fiercely he loved learning it. Hogwarts years were small, and first-years took most of their lessons with all four houses together, so Harry and Ron sat side by side whatever their tables, with Hermione two rows ahead answering every question. Harry found an enemy in Severus Snape, the cold master of Potions and head of Slytherin, who seemed to loathe him on sight. He also found a gift for flying that outstripped students years his senior. At the first-years’ flying lesson Neville fell from his broom and broke his wrist, and Dracaena snatched up his Remembrall and dared Harry into the air after it. Harry, who had never sat on a broom in his life, caught it in a plunging dive. McGonagall saw the catch and, instead of expelling him, made him the youngest house Seeker in a century, handed over to the fanatical Gryffindor captain Oliver Wood.

The Third-Floor Corridor

Not to be outdone, Dracaena challenged Harry that same evening to a midnight duel she never meant to attend. Ron went as his second. Hermione, who had overheard, was waiting by the trophy room to tell them they would lose their houses every point they had, and Neville, locked out of Gryffindor Tower after forgetting the password, trailed after them because he had nowhere else to go. Instead of Dracaena they found the caretaker, Argus Filch, tipped off and hunting. Running from him, the four of them blundered through a locked door into the forbidden third-floor corridor, where an enormous three-headed dog stood guard over a trapdoor.

Halloween

The friendship that would define Harry’s life began on Halloween, and nearly didn’t. After a Charms lesson Ron mocked Hermione within her hearing, a girl with no friends and no idea why, and she spent the afternoon crying in a girls’ bathroom. At the feast that evening Professor Quirrell, the stammering Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher, ran in with word of a mountain troll loose in the dungeons. While the prefects herded the houses to their dormitories, Harry and Ron slipped away to warn her and found the troll already in the bathroom with her. Between Ron’s first real spell and Harry’s nerve they brought it down, and when the teachers arrived Hermione lied to McGonagall to cover for both boys and took the blame herself. That night Ron apologized for what he had said, and Harry for never having stopped him. After that the three of them were friends: a Gryffindor, a Hufflepuff and a Ravenclaw, which much of the school found stranger than the troll.

They had no common room to share, so they shared whatever corner they could find, a library alcove one week and an empty classroom the next. Most of all they had Hagrid’s hut at the edge of the Forbidden Forest, where the gamekeeper fed them rock cakes that could break teeth and became the first adult all three of them trusted.

Nicolas Flamel

Slowly they worked out what the three-headed dog, Fluffy, was guarding. Hagrid let slip the name Nicolas Flamel and could not unsay it. Hermione searched the library for weeks without finding him, until Harry turned out his pockets and found the forgotten Chocolate Frog card. Flamel was the only known maker of the Sorcerer’s Stone, which turned metal to gold and brewed a draught of immortality, and the Stone was the grubby package from the vault. The three grew certain that someone inside the castle meant to steal it, and that the someone was Snape.

The First Match

At Harry’s first Quidditch match, his broom turned savage beneath him and tried to throw him to his death. Hermione, certain she had found the culprit, set fire to Snape’s robes in the stands to break what she took for his curse. Harry kept his seat, caught the Snitch half by accident, and came down more convinced than ever that the Potions master was the thief in waiting.

The Mirror of Erised

Harry and Ron both stayed at the castle for Christmas, and Christmas brought Harry two gifts. The first was an unsigned parcel holding his dead father’s Invisibility Cloak, so fine and so old that it seemed to belong in a fairy tale, with a note that told him only to use it well. The second was what the Cloak led him to: long nights before the Mirror of Erised, an ancient glass in which his mother, his father and a whole family he had never known looked back at him. Ron, brought to see it, found himself in Gryffindor scarlet, holding the House Cup and standing at last above his brothers. On the last night Dumbledore found Harry there and told him gently that the happiest man alive would see only himself in that mirror, exactly as he was. Then he had it moved.

Norbert

The year’s worst trouble came, as it often did, from Hagrid’s soft heart. He had won a dragon’s egg from a hooded stranger over a game of cards, hatched the creature, Norbert, in his wooden hut, and could not bear to give it up. Ron’s brother Charlie, who worked with dragons in Romania, agreed to take it, and Ron was bitten helping to crate it. Harry and Hermione carried the crate to the top of the Astronomy Tower at midnight and handed it over. Dracaena, who had seen too much through Hagrid’s window, tried to get them caught and was caught herself; Neville, out of bed to warn Harry, was caught too. The points came off by the hundred. Gryffindor turned its back on Harry and Neville, Ravenclaw went cold on Hermione, and the shared disgrace bound Harry and Neville together more than any good day had.

The Forbidden Forest

The detention was served in the Forbidden Forest, where something had been slaughtering unicorns and Hagrid meant to find it. Terror slowed Dracaena to a crawl, and she and Harry fell behind the others. When a hooded shape rose from the body of a unicorn and came toward them, Harry put himself between it and her, and Dracaena bolted into the trees. Hagrid’s crossbow drove the creature off before it reached him, and Firenze, a centaur who had found the fleeing Dracaena, named it for them: the ruined remnant of Voldemort, surviving on unicorn blood while he waited for the Stone to make him whole.

Through the Trapdoor

When Hagrid let slip that Fluffy fell asleep at the sound of music, and Dumbledore was called away to London by a false summons the same day, the three were sure the theft was at hand. No adult would believe them, so they resolved to go after the Stone themselves and agreed to meet on the third floor that night.

Only one person tried to stop Harry. Neville planted himself in front of the portrait hole: he had lost points beside Harry once already, and he would not let him get himself expelled, or worse. Harry tried to explain and ran out of time. He put Neville gently aside with a binding spell Hermione had taught him, told the boy stiff on the floor that he was sorry, and went.

Ron and Hermione were waiting on the third floor. Past a sleeping Fluffy lay a gauntlet of the teachers’ own devising: a strangling plant that Hermione’s cool head got them through, a storm of winged keys Harry had to fly down, a giant chessboard where Ron let himself be struck down so the others could pass, and a riddle of potions only Hermione could untangle. Each trial proved just surmountable enough for three determined first-years, as if it had been built for exactly the children who would refuse to stay out of it. The trials thinned the three to one, and Harry walked on alone.

Quirrell

What waited at the end was not Snape at all, but timid Professor Quirrell, and riding the back of his skull, hidden beneath his turban, the barely living face of Voldemort himself. The Stone lay hidden inside the Mirror of Erised, and the Mirror gave it to Harry, who wanted only to keep it from them, and never to Voldemort, whose hunger betrayed him. When Quirrell seized him, the man’s skin blistered and burned at Harry’s touch, and Harry held on until the dark thing tore free of its crumbling host and the black closed over him.

The Leaving Feast

He woke days later in the hospital wing with Dumbledore at his bedside. The Stone had been destroyed, the headmaster told him, and Flamel and his wife had made their peace with the end of their long lives. Quirrell had not been able to bear Harry’s touch because Harry’s mother had died to save him, and a love like that left its mark in the very skin.

Slytherin led the House Cup going into the leaving feast. Dumbledore had a few points left to give, and he gave them: to Ron, for the best-played game of chess in many years; to Hermione, for cool logic in the face of fire; to Harry, for pure nerve; and last of all to Neville, for the courage it takes to stand up to a friend. Three tables erupted in turn, Gryffindor loudest of all for Neville. Then the count was read, and Slytherin still held the cup it had earned. Harry found he didn’t mind. He had cheered louder for Ron and Hermione than for himself, and no hourglass could take the year away from him.

The Journey Home

On the train back to London, Dracaena found him alone in the corridor, for once without Crabbe or Goyle. She spent a minute rubbing the cup in his face, as he had known she would. Then, with no one watching, she held out her hand, as she had on the journey to school. “You weren’t too bad, for a half-blood, Potter.” This time Harry took it, too surprised to do anything else, and she was gone down the corridor before he could find an answer. He still didn’t like her. But he had never expected her to give him anything, least of all that.

Then he went back to his aunt and uncle’s for the summer, changed in the one way that mattered most: he had somewhere to return from now, and people who would be waiting when he came back. The boy who had spent a lifetime believing he was no one had learned, at last, that he was someone, and, far more important to him, that he was not alone.

Changes from Canon

  • Draco Malfoy is reimagined as , Harry’s rival in this continuity. Her side of their first meeting is told in her own Memory, .
  • is sorted into and into , the first Weasley in living memory outside Gryffindor. The three friends come from three houses, and meet wherever they can, most often at Hagrid’s hut.
  • The Sorting Hat weighs Slytherin for Harry, then chooses Gryffindor on its own judgment. Harry doesn’t ask it to.
  • Neville stands up to Harry alone at the portrait hole, and Harry binds him himself, with an apology.
  • Dumbledore’s last-minute points honor Ron, Hermione, Harry and Neville but don’t change the result. Slytherin keeps the House Cup it earned.
  • On the journey home Dracaena offers Harry her hand a second time, and he takes it.
  • The Invisibility Cloak is introduced as something out of a fairy tale, and Harry’s first Chocolate Frog card names Grindelwald. Both seed the Deathly Hallows from the first arc, so they don’t arrive cold in the last.
  • The teachers’ gauntlet beneath the trapdoor is deliberately surmountable, a test built for exactly the children who would refuse to stay out of it. The Mirror of Erised is the true lock.
