The Shape

Michael Myers

Striker

Michael Myers was six years old when he murdered his sister on Halloween night, and the silent, motiveless evil that grew behind his eyes has stalked Haddonfield ever since as the relentless killer known as The Shape.

Nothing in Michael Myers’s childhood explains him. He was an ordinary boy on an ordinary street in Haddonfield, Illinois, until Halloween night of 1963. That night he climbed the stairs of his family home in a clown costume and took a kitchen knife to his older sister, Judith. Then he walked out onto the front lawn and stood in silence before his stunned parents. He never gave a reason. He never gave anything at all. He was committed to Smith’s Grove Sanitarium and spent the next fifteen years without speaking a single word, staring at a wall. Dr. Sam Loomis tried to find the child he was sure was hiding somewhere inside, until he understood that there was no child to find, only something patient, waiting. On the night before Halloween in 1978, it stopped waiting. Michael escaped, stole a pale, featureless mask, and came home. He doesn’t hunt for money, revenge, or pleasure, and he doesn’t hate. Calling what drives him madness would give it a logic it doesn’t have. The Shape simply chooses, watches, and kills, and the lack of any why is the most frightening thing about him. The people he marks are no more guilty than anyone else: a babysitter glimpsed through a window, a family living in the house that was once his. No plea and no locked door will turn him away from them.

No one has ever explained what Michael Myers is, and nothing has ever been able to end him. Whatever lives behind the mask gives him strength far beyond the body that carries it, enough to lift a grown man off his feet with one hand and pin him to a wall. His resilience borders on the impossible. He has been shot, stabbed, burned, and thrown from heights, and he rises again, often before his victims can catch their breath. The moment anyone looks away from the body, it’s gone. He never runs, because he doesn’t have to. He moves with slow, unhurried certainty, appearing in doorways and fading into hedgerows and dark closets as if the shadows were on his side. He kills with the simplest of tools, most often a kitchen knife, the same as the night it began. Yet his truest weapon is the dread he leaves behind. The Shape doesn’t need to be fast or clever, because he won’t stop and he can’t be bargained with, and somewhere out past the edge of the streetlight, he is already watching.

"I met him, 15 years ago; I was told there was nothing left; no reason, no conscience, no understanding in even the most rudimentary sense of life or death, of good or evil, right or wrong. I met this... six-year-old child with this blank, pale, emotionless face, and... the blackest eyes - the Devil's eyes. I spent eight years trying to reach him, and then another seven trying to keep him locked up, because I realized that what was living behind that boy's eyes was purely and simply... evil."

The Real Thing w/ Ghostface

Billy Loomis learned everything he knows from horror movies, and of all the monsters he studied, none ever loomed larger than the silent figure in the white mask. He even shares a surname with the doctor who spent fifteen years staring into Michael’s eyes. Now the Cataclysm has put the real thing in front of him. Stu can barely contain himself; Billy plays it cool, but he studies every step the Shape takes like a student at the feet of the master, and he has never wanted anyone’s approval more. Michael has never once looked his way. Billy tells himself that’s just part of the act.

Two of a Kind w/ Jason

Put Michael Myers and Jason Voorhees in the same clearing and the night goes quiet. Each is the closest thing the other has to a reflection: two masked figures who never speak, never run, and never stay dead, each the walking ghost story of the small town that made him. But Jason kills for a reason. He carries a drowned boy’s grief and a mother’s last command, and he turns them on anyone who strays too near Crystal Lake. Michael has never had a reason at all. Neither gives up ground or says a word. They simply watch each other, two patient things that have each found something they cannot outwait.

Already Dead w/ The Punisher

Frank Castle has spent his war making the guilty answer for what they’ve done, and he has never found anything guiltier than Michael Myers. The Shape kills the innocent, the one line Frank would die before crossing, and he is the one target Frank’s war can’t finish. Frank has emptied magazine after magazine into him, and every time the Shape has stood back up and kept walking. There is no fear to break, no confession to beat out of him, no crew behind him to tear down. The guilty fear Frank because he will never stop coming. Michael is the first thing Frank has ever hunted that he can say the same about.

Nothing to Dream w/ Freddy

Freddy Krueger lives on fear. He slips into his victims’ dreams, turns their worst nightmares into a playground, and grows stronger with every scream, and he never stops talking while he does it. Michael Myers is the one mind in the Cataclysm he can’t get into. Freddy has tried. He has prowled the edges of whatever passes for the Shape’s sleep and found nothing waiting there: no memory to twist, no terror to feed on, only the quiet behind the mask. It drives the Springwood Slasher up the wall. The man who built his name on being everyone’s worst nightmare has finally met someone who doesn’t have any.

No Punchline w/ The Joker

The Joker has always insisted that madness is just one bad day away, and that anyone pushed hard enough will laugh their way over the edge right along with him. Michael Myers is the joke he can’t make land. There was no bad day. There was an ordinary boy on an ordinary Halloween, and then there was the knife: no tragedy, no trauma, no setup at all, only the punchline, delivered without a smile. The Clown Prince of Crime is fascinated by him and offended in equal measure. He keeps performing for the Shape, hungry for an audience, and Michael keeps walking past him as though he weren’t there. For a man who needs the world to laugh, there is no worse insult.
