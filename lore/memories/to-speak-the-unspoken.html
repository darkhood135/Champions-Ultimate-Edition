To Speak the Unspoken

Harry Potter Memory · The Wizarding World

That night, Harry Potter needed time alone.

This wasn’t the first time he’d snuck out to walk the halls, not even close, but it was the first time he had needed to be quite this cautious. Umbridge and her cult gripped the school with an iron fist. Expelled from Quidditch and harassed everywhere he went, Harry could not catch a break. Even when he was out with his friends, it was nearly guaranteed that one of the Inquisitorial Squad would strike him with a point deduction or punishment for even the simplest of rebuttals. A cough or a yawn at the wrong moment was enough to warrant it in their eyes; or, in truth, they never needed anything to warrant it at all. Those who had joined the squad simply sought the power involved: the chance to strip other students of any and all agency.

It was a strange habit, one that had begun with his visits to the Mirror of Erised. He still visited it often, but he had come to enjoy the empty hallways for their own sake. They gave him a new perspective on the school, his true home, and time to plan for whatever came next. That night, his thoughts were fixed on one thing: stopping Umbridge.

As his thoughts escalated, his footsteps grew reckless. Too loud, too noticeable. Before he knew it, he was right in the patrol route of the squad’s most notorious member. He turned the corner and let out a sharp inhale as he nearly walked into her, stopping dead in his tracks. For just a moment, he thought he was safe. That hope crumbled as Malfoy halted. She tilted her head a little, seemingly aware, as she opened her mouth.

“I know you’re there, Potter.” The snide remark cut through the dry corridor. She was alone this time; no Crabbe or Goyle, no other Slytherin. Harry didn’t think he’d been quite that loud. He debated his next move for a moment, holding his breath as he stood beside her. Before he could make one, Malfoy tore the cloak clean off his back, tossed it violently aside, and backed up with her wand drawn. Harry drew his to match. They stood in the corridor, exposed, their wands pointed at one another with intention. Malfoy scoffed, anger creeping across her face as she eyed him up and down.

“You look like a mess, Potter. Spying on me again?” Her predatory eyes locked onto his face as though they could read straight through him.

“No, Malfoy. It’s not always about you,” Harry responded, annoyed.

Her eyes flickered with hurt pride as she began to lower her wand. She knew about his nightly habits by now; this wasn’t new to her. He lowered his own in turn, trying not to escalate anything further.

“To the mirror, then?” For a moment, her voice brought him back to their own night in that room.

“Yes… if you really need to know.” He pocketed his wand slowly, his fears easing a little as he waited for her to let him go on with his night. He couldn’t remember the last time he had truly spoken to her.

“You’re aware I need to report this, right?” Her voice came with a hesitation, and an uncomfortable silence filled the corridor. Harry’s heart dropped. Umbridge already despised him, and her power was great enough that he feared the worst if he were caught for anything else.

“You don’t have to.”

“I do,” she snapped back immediately. “I don’t have a choice.” Though she kept her wand down, Harry felt as though she might raise it at any moment. Now at her mercy, he opened his mouth to speak.

“Why do you always have a problem with me?” The question was genuine, a rising anger behind it. He’d seen Malfoy without her mask; he knew what she was and wasn’t capable of. Like it had never even been there, the purity he’d glimpsed at their dance had faded back into the same old Malfoy she’d always been. Spiteful, impatient, vindictive.

Malfoy snarled, her teeth showing as her silver eyes pierced him with the usual malice. “This shouldn’t be news to you, idiot. Wandering around at night is against policy.” She narrowed her eyes at him.

Harry’s anger began to rise to the surface. “But it’s not about that, is it? It’s never been about the rules for you. It’s about me. Because you can hold this over me.” He stood up straighter, frowning at her, something within him boiling. “You just want me out of the picture.”

“If I did, I would’ve gotten rid of you years ago,” Malfoy pushed out loudly. A long, deadly silence followed before Harry let himself speak again.

“Then why keep acting like this?”

“Because I have to. What do you not understand?” She stomped her foot, frustrated.

“I haven’t even done anything bad! No one would even know I was here. You have a choice, and yet you still choose to—”

“That’s easy to say from your side, Potter. You’ve only ever needed to make the easy choices.” She shook her head.

“How? Just because I’ve been told every day since I got to this school that I’m the one who needs to stop Voldemort, that doesn’t mean it’s not still on me to act—”

“Oh, poor Potter, a little bit of pressure from fellow students! I have duties to fulfill for my father—”

Harry’s voice cut in before she could finish. “So because you’re afraid of your father, I can’t hold you responsible for every time you’ve attacked my friends? Bullied or harassed me for sport? I should just accept that because you ‘need to do it’? What about all the people you’ve been abusing with your new gang?”

“Oh, please, don’t take the moral high ground with me! Acting as if you didn’t manipulate me to get information on my father—”

“Malfoy, I didn’t invite you just to—”

“Oh, so you’re calling your friends liars?”

“What are you even—”

“I overheard them that night, when I took that little cloak of yours. You thought you could hide it from me, didn’t you? I should’ve known a peasant like you would try to take advantage of me.”

“Malfoy, I wouldn’t have asked you if that was the only reason.”

“Right, because your word is trustworthy. You’re despicable, Potter, daring to lecture me when—”

“Do you think I would do that?” Harry roared, angered she would think that way at all.

Malfoy faltered for a moment, her words stuttering before she fired back. “I think you just wanted information from me!”

“Really? Need I remind you that you asked me first? And the first thing you did was mock me because Chao turned me down!” Like it was yesterday, that night flashed into his mind. He remembered how he’d felt: embarrassed, upset, ridiculed.

Malfoy snickered, a mocking grin plastered across her face. “Can’t handle teasing, Potter? I only pitied you anyway, never anything more. It looked good for my reputation to show mercy to such an idiot.”

“Is that how you feel about me?” Harry balled his fists, his face likely turning red now from frustration. “Clearly I made a mistake inviting you at all, since you seem to think so little of me!”

A flash of hurt and regret crossed Malfoy’s eyes as she screamed back. “Keep on playing the victim, Potter! You lied to me! Why would they say those things if they weren’t true?”

“That was their reason, it wasn’t mine!”

“How convenient! I don’t believe you!”

“Believe what you want, but it’s the damn truth! It was my choice! But whatever, I don’t care anymore. Hate me, despise me, I don’t care. Just let me leave and I won’t bother you ever again.”

She faltered again. “I… I can’t do that, you idiot. I have rules to uphold and a group I’m accountable t—”

“Why are you like this, Malfoy? Afraid your pride will be wounded when you get chewed out by Umbridge? God forbid she finds out you showed me grace—” Both their faces were red now, upset and confrontational.

“It’s not about that! It’s not about any of that! If I had a choice—”

“Stop saying that! I’m tired of it. Take some damn responsibility, for once. Recognize that you make these choices yourself!”

“Shut up!” she screamed, in a desperate yet enraged tone he had never heard from her before.

“You’re a coward, Malfoy!” Harry blurted, louder still. Her eyes flinched with hurt and fury, and she gritted her teeth as Harry lunged for his cloak on the floor. He’d soon make a scene, he realized, and he needed to escape. He dived for it and almost had it when Malfoy fired at the cloak. “Accio!” It flew into her hand as Harry nearly tripped.

“Don’t lecture me on what I am and what I’m not!” Malfoy shouted, pocketing the cloak violently. Harry’s fear escalated as he regretted ever leaving the common room that night. What was meant to be a simple walk had turned into a cause for detention, or worse.

“But it’s perfectly okay for you to lecture me and Hermione about our blood status and our—”

“I know what I’ve done, I know what I’ve said! You think I don’t hate myself for it?! You think I don’t know?!” Malfoy yelled, loud enough that someone might hear. Her eyes shimmered with tears she visibly held back, her voice shaking. Harry caught it in an instant. Her words threw him off guard, and he went silent, his breath steadying. They locked eyes in another silence as Harry strained to hear whether anyone was coming. To his luck, they still seemed to be alone. He took a breath and tried to calm himself, the anger still present but muted by his compassion. When he opened his mouth again, the truth spilled out, a sour but honest note. “If you’d just been a better person, we could’ve been friends.”

Like something in her had shattered, Malfoy stared back, a sorrow in her eyes she tried to mask. Harry could tell she felt it, deep down: the acknowledgment that she’d done plenty of wrongs, both to him and to his friends, ones that had kept them apart. For a moment, such a small moment, she let the mask fall. Her snarl turned to a vulnerable frown, and Harry saw a glimpse of Dracaena again. Her lip quivered as she spoke. “I… I would’ve liked that.”

It shook Harry to his core, a rare moment of the girl from that night. But in an instant, the mask slipped back on. The snarl returned, and her words came out with venom. “But you’re an ignorant fool for thinking it could ever be any different.”

Harry began a retort, but realized that his words would accomplish nothing on their own tonight. Malfoy was set in her ways and her family’s ideas, likely trapped into working for Umbridge to keep up her reputation. His shouting, his words… there was no point in hurting her if it wouldn’t accomplish anything. He sighed as they exchanged glances, and her eyes softened with a little regret as she saw him drop his guard. He was done with the shouting, done with the battle.

“Well then, I see that… we’re not going to get anywhere here.” Harry sighed, letting his hands fall to his sides as he looked her in the eyes. “Do what you need to, then. Report me to Umbridge or… whatever you need to.”

Malfoy blinked. Once, then twice. “I… you’re…?” As if she hadn’t expected him to actually relent, guilt crept across her face. He simply nodded, waiting for her next move. Instead of what he expected, she frowned, glancing away as she dug into her pocket. She pulled out the cloak and held it toward him, pain in her eyes and a regretful frown on her lips.

The act tore open that veil once again, the one that parted every now and then in little glimmers of hope to show the heart of the girl beneath all of the venom and the pride. He truly hadn’t expected her to let him go.

To see me suffer, Harry realized, hurts her more than the consequences.

As Harry reached for the cloak, he hesitated. He truly didn’t know whether this would cost her, whether Umbridge would find out that she had let him get away. Maybe he hadn’t shown her enough grace. She shut down his worries by shoving it further toward him, a confirmation to just take it. He pulled it from her grasp lightly and began to put it on, and as the cloak began to cover him, she cleared her throat. “Did… did you really choose to invite me, that night? It wasn’t just some…” Her voice was soft, a little hopeful, longing for something they both knew they couldn’t have. Harry hesitated for a moment, unsure whether to say it, unsure whether to be honest with her and with himself.

“I did. I… I wasn’t even sure of it myself, at the time, but… I…” His eyes met hers for a brief moment, neither of them sure how to go on. “Goodnight, Malfoy.”

He threw the cloak on and began the longest trek back to the common room he would ever take, throwing one last glance at her. She held her gaze in his direction, looking defeated, before her eyes trailed down to her feet.

Harry hadn’t felt like he’d won much of anything. One hope remained in him, however: that, in the midst of it all, she still had that compassion buried beneath.
