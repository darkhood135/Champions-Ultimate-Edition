An Unexpected Rejection

Dracaena Malfoy Memory · The Wizarding World

The whispers echoed in the halls of the Hogwarts Express.

“Potter is in that compartment, right there!”

“Is Potter really on this train?”

“No way! Harry Potter’s here now?!”

Dracaena Malfoy, previously bored to tears, sat up in her seat. If Harry is actually on this train, it certainly beats listening to Crabbe and Goyle chat away.

Is he actually on the train?

She’d always heard about the tale from her father: The Boy Who Lived. The takeaway was always, ultimately, that the Potter family (and especially Harry himself) were a lesser intrusion, not to be taken as anything more than a nuisance. Yet, she couldn’t help but feel a little excited to finally meet the man of legend. She especially was enthusiastic at the idea of making a new friend since, in truth, she always distanced herself from others to keep her social standing. But now? Harry was the talk of the town. Making friends with him would surely be easy and beneficial. But, most importantly to Malfoy, it would be a welcome change.

Hopefully, she thought, this would please father.

She stepped outside of her own compartment, tired but curious. Her lackeys, upon noticing her departure, immediately followed her, hopping out of their seats with the same energy.

“What’s wrong, Malfoy?” Crabbe asked, cracking his neck as Malfoy accelerated down the hall. Dracaena ignored him, focusing entirely on her task at hand. Upon reaching one student, who wasn’t in a compartment, Malfoy tapped him on the shoulder.

“Excuse me, mind telling me which compartment Potter is in?” The boy turned around, confused.

“Um… I think that one over there?” Malfoy felt Crabbe and Goyle staring at the boy from behind her. “B-but I’m not entirely sure… please, that’s all I know.” Noting the boy was already nervous, Malfoy nodded, motioning to the other two to accompany her, as she proceeded to the compartment in question. Her swift movements were unimpeded as the others moved away without question, the halls as her red carpet.

As she finally reached the compartment in question, she looked at the door and took a moment to fix her hair. She questioned Goyle in a whisper. “Do I look presentable?” The last thing she’d want is to leave a bad impression on Harry Potter of all people. Goyle nodded his head quickly, a toothy grin on his face. “Of course you do! You always do.” He responded, a little too loudly. Malfoy grimaced at him, putting a finger to her mouth to shut him up, as she prepared one last breath before opening the door.

Useless fools. They only tell me what I want to hear.

The door opened dramatically, as Malfoy walked in for her grand entrance. There sat two boys, a ginger and a black-haired one, who glanced up at her in confusion. Immediately, Malfoy recognized the black-haired boy from the papers. This was him, and he looked just as cool in person.

“Is it true? You’re Harry Potter?” She exclaimed, an unintended amount of excitement rolling off of her tongue. “Everyone’s been saying that Harry Potter is in this compartment.”

The Potter boy nodded, pressing his glasses to his face. “Yes, it is.” Potter glanced between Crabbe and Goyle, looking uncomfortable. “So, who’s…?”

She’d forgotten the other two were with her. How bothersome. “Oh, right… This is Crabbe and this is Goyle.” She uttered, carelessly motioning to both of them. “And, most importantly, I’m Malfoy. Dracaena Malfoy.” She tossed her hair aside in a sweeping motion, putting her hands on her hips as she stood with an unbelievable amount of pride. She’d practiced this plenty of times, left many awestruck. She was the beautiful Malfoy daughter, after all.

Almost immediately, the ginger began concealing a laugh.

Frustrated and a little flustered, Dracaena placed her attention onto the ginger for but a moment. “Is my name funny to you?” Her face must’ve shown her frustration as the ginger waved his hand to de-escalate the situation.

“Sorry mate, but that motion was…” The boy began laughing completely now. Looking over at Harry, Dracaena noticed he too had a slight, though concealed, smirk.

No, wait, this isn’t how this was supposed to go.

Dracaena began plummeting internally, unsure how to tie this back into her planned proposal. Suddenly, and thankfully, Crabbe put his hand on Malfoy’s shoulder, pointing at the ginger boy.

“Ah, Malfoy… this one’s a Weasley.” Crabbe snorted as though the boy were mere filth. “Those blokes are the red-headed, broke peasants… none of which are worth the effort. No wonder he can’t recognize your elegance.” Weasley’s eyes quickly filled with rage as Crabbe and Goyle began laughing, their laughs sounding more like barks of dying dogs. Dracaena forced out her own laugh. After all, he mocked her first. “Ah, I see… well, that doesn’t mean much coming from you, does it Weasley?” Dracaena uttered, reducing Weasley in her eyes and hopefully in Potter’s. She glanced back at Harry, her initial proposal now clear in her mind.

“You’ll soon find out who’s worth your time, Potter. You don’t want to go making friends with the wrong sort. But don’t worry, I’ll graciously be your guide to this place, and maybe even be your friend. How’s that sound?” She held out her hand with complete confidence, waiting for Harry to take it. Harry simply met her eyes, trailed to her hand, then back to her face.

“I think I can figure it out on my own, thanks.”

What?

Dracaena couldn’t move. Within a moment, she felt out of place.

How didn’t this work? This works with… everyone else.

She rushed to cover her own embarrassment, spewing out whatever words came to her head. This was all the fault of that damn Weasley. “Unbelievable. I offer you a grand opportunity and you can’t even take it… all for the sake of a Weasley. You hang around people like him and you’ll never make it in this school.”

Weasley, clearly infuriated now, stood to his feet. “Say anything more about my family.” He said, his cheeks now as red as his hair. Dracaena, a little intimidated and a little guilty, opened her mouth to speak when Crabbe darted in front of her.

“You’re going to fight us now?” Crabbe ecstatically boomed as Weasley recoiled slightly. Dracaena, worried about the commotion, tugged on Crabbe’s robe. He ignored Malfoy, determined, like a snake going in for the kill. Unexpectedly, Potter stood; His glasses shone as the light reflected off of them, and he stood his ground.

“Unless you get out now.” Potter uttered, his bravery radiant in comparison to most who stood before Crabbe. Dracaena couldn’t help feeling, despite the rejection, a little impressed. Before Dracaena could try to get them to back off again, Goyle flung himself forward as well.

“But we don’t feel like leaving yet, do we? We already ate all of our food back at our compartment, and it looks like you still have a few. Those will be going to her, thanks.” Before Dracaena could even process his actions, Goyle lunged for the chocolate, attempting to grab hold, before suddenly letting out a piercing screech. On his finger was a small rat, biting down intensely to the point it drew blood. Dracaena took a step back, knowing now she had no control of the situation. As Goyle flung the rat off of his finger, he flew out of the compartment, clutching his hand as he darted away. Crabbe quickly followed, as the two fools left their so-called leader behind.

Now, it was just her, Weasley and Potter. Weasley, now focused on the disgusting rodent that was apparently his, paid her no mind. Potter, however, glared at her. She tried to pretend it didn’t hurt, that maybe she could say or do something else, but her eyes began to water again. “This… this isn’t over, Potter! No one embarrasses a Malfoy and gets away with it!” Before she wept any tears in front of him, she bolted out of the room in the same direction.

Those people, who previously moved for her effortlessly, now let out quiet laughs as she raced down the train in tears.

Maybe her father was right. Maybe, just maybe, Potter was a threat.
