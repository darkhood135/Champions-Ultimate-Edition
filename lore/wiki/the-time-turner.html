A Time-Turner is a small hourglass hung on a long gold chain. Its name promises more than it does. A Time-Turner sends no one back in time. Turned, it does nothing at all, unless a wrong is being done close by. Then time runs backward around that one wrong alone: a binding unwinds, a lock undoes itself, a wound closes, while the rest of the world, its bearer included, carries on as before. The bearer cannot choose what it undoes. It chooses for itself, and it seldom chooses to act., “What is needed, and what is meant”

Every Time-Turner ever found came from , and the Ministry of Magic kept them for centuries without understanding them. In 1993 , one of the few who did, arranged for to carry one. It never moved for her timetable. It answered twice in a single night in June 1994, freeing the Hippogriff Buckbeak from the executioner’s axe and from the Dementor’s Kiss., “The Time-Turner” Two years later every Time-Turner in the Ministry was destroyed, and none is known to have survived., “The Department of Mysteries”

Description

A Time-Turner is small enough to wear under a robe: a tiny, sparkling hourglass hung on a very long and very fine gold chain. It is turned by hand like any hourglass. Nothing about it looks dangerous, and nothing marks it out from a trinket until the sand runs on its own.

Nature

How it works

A Time-Turner reverses time locally. When it answers, the moments of a single wrong run backward around its victim and nothing else: whatever was done to them during that wrong comes undone, as though it had never been done. The world around it does not rewind. No one is carried anywhere, no one meets an earlier self, and the person who turned the hourglass sees the whole thing happen in front of them, in the present.

Its reach is short. It answers only to a wrong being done close by, so a Time-Turner turned in one room does nothing for an injustice in the next.

A mind of its own

Turning the hourglass is only an invitation. The Time-Turner decides for itself whether to answer, and if it does, what to undo and how far. It belongs to the ancient magic that answers to need rather than command: it will move only for an injustice still in progress, and no order, licence or charm can make it turn for anything else. Its bearer may hope for one outcome and see another. On the night Buckbeak was to die, Hermione Granger turned it hoping to save him somehow. It did not stop the executioner. It untied the knot that held the Hippogriff, and left the rest to him., “The Shrieking Shack”

For the same reason it almost never acts. Hermione wore one at every lesson for most of a school year, and the sand never once ran., “The Firebolt”

Limits

A Time-Turner can undo what is still being done: it can loosen bonds, open a cell, or close a wound inflicted in the course of the wrong, scar and all. It cannot touch a wrong that is finished. It cannot bring back the dead, because a life already lost is a wrong already done: nothing brings back the dead, not grief, not a Time-Turner, not all the magic in the world. It cannot send its bearer, or anyone, back in time, and it will not act on a wrong it is too far away to reach.

Understanding and misunderstanding

For most of their history the Time-Turners have been relics whose power was rumoured rather than known. The name suggests a way to turn back the clock, and most of the wizarding world, the Ministry included, assumed that was what they did: that a bearer could go back and live an hour again. The Ministry licensed and lent them on that belief for generations, and the hourglasses sat still in the hands of everyone who tried.

Origin

No one knows who made the Time-Turners. Every one ever found came out of Hogwarts, and no other school, family or ministry has ever turned one up. That points to someone connected with the castle in its earliest centuries. Some have guessed at one of the four founders, whose names are on so much of the castle’s oldest magic, but no record says so.

At some point after its founding, the Ministry of Magic took the school’s Time-Turners into its own keeping and shelved them in a hall of the Department of Mysteries, near a room where the Unspeakables studied time itself.

History

Hermione’s Time-Turner (1993–1994)

Main article:

In Hermione Granger’s third year at Hogwarts, Dumbledore arranged for her to carry a Time-Turner. The Ministry signed it over believing it would let her live each hour twice and take every class the school offered. Dumbledore knew it would do no such thing, and gave it to her anyway. It never once turned. Hermione kept up her impossible timetable by sheer effort, and ran herself ragged through a year in which she was also feuding with over her cat and his rat, and admitted to no one how close she was to breaking.

The night of Buckbeak and Sirius (1994)

Main article:

On the evening of Buckbeak’s execution, Harry, Hermione and Ron left Hagrid’s hut as the Ministry’s party came down the lawn, and from the pumpkin patch they saw the executioner raise his axe. Hermione, with nothing else left to try, turned the Time-Turner, and for the first time all year it answered. Around Buckbeak alone the moment ran backward: the knot that tethered him unwound itself, the axe bit into the fence, and the Hippogriff was gone into the Forest.

The same night the truth came out in the Shrieking Shack: Sirius Black was innocent, and had betrayed the Potters. Pettigrew escaped. At the lake, when the Dementors came, Harry cast the stag himself, but Sirius was taken anyway, and the Ministry ordered the Kiss without a hearing., “The Lake”

Harry and Hermione told Dumbledore the truth. He gave them nothing anyone could blame him for: only where Sirius was held, and that what they needed was more time. They worked out the rest. The Time-Turner had answered at the paddock because a wrong was being done in front of it, and a man condemned for a crime he had not committed was another. They found Buckbeak at the edge of the Forest and flew him up to the tower window where Sirius waited, and Hermione turned the hourglass. It answered a second time. The bars and the bonds ran backward for Sirius alone, and he climbed out onto Buckbeak’s back and flew away, still a fugitive.

Hermione dropped the extra classes at the end of the year and gave the Time-Turner back to the Ministry.

The destruction of the Time-Turners (1996)

Main article:

In June 1996 Harry and five friends followed a false vision to the Department of Mysteries, where the Death Eaters were waiting. The battle tore through the hall where the Ministry kept its Time-Turners, and every one of them was unmade in a storm of breaking glass and spinning sand. In the same battle Sirius Black fell through the veil, and no Time-Turner could have brought him back.

No Time-Turner is known to have survived. Whether the castle they came from still holds any that no one has found is not known.

Changes from Canon

  • A Time-Turner sends no one back in time and creates no second self. It reverses time locally, for one wrong in progress, and leaves the rest of the world untouched.
  • It has a mind of its own. Turning it is only an invitation; it decides whether to act and what to undo, and it rarely acts at all.
  • It can close wounds but cannot bring back the dead, because a death is a finished wrong.
  • Most of the wizarding world, the Ministry included, misunderstands what Time-Turners do. Dumbledore is one of the few who knows, and he arranges Hermione’s.
  • Buckbeak is saved at the axe, before the Shrieking Shack. Harry casts the stag at the lake himself, in the moment, and Sirius is freed from the tower by a second turning.
  • Every Time-Turner ever found came from Hogwarts; the Ministry only kept them. As in canon, every one in the Ministry is destroyed at the Department of Mysteries.

Trivia

  • The Ministry lent Hermione a Time-Turner to live each hour twice, which no Time-Turner has ever done. Her famous timetable was kept up on effort alone.
  • Had a Time-Turner still existed in 1997, it could have closed the wounds Sectumsempra left on , scars and all. The last of them had been destroyed the year before.
  • The Ministry’s Time-Turners were shelved near a room where a hummingbird hatched, aged and hatched again inside a bell jar of light, endlessly, without ever leaving it.

Behind the scenes

  • The Time-Turner was reworked to close the plot holes canon’s time travel opens, above all why no one used one to undo the war or the deaths in it. It now works more like a localized reversal than a time machine, loosely in the spirit of Marvel’s Time Stone, except that it can never undo a death.
  • The infobox picture is an officially licensed replica by The Noble Collection, which follows the films’ design of rings around a central hourglass. The books describe something simpler: a tiny hourglass on a long, fine chain.
