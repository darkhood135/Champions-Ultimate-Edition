Curly

Orion "Curly" Carling

Defender

The captain of the doomed freighter Tulpar, rebuilt across three years in metal and grief, Curly carries the weight of a crew he could not save — and a guilt no distance has ever let him outrun.

Curly was the kind one, and that was the whole tragedy of him. The captain who saw the best in everybody and worked to keep the peace, he mistook gentleness for goodness and looked away from the one thing that demanded he not — and by the time silence had run its course, his failure to act on what Jimmy had done to Anya had cost every soul aboard. When Jimmy drove the Tulpar into ruin, Curly alone lived, dragged from the wreck by a rescue vessel and pieced back together over three years of grafts and reconstruction, only to be returned to a world that had already decided he was the villain — the lone survivor, blamed for the deaths of friends whose bodies were never found, denied any life of his own. Yet no verdict the world could hand down is crueler than the one he passes on himself. He is a man drowning in a responsibility he shouldered far too late, haunted most of all by Anya — the crewmate he failed, whom he was forced to watch die, whose voice has never once left him — and hollowed out by the bitter, belated hatred of a friend he could no longer pretend not to see. He did not want to survive it. He did anyway, and surviving has been its own long sentence.

What holds Curly together now is as much machine as man. Robotic prosthetics carry a body that by every right should not have lived, and he moves through the world on limbs of steel and servomotor, enduring where flesh alone would have given out — no warrior and no hero, simply a man with an unfathomable, punishing will to keep standing when so much of him longs to stop. His strength was never power; it is the plain and terrible refusal to lie down and let the guilt finish what the crash began. And then comes the impossible. Torn out of his ruined life and flung into a war spanning shattered realities, Curly is met by the one thing he never let himself hope for: Anya — alive, whole, unharmed — the person he failed above all others, given back to him by a broken multiverse. For the first time since the Tulpar, the man whose deferred responsibility destroyed everything he loved is handed the one thing he never dreamed he'd be offered again: the chance to finally take it.

"Am I figured out? Is this all I'll ever be?"

Forgiveness, in Time w/ Anya

There had been something between Curly and Anya once, before it all — an ease, a warmth, a quiet gladness in each other's company that, on a better ship in a better life, might have grown into something more. That is what makes her the heaviest ghost he carries: he did not merely fail a crewmate, he failed someone he cared for, saw her fear and did nothing, and was forced to watch her die with all of it left unspoken between them. So when she stands before him alive, whole, and real, it very nearly unmakes him. He can barely meet her eyes. That old warmth rises in him alongside a guilt so total he feels he has no right to a single moment of her presence — nothing to offer but an apology that could never be enough. He does not expect her to forgive him; he isn't certain he could bear it if she did. But for as long as this impossible second chance lasts, he will spend every moment trying to be worthy of simply standing beside her again.

The Weight of Doing Nothing w/ Spider-Man

Curly and Spider-Man carry the same wound. Both learned, in the cruelest way possible, that doing nothing when you could have acted is its own kind of guilt — that someone you love can die because you looked away or hesitated a moment too long. Where Peter forged that lesson into a creed and a lifetime of showing up, Curly let it very nearly drown him; and in the younger hero he finds the rarest thing of all — someone who understands the exact shape of his failure without needing a word of it explained, and who proves, simply by standing there, that such a weight can be carried toward something good rather than merely crushing the one who bears it. Peter offers him no absolution. He offers something Curly needs far more: the quiet proof that a man can fail someone and still spend the rest of his life becoming worthy of a second chance.

Rebuilt Against Their Will w/ Cyborg

No one alive understands what it is to live in a body you never chose quite the way Victor Stone does. Both men were dragged from a catastrophe and remade in steel and circuitry — kept breathing by machinery that saved them and stole something from them in the same instant, each a survivor where others were not so lucky. Curly carries the guilt of that survival like a stone, and in Cyborg he finds someone who has walked further down the very same road: a man who knows the alienation of a reflection that no longer looks like you, the grief of the life the machine replaced, and — hardest of all — the slow, stubborn work of proving the person inside the metal is still worth something. They don't need many words for it. The understanding between two men rebuilt against their will runs deeper than speech.
