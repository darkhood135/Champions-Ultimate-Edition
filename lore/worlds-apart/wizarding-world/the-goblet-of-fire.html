The Goblet of Fire is the fourth story arc of in . It covers ’s fourth year at , when he is bound against his will to compete in the Triwizard Tournament. It brings the Yule Ball and the warmest weeks he and would ever share, and it ends in a graveyard with dead and returned.

Story

The Quidditch World Cup

The summer before Harry Potter’s fourth year ended on an image that would hang over everything after it. He had gone with the Weasleys and Hermione to the Quidditch World Cup, a night of color and noise and ordinary wonder, where Ireland beat Bulgaria and its young Seeker, Viktor Krum. He woke in the dark to screams. Masked wizards were setting the campground alight and tossing a Muggle family through the air for sport. Running through the woods, the three of them met Dracaena Malfoy, alone. She told Hermione, with a sneer that was plainly also a warning, that the masked ones were after Muggle-borns, and that she had better keep her head down. Then she was gone. Above the woods a green skull unspooled across the sky with a serpent for its tongue: the Dark Mark, the banner of Voldemort’s followers, unseen for thirteen years. Among the masked figures who melted away before the Ministry could name them was Lucius Malfoy. By morning he was a respectable man again.

The Ministry needed someone small to blame, and found one: a trembling house-elf named Winky, discovered beneath the Mark with a wand in her hand. Her master, Bartemius Crouch, a rigid, powerful Ministry official, dismissed her on the spot, though she had done nothing and adored him. It was a small injustice on a large night, and it would not have mattered to most of the people who saw it. It mattered to Hermione.

The Tournament

Hogwarts opened that year to spectacle. The ancient Triwizard Tournament had been revived, and two schools, Beauxbatons and Durmstrang, arrived in state to compete, Viktor Krum among the Durmstrang students. The new Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher was the legendary Auror Alastor “Mad-Eye” Moody, scarred and paranoid, who showed his fourth-years the three Unforgivable Curses on a spider. When the Cruciatus Curse made it writhe, Neville went white and silent, and Harry didn’t yet know why.

A single champion would be chosen from each school by the Goblet of Fire, and only students of age could enter; the headmaster drew a line around the cup to make sure of it. On the appointed night the Goblet named Fleur Delacour for Beauxbatons and Krum for Durmstrang. For Hogwarts it named Cedric Diggory, and the Hufflepuff table erupted. It was their house’s first champion in living memory. Then the Goblet choked out a fourth name. Harry’s.

He had not entered, and no one who looked at his face believed he had. But a name accepted by the Goblet is a binding magical contract, and it doesn’t ask whether the one it has chosen is willing, or old enough, or the victim of someone else’s scheme. Harry was bound to compete against grown champions in a tournament that had killed people.

Hufflepuff

Hufflepuff took it hardest. Their champion’s glory had been stolen in the same breath it was given, and they blamed the boy who had stolen it. Ron was caught in the middle. The house that had finally made room for him now despised his best friend, and the old ache of standing forever in a famous friend’s shadow did the rest. Ron let himself believe Harry had found a way to enter, because the truth, that it had simply happened to Harry again, was harder to live beside. For weeks the two didn’t speak. The Daily Prophet’s Rita Skeeter, meanwhile, wrote whatever sold, and Harry read about his tragic, tearful inner life in the morning papers.

The Slytherins turned the whole affair into a joke. Badges bloomed across half the castle: POTTER STINKS. The girl behind them needed a quick, clever hand to make so many, and so turned, for the first time, to a Slytherin she had never once bothered to notice. Dracaena wanted something done, and Ada Khumalo could do it, and that was the whole of why she spoke to her. It should have ended there. It didn’t. Ada was unbothered, unimpressed and quietly kind, and she saw through Dracaena’s performance faster than anyone ever had. The badges didn’t go unanswered either. Hermione charmed a reply, POTTER RULES, and pinned it on in the middle of the Slytherins’ crowing.

The First Task

The night before the first task, Hagrid showed Harry what it was: dragons, one for each champion to get past. Harry went straight to Cedric and told him. It wasn’t fair otherwise, and Cedric was the one champion who hadn’t been told. In the arena Harry summoned his Firebolt, outflew a Hungarian Horntail, and came through near the top of the judges’ marks. Watching him face a dragon stripped the grievance out of Ron all at once; no one would choose this, the danger was real, and a brother was a brother. The two mended the rift as clumsily and completely as boys do. Cedric repaid the dragons with a hint about the golden egg that held the second task’s clue, and some of Hufflepuff, hearing what Harry had done for their champion, began to thaw.

The Elf Liberation Alliance

Hunting for Winky one afternoon, Hermione found the Hogwarts kitchens, run every day by more than a hundred house-elves who were paid nothing and owned in all but name. Winky was there, drinking herself into a stupor over her lost master. So was Dobby, the only elf in the castle drawing a wage, because Dumbledore had insisted on paying him. Dumbledore had argued against the old arrangement for years and been overruled by habit and the Ministry both. Hermione didn’t accept it. She founded a movement, the Elf Liberation Alliance, and Harry and Ron heard her out and stood with her without a single joke between them.

Ada

By winter, Dracaena and Ada were something Dracaena had never had: real. It had cost her a bad moment to get there. She had worked out that Ada was a half-blood, and had said something small and ugly out of pure reflex. Ada simply shrugged it off, finding the whole idea that it should matter faintly ridiculous, until Dracaena heard herself and was ashamed, and apologized. Dracaena made her swear to keep the friendship secret. Her father could never know she had chosen, of all people, a girl like Ada.

Ada had also watched her friend circle Potter’s name for weeks. She said aloud what Dracaena would not: that she had a crush, a real one, on the Potter boy. She weathered the furious denial that followed, and told her gently to just ask him to the Yule Ball.

The Invitation

Harry had spent weeks working up the nerve to ask Chao Zheng, the Ravenclaw Seeker he could barely speak around. She turned him down kindly and finally; she was already going with Cedric. He was standing in the wreck of that small humiliation when Dracaena found him. She came at him sideways, the way she came at everything: she pitied him, she said, and supposed she’d let him take her if he was so desperate. Harry heard only the blade, told her to leave him alone, and left. He didn’t see that he had thrown her own courage back in her face. She had meant it. She walked away with her chin up and her eyes streaming.

That night neither of them slept. Harry lay awake turning the scene over, and the more he turned it the less it looked like an attack. Across the castle, in the dungeons, Dracaena wept alone: not at Harry, in the end, but at herself, at the armored girl she showed the world every day, and at how surely her father and her name forbade her to be anyone kinder.

The Plan

The making-up, when it came, wore a disguise. Hermione had been watching Dracaena with a scientist’s eye, and she announced that Malfoy plainly had feelings for Harry. An invitation to the ball, she argued, might be the surest way to learn what Lucius was planning. Ron agreed, hesitantly. Harry seized the idea faster than he should have. He already wanted to go to her and apologize, and the plan gave him a reason he could say out loud, to his friends and to himself, that wasn’t “I think I was wrong about her.”

So he went to her. He found her apart from the others at a quiet hour, the way she had once found him on a train, and lost his nerve twice before he asked her to be his partner. Her mask came up first, all reflex and scorn, and then, for a moment she clearly hadn’t planned, it came down, and she said yes. They told no one.

The Yule Ball

The castle found out all at once. When the champions opened the ball with the first dance and Harry Potter walked into the light with Dracaena Malfoy on his arm, the hall went silent, every table alike, Ron and Hermione among the stunned. Then they danced, and for the length of one song every wall she had ever built came down, and Harry saw her, the girl under the performance, for the first time. Whatever the plan had been, it wasn’t the plan anymore, and he knew it even if he wouldn’t say it. Elsewhere in the hall, Neville danced with Ginny, Cedric with Chao, and Hermione arrived on the arm of Viktor Krum, which Ron spent the evening discovering, too late and too sourly, that he had feelings about.

For a few weeks Harry and Dracaena were quietly something. It was the warmest stretch the two of them would ever have. One evening she caught him slipping out of his Invisibility Cloak to visit the Mirror of Erised, which he had found again in the Room of Requirement, the room that gives you what you need. He was there to see his parents. When she looked into it herself, half laughing, she saw only herself and Harry, and no one else at all. She took the Cloak when she left, a small theft, a joke between them.

The Second Task

The second task was set in the black water of the lake, where each champion had an hour to recover the person they would miss most. For Harry it was Ron. Dobby brought him the gillyweed that let him breathe underwater. Cedric came for Chao, Krum for Hermione, and Fleur, driven back, never reached her little sister. Harry, too decent to leave a child behind, brought Gabrielle Delacour up as well as Ron, surfacing last and half drowned. The judges marked him high for it. Ron spent weeks telling the story, and never once mentioned what it meant to him to be the person Harry would miss most.

Overheard

Then the Cloak kept its promise of ruin. Wearing it, unseen, Dracaena overheard Ron and Hermione speaking of the Yule Ball as a plan: the invitation, the idea of using her to get at her father. Every warm thing of the past weeks turned to ash. She pressed her hand over her mouth to keep from being heard and fled, certain she had been played from the first dance. She couldn’t know the plan had stopped being the plan long before. She knew only the words, and they matched exactly what she had always feared: that no one like Harry Potter would ever truly want a Malfoy. She went cold, and kept her distance, and waited for the year to prove her right.

Crouch

Bartemius Crouch had stopped appearing at the tournament, sending word that he was ill. In May he stumbled out of the Forbidden Forest in front of Harry and Krum, raving, begging to warn Dumbledore of something, and vanished again before Dumbledore arrived.

In Dumbledore’s office Harry fell into a Pensieve, a basin of stored memories, and watched the trials from the end of the first war. Crouch had run them as head of Magical Law Enforcement, and run them hard. He had sent Sirius Black to Azkaban without a trial at all. He had sentenced his own son, Barty, to Azkaban with the Lestranges for torturing two Aurors, Frank and Alice Longbottom, into madness. Harry learned then why Neville had gone white at the Cruciatus Curse. His parents had been in St Mungo’s for thirteen years, and didn’t know him.

The Third Task

The third task sent the champions into a hedge maze with the Triwizard Cup at its heart. Harry and Cedric, saving each other on the way, reached it together. Each told the other to take it. In the end they agreed to take it together, for Hogwarts. The Cup had been turned into a Portkey. It seized them both and dropped them in a graveyard far away.

The Graveyard

Cedric was murdered on a word, for being in the way. Harry was bound to a headstone and bled for a potion, and out of a cauldron rose Lord Voldemort, remade and more terrible than any story had told. He was not the half-living wraith of the whispers but a thing of appalling power. The dark around him bent to his will like water finding a drain, and the air itself was heavy with him, as though the world had agreed to obey. He called his followers with the Mark, and they came. Voldemort’s old followers knelt in a circle in the dark, Lucius Malfoy foremost, thirteen years of respectability shrugged off in an instant.

Harry stood a child before a god, and lived only because when their wands met they refused each other. The two cores were brothers, and they locked in a cage of golden light. Out of Voldemort’s wand came echoes of the people it had killed: an old Muggle caretaker, a witch Harry didn’t know, Harry’s own mother and father, and Cedric, who asked him to take his body back to his parents. They shielded Harry for the half second he needed. He seized Cedric’s body and the Cup and was gone.

Moody

He came back to the castle with a dead boy in his arms, and Cedric’s father’s cries carried across the grounds. In the confusion Moody took Harry away to his office, and there the truth came out. Moody had never been Moody. He was Barty Crouch’s son, escaped from Azkaban, wearing the real Moody’s face on a potion taken hour by hour all year. He had put Harry’s name in the Goblet, steered him through every task, and turned the Cup into a Portkey. Dumbledore, McGonagall and Snape broke in on him. Under a draught that strips the truth out of a person, he confessed: he had murdered his own father in the forest, and the real Moody had spent the year locked in a trunk.

The Ministry chose that moment to prove what it was for. Cornelius Fudge arrived with a Dementor at his side, and before Crouch could repeat a word of his confession to anyone who mattered, the Dementor kissed him. The only witness was left an empty shell. Faced with Harry’s account and Dumbledore’s, Fudge decided that believing Voldemort had returned would cost him too much, and refused to believe it. Dumbledore sent his allies out that night to gather the old resistance. The war had begun, and the government charged with fighting it had chosen to pretend it hadn’t.

The Line

For Dracaena the Dark Lord’s return was not news from far away. It was her father kneeling in that circle. It was her house and her name and her whole future, called back to the side of the thing in the graveyard. The hurt of the overheard plan and the terror of the war arrived together, and she came to Harry carrying both, with his Cloak folded over her arm. She pushed it into his hands, and he understood, too late, how she had heard. Then she struck him across the face. It was two blows at once: how dare you use me, and stay away from me, from all of this, before it kills you. The worst of her fury was turned inward, because she knew her family, and knew who he was, and could not truly fault him for trying to stop her father. She gave him her warning and walked away, and the few warm weeks closed behind her like water. They were on opposite sides of a war now, and both of them knew it.

The Leaving Feast

The Great Hall was hung in black for the leaving feast. Dumbledore told the whole school the truth the Ministry wouldn’t: that Cedric Diggory had been murdered by Lord Voldemort. He asked them to remember a boy who had been good and kind and brave, and who had died because he stood in the way. The Hufflepuff table rose first. Ron stood with his house, and for once there was no question in his mind where he belonged, or who Cedric had been to all of them. Chao Zheng couldn’t stand at all.

Harry gave his thousand-Galleon prize to Fred and George to start their joke shop, because the world was going to need a laugh, and went home that summer changed in a way the earlier years had only rehearsed. He had watched a friend die for nothing, seen the enemy whole, and heard the adults in power deny it to his face. He had also lost, to a war and a name and her own fear, the first person outside his small circle he had let all the way in. Being right would not make anyone believe him. And the lines of the coming fight ran straight through the people he had only just begun to love.

Changes from Canon

  • With in , Cedric is his house’s champion. Ron’s falling-out with Harry comes from being torn between his house and his friend as well as from jealousy, and at the leaving feast he stands with Hufflepuff for Cedric.
  • Harry’s fairness toward Cedric over the dragons begins Hufflepuff’s thaw toward him.
  • ’s POTTER STINKS badges bring her to , an original character and her first true friend. Hermione answers with POTTER RULES.
  • The Yule Ball is overhauled. Dracaena’s sideways invitation is refused, then Harry asks her himself, and they open the ball together. The plan to use the invitation is Hermione’s; Ron only hesitantly agrees.
  • , replacing Cho Chang, is Harry’s crush for this year only.
  • The Mirror of Erised turns up in the Room of Requirement, where Dracaena sees only herself and Harry in it.
  • Hermione’s movement is the Elf Liberation Alliance, taken seriously, and Harry and Ron back it. Dumbledore opposes the unpaid labor in the kitchens and pays Dobby a wage.
  • At the World Cup, Dracaena’s sneer at Hermione is also a warning.
  • Voldemort returns far more powerful than in canon, bending the dark around him to his will.
  • Dracaena returns the Invisibility Cloak when she slaps Harry, which tells him how she overheard the plan.
  • Crouch Sr. sent Sirius Black to Azkaban without a trial, and Fudge’s Dementor destroys the only witness to Voldemort’s return. Both are part of the Ministry’s record in this continuity.
