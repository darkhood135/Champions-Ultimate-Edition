Ronald Bilius Weasley (born 1 March 1980) is a pure-blood wizard of , the sixth son of Arthur and Molly Weasley, and the first Weasley in living memory to be sorted anywhere but . The Sorting Hat put him in over his own protest. On his first night crossed the Great Hall to sit beside him, and the two became brothers in all but blood. With they made a friendship that spanned three houses, and later, with , all four.

Ron spent his first years wishing for scarlet and the rest of them learning what yellow meant. He stood by Harry when his own house turned on him, became Hufflepuff’s prefect and its Keeper, and won the house its Quidditch Cup to a Slytherin song it turned into its own chant. In the war he left Harry once, when the locket found his oldest wound, and came back. He destroyed two of Voldemort’s Horcruxes: Slytherin’s locket, and Helga Hufflepuff’s own cup, alone in the Chamber of Secrets. In the final duel he led the defense at the Dark Lord’s ward, so that no Death Eater crossed it. Afterward he forgave Dracaena sooner than anyone expected, and his testimony turned the court at her trial.

Ron is an ordinary young man in body: tall, with a Keeper’s reflexes and nothing beyond them. Peak-human fighters like Batman or Captain America would put him on the floor before his wand came up, and anything at Spider-Man’s speed could close on him between one spell and the next. What makes him dangerous is the board, not the blow. He is the chess player who saw his own capture as the winning move at eleven, and the young man who looked at the Dark Lord’s ward, saw where the elite Death Eaters would try to break through, and threw a house’s worth of students into that gap so that none of them did. In a crossover battle he is the one reading the field for everyone else. That makes him a natural Booster, but a human-scale commander: Batman out-thinks him, Iron Man out-guns him, and against city-level threats and beyond (the Hulk, Superman, any Super Saiyan) he has nothing but nerve, a plan and the people he can rally.

Within his own world his Combat sits just under ’s. He is an expert battle-wizard, level with and , and in the Battle of Hogwarts he killed a seasoned Death Eater on the stairs. His Intellect sits above Harry’s and below both girls’: his mind is tactical rather than scholarly, quickest when a fight is already moving. His raw magical output matches the other three. His versatility is narrower than theirs: he has no Parseltongue (he can only imitate it), no ancient protection and no catalogue of advanced charms. His corporeal Patronus is his one rule-bending ability, a shield of pure feeling that drives off Dementors. At 22 he ranks last of the four on paper, but none of the others did as much to decide where a fight would be won.

History

Worlds Apart

Family background

The Weasleys are one of Britain’s oldest pure-blood families, and among the poorest. Generations of them had married whomever they loved, Muggle-born or not, and the families who still counted bloodlines called them blood traitors for it. Arthur Weasley worked at the Ministry of Magic in the Misuse of Muggle Artefacts Office, a post with little pay and less prestige, which he loved for the plugs and batteries and the ingenuity of people who had to manage without magic. Molly Weasley, born a Prewett, had lost both her brothers, Gideon and Fabian, to Death Eaters in the first war. She raised seven children at the Burrow, a crooked, many-storeyed house held together by magic in the countryside near Ottery St Catchpole.

Every Weasley in living memory had been sorted into Gryffindor. By the time Ron was old enough to notice, his brothers had set a standard he could not see past. Bill was Head Boy and went on to break curses for Gringotts in Egypt. Charlie captained the Gryffindor Quidditch team and went to Romania to work with dragons. Percy was a prefect on his way to being Head Boy. The twins, Fred and George, were already legends of a different kind.

Early life (1980–1991)

Ron was born on 1 March 1980, the sixth son and the last before his sister, Ginny. Nothing in the Burrow was ever new by the time it reached him. He had Bill’s old robes, Charlie’s old wand and Percy’s old rat, a fat grey animal called Scabbers who slept most of the day and had been in the family for years. His fear of spiders dated from the age of three, when Fred turned his teddy bear into a large and hairy one after Ron broke his toy broomstick. He grew up on Quidditch, wizard’s chess and the Chudley Cannons, the worst team in the league, who were entirely his.

He went to school in 1991 with second-hand everything and a quiet certainty that he would never be anything but the youngest Weasley boy, following five brothers into the same house.

First year (1991–1992)

Main article:

On the Hogwarts Express he shared a compartment with Harry Potter, who had never heard of Quidditch and had no idea what a famous boy was supposed to do with a poor one. They split a mountain of sweets from the trolley and were friends before the train left London. Then Dracaena Malfoy came to the door with Crabbe and Goyle, and Ron could not keep a straight face at the way she tossed her hair. Crabbe called his family red-headed, broke peasants; Dracaena told Harry he would never make it at Hogwarts if he kept company like Ron. Ron stood up shaking (), and when Goyle reached for their Chocolate Frogs, Scabbers bit him to the bone and the three of them fled.

At the Sorting he was sure. He had told Harry so in the line, and that if anyone was destined for Gryffindor it was the Boy Who Lived. He laughed at Dracaena arguing with the Hat and was relieved when Hermione Granger went to Ravenclaw. Then the Hat sat on his own head for a moment and called Hufflepuff. Ron asked it, in front of the whole hall, whether it was sure, and what about Gryffindor. It refused him, and sent him to the yellow-and-black table white and close to tears. Fred and George were the first on their feet to cheer for him anyway. When the feast began, Harry got up from the Gryffindor table, crossed the silent hall and sat down beside him. Ron called it one of the nicest things anyone had ever done for him ().

It eased the evening, but it couldn’t fix what hurt. That night in the Hufflepuff dormitory he broke down where no one could see him: his brothers’ table across the hall, his mother’s letters still to come, and his name already being whispered as the Weasley who wasn’t (). It was the twins who found him. They told him he would be the greatest Hufflepuff who ever lived, and would honor the family name more there than he ever could have in Gryffindor. He did not believe them yet, but he went back to his bed a little more determined ().

Hogwarts years were small, and most lessons gathered all four houses, so Ron sat beside Harry whatever their tables. At the first flying lesson he stood next to him (). He was Harry’s second at the midnight duel Dracaena never meant to attend, and he ran with him, Hermione and Neville into the forbidden third-floor corridor, where a three-headed dog guarded a trapdoor.

He was not kind to Hermione at first. After a Charms lesson on Halloween he mocked her within her hearing, and she spent the afternoon crying in a girls’ bathroom. When a troll broke into the castle that evening, Ron and Harry went to warn her and found it already there. Ron cast his first real spell, levitating the troll’s own club and dropping it on its head, and Hermione took the blame from McGonagall to cover them (). That night Ron apologized to her, and Harry apologized for never having stopped him. After that the three of them were friends: a Gryffindor, a Hufflepuff and a Ravenclaw ().

He stayed at the castle for Christmas. Brought to see the Mirror of Erised, he found himself in Gryffindor scarlet, holding the House Cup and standing at last above all his brothers. When Hagrid hatched a dragon in his hut, Ron’s brother Charlie agreed to take it, and Ron was bitten helping to crate it, which left him in the hospital wing the night the others were caught.

Beneath the trapdoor, the third of the teachers’ trials was a giant chessboard, and Ron played it. He read the game faster than anyone in the room. When he saw that the only way through was to let himself be taken, he sent Harry and Hermione on and stood still while the white queen struck him down. At the leaving feast Dumbledore gave his points to four students, and Ron was the first: fifty to Hufflepuff, for the best game of chess Hogwarts had ever seen and for loyalty unmatched. His new house mobbed him (). Slytherin kept the cup it had earned. On the platform at Hogsmeade, Crabbe and Goyle held him and Hermione off the train long enough for Dracaena to find Harry alone, and when Ron burst in he found her there. Harry told them afterward that she had seemed to want to be friends. Ron laughed it off: the bully of the school only wanted another follower ().

Second year (1992–1993)

Main article:

When Harry stopped answering letters that summer, Ron and the twins flew their father’s enchanted car to Privet Drive in the dead of night, tore the bars off his window and brought him to the Burrow. It was the first home Harry had ever wanted to stay in. Nobody at the Burrow minded the yellow-and-black scarf on Ron’s bedpost half as much as Ron did. When Harry’s Floo powder dropped him into Knockturn Alley, Ron was among the Weasleys who found him again outside Gringotts, worried sick about where he had gone (). At Flourish and Blotts Dracaena needled him in front of her father until the rest of the Weasleys closed ranks around him ().

On the first of September the barrier sealed itself in front of Harry and Ron. They chased the train in the flying car and crashed it into the Whomping Willow, and Ron’s wand snapped in the wreck. The next morning a Howler from his mother screamed his disgrace across the Great Hall, and the Hufflepuff table, which had barely got used to a Weasley in its ranks, heard every word. That same night Ginny had been sorted into Gryffindor, the house that should have been his.

When Dracaena called Hermione a Mudblood in front of the Quidditch teams, Ron tried to curse her for it (). The spell rebounded off his broken wand and left him heaving up slugs into a basin in Hagrid’s hut (). A few days later Dumbledore called him to his office. They talked for a while, about the Howler and about being the first Weasley outside Gryffindor, and then the headmaster mended his wand (). It never failed him again.

When Justin Finch-Fletchley, a Hufflepuff, was found petrified and Harry was found standing over him, Hufflepuff turned on Harry, and Ernie Macmillan said in front of the whole house that Potter was the heir of Slytherin. Ron told his housemates they were wrong and kept telling them, at a table where he was already the odd one out (). At Christmas he drank Hermione’s Polyjuice Potion and walked into the Slytherin common room as Crabbe, with Harry as Goyle, to question Dracaena. They came away sure she was no one’s mastermind ().

When Hermione was petrified, Ron sat beside her in the hospital wing and told her what she meant to him, because she could not hear it (). When Hagrid was taken to Azkaban, Ron followed Harry and the spiders into the Forbidden Forest, white with terror the whole way, to the lair of the acromantula Aragog. Ginny had been taken into the Chamber. Ron had been at another table, in another house, and had not seen the diary draining her all year. He and Harry dragged Gilderoy Lockhart down the tunnel with them. Lockhart drew his wand to wipe their memories, and Harry was faster. His half-cast charm brought the roof down between the two boys, and Ron spent the next hour on the far side of the rockfall with a disarmed Lockhart, shifting stones to make a way back.

At the end of the year Hufflepuff, which had spent half of it shunning him for standing by Harry, apologized to them both, Ernie Macmillan first ().

Third year (1993–1994)

Main article:

That autumn Hufflepuff had a new captain and Seeker, Cedric Diggory, as decent as he was good, and Ron worshipped him a little. In November Hufflepuff played Gryffindor in a howling storm, and when the Dementors swept onto the pitch and Harry fell fifty feet from his broom, Cedric caught the Snitch without seeing it. Hufflepuff had won its first match in years. Ron spent the evening at Harry’s bedside instead of at the party ().

On Halloween Sirius Black, escaped from Azkaban, had slashed the Gryffindor portrait trying to reach a Weasley boy everyone assumed slept there. In February he found the right door. Ron woke in the Hufflepuff dormitory to see Black standing over his bed with a knife, and his scream drove the man off (). No one could work out why Black kept going after Ron. All year Hermione’s new cat, Crookshanks, had hunted Scabbers across the castle. When Ron found blood on his sheets and ginger hairs on the dormitory floor, he stopped speaking to her entirely.

In Hogsmeade, Dracaena caught him in a lane and tormented him, until a hail of mud from nowhere drove her off in tears. It was Harry under his Cloak, who later went after her alone to apologize (). When Buckbeak’s appeal was lost, Ron found Hermione in tears, let the feud over the rat go and apologized for judging her, and he was the first person she told that the Time-Turner had never worked (). When Hermione struck Dracaena across the face that spring, Ron howled with laughter, and kept the joke going until he saw Harry’s face (). Both his friends turned on Harry in astonishment, and the three of them had their first real argument about her. Ron was the one who closed the distance. He found Harry alone on the castle steps one evening, and in the talking-out that followed he understood that what had stopped Harry laughing was not softness but heart, the kind he might do well to learn from ().

On the evening of Buckbeak’s execution, Scabbers turned up alive in Hagrid’s milk jug. Walking back up the lawn, a great black dog seized Ron and dragged him, and the rat, through the roots of the Whomping Willow into the Shrieking Shack, breaking his leg on the way. There, with his leg useless beneath him, he learned that the dog was Sirius Black, an innocent man, and that the rat he had carried in his pocket for three years was Peter Pettigrew, who had betrayed Harry’s parents to Voldemort and hidden for twelve years as a Weasley family pet. That was why Black had kept coming for him. On the train home a tiny owl arrived with a letter from Sirius, who sent the owl as a gift for Ron, to replace the pet he had lost. Ginny named it Pigwidgeon, and Ron called it Pig ().

Fourth year (1994–1995)

Main article:

He went with Harry and Hermione to the Quidditch World Cup and fled with them through the burning campsite when masked wizards came out of the dark (). At Hogwarts the Goblet of Fire named Cedric Diggory as the school’s champion, the first Hufflepuff champion in living memory, and the Hufflepuff table erupted. Then it named Harry too.

Hufflepuff took it hardest, and Ron was caught in the middle. The house that had finally made room for him now despised his best friend, and the old ache of standing in a famous friend’s shadow did the rest. He let himself believe Harry had found a way to enter, because the truth, that it had simply happened to Harry again, was harder to live beside. For weeks they did not speak. It ended the morning of the first task. Watching Harry face a Hungarian Horntail stripped the grievance out of him all at once: no one would choose this, and a brother was a brother (). When Hermione found the castle’s unpaid kitchen elves and founded the Elf Liberation Alliance, Ron heard her out and stood with her without a single joke ().

The Yule Ball found him in second-hand dress robes and a sour mood, discovering, too late, that he had feelings about Hermione arriving on the arm of Viktor Krum (). When Hermione proposed using a ball invitation to learn what Lucius Malfoy was planning, Ron agreed hesitantly (). Months later, Dracaena overheard the two of them talking about the plan under Harry’s Cloak, and took it as proof that she had been used ().

In the second task Ron was the person Harry would miss most. He was put into an enchanted sleep and woke at the bottom of the lake, where Harry had come for him and, too decent to leave a child behind, for Fleur’s sister too. Afterward Ron told his brothers he had been an idiot ever to leave Harry’s side. Harry was the greatest man he had ever known, and he was honored to be the one Harry had come for, though he never said so to Harry ().

Cedric died in the graveyard at the end of the third task. At the leaving feast Dumbledore asked the school to remember him, and the Hufflepuff table rose first. Ron stood with his house. He had lost the Hufflepuff he had wanted to become, and for once there was no question in his mind where he belonged ().

Fifth year (1995–1996)

Main article:

At Grimmauld Place that summer, his letter made him a Hufflepuff prefect, which surprised Ron most of all. Molly cried over the badge, and bought him a new broom, a Cleansweep Eleven, to celebrate (). For once Fred and George could manage only half a joke. Percy, who had sided with the Ministry over his family, did not come home to see it.

When the Ministry refused to teach real defense, Ron was among the first to push Harry into teaching it in secret, and signed his name in the back room of the Hog’s Head. Dumbledore’s Army met all year in the Room of Requirement, and Ron learned there to fight properly for the first time, the Patronus Charm among it. He made the Hufflepuff team as Keeper. The Slytherins wrote him a song, “Weasley Is Our King”, to sing whenever the Quaffle went past him, and for a while it did. By the last match of the season he was saving everything. Hufflepuff won the Quidditch Cup on his saves, and the house sang the Slytherins’ song back at them with new words. It has been Hufflepuff’s chant ever since.

That December Harry dreamed of a snake striking a man asleep on watch in a Ministry corridor. It was Ron’s father, and Harry’s warning saved his life. Ron spent Christmas at Grimmauld Place with Harry, and at St Mungo’s he was there when they turned a corner and found Neville Longbottom beside the parents who no longer knew him, and kept Neville’s secret afterward (). In the spring he watched Fred and George leave the school in a storm of fireworks and swamp.

When Harry was lured to the Department of Mysteries, Ron flew there with him on a Thestral, beside Hermione, Neville, Ginny and Luna. In the fighting a curse left him dazed and giggling, and in the Brain Room he summoned one of the brains from its tank. Its tentacles of thought wrapped his arms and left scars that never fully faded. He was carried out alive. Sirius was not ().

Sixth year (1996–1997)

Main article:

Ron spent most of his sixth year tangled up with Lavender Brown while Hermione pretended not to mind. He was one of the many who heard Harry’s certainty that Dracaena was up to something and put it down to lovesickness over the Yule Ball.

On his seventeenth birthday, in March, Harry asked Professor Slughorn, who was eager to please him, for something to toast his friend with, and Slughorn opened a bottle of mead he had meant as a gift for Dumbledore. Ron drank first. The mead had been poisoned, and he came within a breath of dying on the floor of Slughorn’s office before Harry forced a bezoar down his throat (). Hermione sat by his hospital bed for days, and the Lavender business ended soon after (). When Harry carried a bleeding Dracaena out of a sixth-floor bathroom that spring, Ron and Hermione were at the front of the crowd, pleading for answers he could not give ().

On the night the Death Eaters came through the Vanishing Cabinet, Ron answered the warning Harry had sent through the Army’s enchanted coins, one of only five who did. He fought them in the corridors beside the Order. Fenrir Greyback savaged his brother Bill. After Dumbledore’s funeral, Harry told his friends he would not be coming back to Hogwarts. Before he could argue, Ron told him that it wouldn’t see Ron either ().

The hunt and the Battle of Hogwarts (1997–1998)

Main article:

For the Seven Potters he drank Polyjuice and became one more Harry, flown out beside Tonks. They reached the Burrow alive. Before Bill’s wedding, the Minister for Magic came in person with Dumbledore’s will: for Ron, a Deluminator, the headmaster’s own device for stealing and returning light, with no explanation. When the Ministry fell in the middle of the dancing, Hermione Disapparated with Harry and Ron, and when two Death Eaters found them in a Muggle café that night, Ron wanted to kill them. Harry would not let him. He went into the Ministry in the body of a maintenance worker, Reg Cattermole, and was splinched in the escape.

Through the autumn they moved from wood to hillside to riverbank, wearing Slytherin’s locket in turns, and it found the weakest place in each of them. In Ron it found the boy who had not made Gryffindor: the sixth Weasley son with nothing his own, frightened for his sister at Hogwarts and sure that Harry and Hermione would be better off without him. One night in the rain he said so, and left.

He regretted it within the hour and could not find them again. For weeks he searched, until one night the Deluminator spoke with Hermione’s voice saying his name, and its light carried him back. He reached the Forest of Dean in time to see Harry go into a frozen pool after the sword of Gryffindor, and the locket tighten round his neck to drown him. Ron went in and pulled him out. Harry opened the locket and gave him the sword. What came out of it spoke in all of Ron’s worst fears: his mother loved others more, the girl he loved wanted someone else, and he was the Weasley who couldn’t even make Gryffindor, too ordinary for his own family and too weak to be anyone’s friend. Ron drove the sword through it. He had chosen his friend and come back, and when Hermione had finished being furious with him, she forgave him too ().

At Easter Harry spoke Voldemort’s name, and Snatchers caught all three of them and dragged them to Malfoy Manor. Ron was thrown into the cellar and listened to Hermione being tortured on the floor above, shouting her name until his voice gave out. His wand was taken. When Wormtail came down to see what the noise was and died by his own silver hand, Ron took the dead man’s wand, and it served him for the rest of the war. Dobby took them out, and died on the sand at Shell Cottage (). Ron went with Harry and Hermione into Gringotts after Hufflepuff’s cup, his own house’s relic (), and out again on the back of a blind dragon.

At Hogwarts, while Harry and Hermione hunted the diadem in Ravenclaw Tower, Ron went down alone into the Chamber of Secrets, opened it with a fair imitation of the Parseltongue he had heard Harry speak, and destroyed Helga Hufflepuff’s cup himself with a basilisk fang (). When the castle chose to fight, the houses took the walls together, and Ron stood at the head of the Hufflepuffs. He went with Harry and Hermione to the Room of Requirement for the diadem and came out of the Fiendfyre alive (). When they were clear, he said the kitchen elves should be warned to get out before they died for wizards too, and Hermione kissed him. Then the two of them went down to the kitchens themselves to warn the elves, and to tell them how Dobby had died: free, and for his friends ().

His brother Fred died laughing as the wall they stood behind came down. In the hour’s truce Ron and Hermione went looking for Harry and found Neville instead, who told them where Harry had gone and why, and that he had let him go. They turned on Neville, and on Harry. Ron made for the doors, and Neville stood in front of them (). They were still standing there when Voldemort’s voice filled the castle to say that Harry Potter was dead. Ron went out into the courtyard knowing that his friend had walked to his death willingly ().

When the battle came roaring back, Goyle’s father died at Ron’s hand on the stairs. It was the same man who, the year before, had left Ada Khumalo for dead in a corridor. When Voldemort raised a ward around his duel with Harry, magic that only the Dark Mark could cross, the last of his elite Death Eaters made for it to finish what their master had not. Ron would not let them. He gathered the Hufflepuffs and anyone else within reach and led them straight at the Death Eaters, so that none of them crossed (). Dolohov fell to Flitwick, and Yaxley went down under George and Lee Jordan and was taken alive. Hermione found Dracaena bleeding on the stones and healed her, then ran to help Ron before he got himself killed. When Voldemort fell, it had taken one of each of the founders’ houses to end him: Harry’s spell, Dracaena’s hand, Hermione’s healing, and Ron’s line at the ward.

Aftermath (1998)

In the Great Hall the Weasleys gathered around Fred, and Ron held his mother (). When Harry found him and Hermione and apologized for walking into the forest without a word, they were too glad to have him back to hold it against him. Before the day was out, Harry brought Dracaena to them. Ron forgave her more quickly than anyone expected. He trusted Harry’s sense of character, and he had seen what she did at the ward ().

Term went on for the few weeks that were left of it. Professor Binns, who had not noticed there had been a battle, set an essay on the goblin rebellion of 1890 all the same, to Ron’s and Dracaena’s loud disgust. The four of them studied together through the long afternoons, starting over warily on all sides (). At Dracaena’s trial before the Wizengamot, Katie Bell testified against her first. Neville, a dozen others, Hermione and Ada spoke for her. Then Ron testified, knowing the mead that had nearly killed him had been hers, and many in the court who had come to see her punished changed their minds (). On the last day he came stomping across the field by the lake to tell Harry and Dracaena the train was leaving, and the four of them took one compartment home ().

The summer after (1998)

Main article:

That July Ron went with Hermione to Australia to find the parents who no longer knew they had a daughter, and was beside her when she gave them back their memories, and when their first feeling was anger at what she had taken (). They were home in time for Harry’s eighteenth birthday. Dracaena planned the surprise party at the house on Privet Drive in secret, and Ron helped in his own messy way, coming away with a new respect for how much she loved his best friend (). When Harry came home that evening, the house was full of his friends, and the Weasleys first among them ().

Worlds Entwined

Not yet written.

Physical appearance

Ron is tall and lanky, with big hands and feet and a long nose, and he grew into his height late and clumsily. He is taller than Harry in every year of school and the tallest of the four friends as an adult. He has the Weasley red hair, a face full of freckles and blue eyes, and colors easily: his ears go red long before the rest of him when he is embarrassed or angry, and his whole face goes the shade of his hair when someone insults his family. His robes were second-hand for most of his school life. In fifth year he wore his prefect badge on them with a pride he tried and failed to hide.

The war left its marks: thin scars on his arms where the brains of the Department of Mysteries wrapped him in their thoughts in 1996.

Personality and traits

Ron is funny, quick-tempered, warm and stubborn, and more insecure than anyone around him realized for a long time. He grew up the sixth son in a house where everything had belonged to someone else first. His brothers were Head Boy, Quidditch captain, prefect and the school’s best pranksters, and every one of them was a Gryffindor. The Sorting took the one thing he had been sure would be his, and the fear it left behind, that he was ordinary and would always come second, runs through every one of his failures. It is why he could believe Harry had entered the Triwizard Tournament and why the Yule Ball turned him sour. It was what the locket reached for in the rain, in the words it chose: the Weasley who couldn’t even make Gryffindor.

He was not a good-hearted boy by default. On the train he laughed at Dracaena, and in the line at the Sorting he was glad to see Hermione go to another house. He mocked Hermione until she cried, and in third year he howled when Dracaena was struck. Much of what he became, he learned from Harry. When Harry would not laugh, Ron was the one who went and found him on the castle steps. Out of that talk came the first time he understood that refusing to kick someone already down is not softness. From then on his loyalty grew into something larger than taking his friend’s side: a willingness to stand for what his friend stood for even when it was inconvenient. It is the same willingness that let him forgive Dracaena faster than Hermione could.

The Hat did not make a mistake. Hufflepuff values the ones who remain when everyone else would abandon someone in trouble, and Ron did that before he ever wore the colors. He kept telling his housemates they were wrong about Harry at a table where he was already an outsider. He spent his house’s first win in years at a hospital bed, and he went into the lake as the person Harry would miss most. His defining failure, walking out in the rain, was the one time that loyalty broke, and he came back. The Mirror showed him in scarlet at eleven. By the end of the war he was standing at the head of the yellow-and-black line, and no longer wanted to be anywhere else.

Under the jokes he is a strategist. He sees a fight the way he sees a chessboard: which pieces matter, which can be spent, where the other side will try to break through. It made him a Keeper who read the Chasers before they shot, and a commander who knew at the ward that the battle would be lost not in the duel but in the gap beside it. He is brave in the way that frightened people are brave. He followed the spiders into the Forest white with terror, and he walked into the Chamber alone. He has a temper, a stomach that rarely stops, and a gift for saying the wrong thing first and the right thing afterward. With the people he loves he is plainly, unembarrassedly devoted: “hon”, by the end, to the girl he used to make cry.

Magical abilities and skills

Strategy and wizard’s chess

Ron is the finest chess player of his generation at Hogwarts. Beneath the trapdoor at eleven he played the teachers’ giant board as a living piece, saw that the only winning line required his own capture, and took it. The game is how he thinks: he reads positions, sacrifices and threats faster than almost anyone in the castle. In the Battle of Hogwarts that habit became leadership, and at Voldemort’s ward he saw where the real danger lay and threw everything he had at it.

Quidditch

He was a nervous Keeper at first, and the Slytherins made sure everyone knew it. Once his nerve settled he became an excellent one, reading the Chasers before they shot, and he won Hufflepuff the Quidditch Cup in his fifth year almost single-handed.

Dueling and battle

Trained in Dumbledore’s Army and tested in every fight of the war, Ron is an expert battle-wizard. He fought Death Eaters at the Department of Mysteries and in the Hogwarts corridors at sixteen. In the final battle he killed Goyle’s father, an experienced Death Eater, on the stairs, and led the defense that kept Voldemort’s elite from the ward.

Patronus Charm

He learned the Patronus Charm in the Room of Requirement. His corporeal Patronus is a Jack Russell terrier, small, quick and loyal, the same loyal, scrappy creature his friends would have chosen for him.

Imitating Parseltongue

Ron is no Parselmouth. In the Battle of Hogwarts, having heard Harry open the Chamber of Secrets, he went down alone and opened it again with a fair imitation of the hissing words, a feat of memory and nerve that let him reach the basilisk’s fangs.

The Deluminator

Dumbledore left him the Deluminator, a device that can steal the light from lamps and give it back. For Ron it did more. When he wanted to return, it carried Hermione’s voice speaking his name, and its light led him back to his friends. Dumbledore had foreseen that he would need it.

Possessions

  • Charlie’s old wand (unicorn hair). A hand-me-down that snapped in the crash into the Whomping Willow in 1992 and backfired on him until Dumbledore mended it. It served him for six years and was taken from him at Malfoy Manor in 1998.
  • Peter Pettigrew’s wand. Taken from the dead Wormtail in the Manor cellar. Ron fought the Battle of Hogwarts with it.
  • The Deluminator. Left to him in Dumbledore’s will; it brought him back to Harry and Hermione.
  • Scabbers. Percy’s old rat, passed down to Ron, who was in truth Peter Pettigrew in Animagus form. Revealed in 1994.
  • Pigwidgeon. A tiny, overexcited owl, sent to him by Sirius Black in 1994 to replace Scabbers.
  • His Hufflepuff prefect badge (1995) and a Cleansweep Eleven, the broom his parents bought to celebrate it, which he flew as Hufflepuff’s Keeper.

Relationships

Family

Arthur and Molly Weasley

His parents love all seven of their children loudly and without reserve, which never quite stopped Ron from feeling like the least of them. Molly’s Howler shamed him in front of his new house, and the same Molly cried over his prefect badge. Arthur nearly died in a Ministry corridor in 1995, saved by Harry’s vision. In the Great Hall after the battle, with Fred laid among the dead, Ron held his mother.

Bill and Charlie

His eldest brothers, Head Boy and Quidditch captain, were the standard Ron grew up measuring himself against. Charlie took Hagrid’s dragon off their hands in Ron’s first year. Greyback’s attack in 1997 scarred Bill for life, and Ron was in the corridors fighting the same Death Eaters that night.

Percy

Prim and ambitious, Percy sided with the Ministry over his family in 1995 and stopped coming home. He came back in the Battle of Hogwarts, apologized to all of them in the middle of a corridor, and was forgiven.

Fred and George

The twins teased him mercilessly. Fred turned his teddy bear into a spider when he was three, and told him the Sorting would hurt. They were also the first in the Great Hall to stand and cheer when the Hat called Hufflepuff. That night they told him he would be the greatest Hufflepuff who ever lived. Fred died in the Battle of Hogwarts, laughing at Percy’s joke as the wall came down.

Ginny Weasley

His only sister and the youngest of them. She went to Gryffindor, the house he had wanted, and because he sat at a different table he never saw the diary draining her through her first year. By the war she was fighting beside him, and the fear for her at Hogwarts under Snape was part of what the locket used against him.

Harry Potter

See also:

His first friend at Hogwarts, and the brother he chose. Harry crossed the Great Hall on their first night to sit beside him, and Ron has measured friendship by that moment ever since. Their bond broke twice: over the Goblet of Fire, when Ron let jealousy tell him Harry had lied, and over the locket, when he walked out into the rain. Both times he came back, and both times Harry took him back without making him beg. Ron is the one who closes the distance first after their quarrels. It was Harry’s example, in the moment he would not laugh at a girl who had been struck, that made Ron a better man. In the end Harry was the person Ron would follow anywhere, and Ron was the person Harry would miss most.

Hermione Granger

Main article:

He made her cry on Halloween of their first year and spent the next seven years making up for it. They fought over cats and rats, over Krum, over Lavender Brown, and over nearly everything else. She sat by his hospital bed for days after the mead. When he came back to the tent, she was furious with him for a long time before she forgave him. They are a Hufflepuff and a Ravenclaw who complete each other’s weaknesses: her learning and his instinct, her caution and his nerve. In the Battle of Hogwarts, when Ron said the kitchen elves should be warned, Hermione kissed him, and they went to warn the elves together. They have been together ever since.

Dracaena Malfoy

From their first meeting on the train, Dracaena was the enemy of everything Ron was: rich where he was poor, proud where he was unsure, and contemptuous of his family to his face. He laughed at her and she tormented him. He assumed the worst of her long after Harry had begun to see more, and in sixth year the mead she sent for Dumbledore nearly killed him. Yet when the war ended he forgave her faster than anyone expected. He trusted Harry’s judgment, and he had seen her turn on the Dark Lord at the ward. At her trial he testified for her knowing exactly what she had cost him, and his testimony did more than any other to change the court’s mind. By the summer after the war, he was helping her plan Harry’s birthday and liking her more than he would admit.

Hufflepuff

His house was slow to accept him and, for a while, he was slow to accept it. Hufflepuff turned on Harry twice, in his second year over Justin Finch-Fletchley and in his fourth over Cedric Diggory. The first time Ron stood against his whole house; the second time he was torn in half by it. Ernie Macmillan led the house’s apology in 1993. Cedric, his house captain, was the Hufflepuff Ron wanted to become, and when the house rose for him at the leaving feast Ron rose with it. By fifth year he was the house’s prefect and the Keeper who won its Cup. In the last battle the Hufflepuffs followed him to the ward.

Neville Longbottom

A Gryffindor and a fellow member of Dumbledore’s Army. Ron kept the secret of Neville’s parents after St Mungo’s. In the hour before the end, Ron turned his grief and rage on Neville for letting Harry walk into the forest, and Neville stood in front of the doors and would not let him follow.

Albus Dumbledore

The headmaster gave Ron his first points for loyalty and mended his broken wand when Ron was at his lowest in second year. He left Ron the Deluminator, as though he had known that one day Ron would need a way back.

Lavender Brown

His girlfriend for most of sixth year, more by drift than design. The relationship ended soon after Hermione’s vigil at his hospital bed.

Crabbe, Goyle and Goyle’s father

Crabbe insulted his family the first time Ron met him, and he and Goyle spent six years at Dracaena’s shoulder. Both died in the Fiendfyre in the Room of Requirement. Goyle’s father, who had left Ada Khumalo for dead the year before, died at Ron’s hand on the stairs in the Battle of Hogwarts.

Etymology

Ronald comes through Scottish Gaelic Raghnall from the Old Norse Rögnvaldr, built from regin (“counsel”, “the gods’ decree”) and valdr (“ruler”): a counselor’s name for a boy whose gift turned out to be advising the fight. His middle name, Bilius, is his uncle’s, and sounds like bilious, “ill-tempered”, which suits his temper as well. Weasley recalls the weasel: small, quick, underestimated and much maligned, like a family the old pure-blood houses looked down on. The badger of his house is the weasel’s larger, steadier cousin.

Changes from Canon

  • He is sorted into Hufflepuff rather than Gryffindor, over his own protest, and his arc becomes learning to own that house’s loyalty. Harry crosses the Great Hall on their first night to sit with him.
  • His Mirror of Erised vision shows him in Gryffindor scarlet with the House Cup, standing above his brothers, rather than as Head Boy and Quidditch captain.
  • Dumbledore mends his broken wand in his second year, so he never gets a new one. Lockhart’s memory charm never backfires; Harry disarms Lockhart instead.
  • Sirius Black breaks into the Hufflepuff dormitories, not Gryffindor Tower, to reach him.
  • Cedric Diggory is his house captain, and he is torn between his house and Harry when the Goblet names them both.
  • He and Harry back Hermione’s Elf Liberation Alliance (canon’s S.P.E.W.) wholeheartedly.
  • He is a Hufflepuff prefect and Hufflepuff’s Keeper, and “Weasley Is Our King” becomes Hufflepuff’s victory chant when he wins the house the Quidditch Cup.
  • He is poisoned by the mead on his own birthday, after Harry asks Slughorn for something to toast him with. Love potions are outlawed, so there are no spiked chocolates and no antidote errand.
  • The locket taunts him as the Weasley who couldn’t even make Gryffindor.
  • He destroys Hufflepuff’s cup alone in the Chamber of Secrets, and leads the Hufflepuffs on the walls.
  • He kills Goyle’s father in the Battle of Hogwarts, and leads the defense at Voldemort’s ward during the final duel.
  • He forgives Dracaena Malfoy, who takes the place of Draco as Harry’s rival, soon after the battle, and testifies for her at her trial.
  • There is no “Nineteen Years Later”: his story continues after the summer of 1998.

Trivia

  • Ron was born on 1 March 1980, three months before Dracaena, five months before Harry, and about five and a half months after Hermione.
  • He is the only one of the four whom the Sorting Hat refused to his face. Harry made no wish, and Dracaena demanded Slytherin and got it.
  • His Champion theme, “King of Hufflepuff”, comes from an AGL LR Jiren track in Dragon Ball Z Dokkan Battle, and is named for the chant Hufflepuff made out of a Slytherin taunt.
  • His Boggart is a giant spider, a fear that dates from Fred’s prank on his teddy bear. In second year he followed the spiders into the Forbidden Forest anyway.
  • For three years he carried the man who betrayed Harry’s parents in his pocket.
  • He destroyed two Horcruxes, Slytherin’s locket and Hufflepuff’s cup: the relic of the house that refused him and the relic of the house that took him in.
  • At eleven the Mirror of Erised showed him in Gryffindor scarlet. At eighteen he led the Hufflepuffs in the last stand of the war.
  • The Death Eater he killed on the stairs, Goyle’s father, was the man who had left Ada Khumalo for dead a year before.
  • Ron’s line at the ward was one of the four parts of the spell that ended Voldemort, one for each founder’s house.
