An Unexpected Acceptance

Dracaena Malfoy Memory · The Wizarding World

As the Hogwarts Express waited at the platform, Dracaena caught a glance of Harry Potter waiting alone, looking around as though searching for his friends. Even amidst the bustling crowd of students (each with their own luggage and relics), his messy hair and unique glasses stood out. Of course, she knew who he was looking for; Weasley and Granger. Those two, always getting in her way. With them in the picture, she could never catch Potter alone for long, their presence always nearby. All she needed was a few moments to try to convince him to be her ally. Even though her father didn’t like him, she figured, given enough time, she could convince him that Potter was a valuable resource.

Though, if she was being honest with herself, she just wanted to be friends with him. Potter was no threat after all; he was just another boy, one with priorities she couldn’t understand. She knew he’d likely reject a flat-out offer (like he did before), so instead she sought to simply… congratulate him on a good game. A good run at the House Cup, an impressive showing. The Malfoy within her was somewhat disgusted, but… maybe it’d be good for her to drop her pride for just a moment. She actually found herself a little happy at the chance to be nice to him. Maybe then he wouldn’t dislike her as much.

Father wouldn’t agree with that. But… he wouldn’t know, either. And… if it worked, then he’d be happy I took an enemy off the table!

Suddenly, the plan was sounding like a win all around. She glanced between Crabbe and Goyle, snapping her fingers.

“Hey, you two. Once Granger and Weasley show up, I need you to hold them off from boarding the train for me.” Crabbe nodded his head. “For how long, Malfoy?” She grabbed her chin, thinking for a moment. “As long as you can, I suppose. But let them board before it leaves, got that? And make sure Potter still boards!” She turned around, eying them both down, confirming they understood. “Yes ma’am.” Goyle responded, mimicking a salute with a crude laugh. Without another word, she turned with style and watched Potter’s every move, waiting to see where he would board. As soon as he took one step onto the train, she darted over to try to reach his position. Behind her, she could hear an irritated Weasley begin bickering.

Potter had, unsurprisingly, sat in the same compartment as the start of the year, closing the door behind him. She prepped herself, running it through in her head one last time before steadying herself. She slowly opened the compartment door, glancing around the cramped yet cozy room as Potter noticed her with a startled alertness. Instantly on edge, he sat up in his seat as Dracaena sat across from him slowly, a flat expression across her face the entire time.

You know the drill, Malfoy.

As Potter opened his mouth to speak, Dracaena put up her hand to stop him.

“Before you say anything,” she started, “I’m not here to take the seats from your half-wit friends.”

“Then… what do you want?” he responded, his tone already agitated and his expression practically begging her to leave. Dracaena ignored it as she continued, averting her eyes as she laid back in the seat of the compartment. “I wanted to say…” She hesitated as she let out the next two words, her pride paining her to even admit it. “Well played.”

“What?”

“You lost, of course. We were destined to win from the start but… all things considered? You weren’t too bad, for a half-blood, Potter.” Dracaena admitted, her eyes remaining focused on the window as she maintained her bored expression. She’d found his actions this year a lot cooler than she’d admit, of course. When she finally looked back at Potter, he was looking at her with a blank expression. “This is about the… House Cup? Really? That’s all you wanted to say?” he spoke, unmoving. Dracaena scowled, frustrated, as she looked at him through his dumb, round glasses.

“Well yes, of course. What else would I be referring to, genius?” she spat, crossing her arms as he kept his blank expression.

Why must he never listen to anything I say?

Potter blinked twice before opening his mouth again. “So what? You just wanted to rub it in my face?” Dracaena audibly groaned, looking up at the ceiling with frustration as she felt her ears begin to go red. “Are you even listening to me, moron? I told you already…” She, against her own judgment, looked at Potter directly. “Well played. You… you did well, is all I’m saying. So… good job, anyway, despite your pathetic loss.”

She’d leaned over and extended her hand before she’d even realized she had done it, something she regretted instantly.

He’s going to reject you again, moron! What are you doing?!

It was too late now, so she kept her hand held out. She knew he’d just reject it, like he’d done before. It’s not like she’d been any nicer to him than before, so… it was a courtesy, nothing more.

Potter stared at her hand for a moment, pondering. It felt like minutes to her as the awkward silence stressed her out. Then, to her shock, Potter took her hand, shaking it lightly as she limply reciprocated. “Alright then… fine. Congratulations, I suppose.” he responded, annoyed but surprisingly genuine. She looked down at their hands and then back up at him with utter shock. Realizing it must’ve been showing on her face as Potter lifted an eyebrow, she retracted her hand swiftly and immediately returned to her casual position. She chuckled as a sly smile crossed her face.

“What a shock, a Gryffindor actually willing to admit their inferiority. Well, I suppose you were always a good sport, Potter.”

“I try to be. Though, it’s pretty tough when you try to rub it in my face all the time.” he argued, looking at the compartment door with quick glances like he couldn’t wait for Weasley or Granger to show their face.

“Why wouldn’t I? You seem to always need reminders on how much better I am.” Dracaena laughed, twirling her hair with her index finger as she eyed down Potter, pretending she didn’t notice how uncomfortable he was. He crossed his arms as he sighed. “I mean… the other houses still did well, all things considered. Gryffindor was pretty upset since—”

“You got that detention? Yeah, I’m pretty surprised myself that they scraped together all those points after you and Longbottom nearly doomed them.” she remarked as she kept her smug glance fired his way. He narrowed his eyes at her. “… Are you serious?”

“What?”

“You act like you weren’t there with us.” Potter said, tilting his head a little as she waved her hand to dismiss whatever he was saying. “Yeah, yeah. Sorry, I must have forgotten, given I so courageously faced the dangers.” she spoke, a blush creeping onto her face as she recalled how scared she had truly been. No one would ever know besides him, Granger and Longbottom; she’d make sure of it.

“Right…” Potter groaned, rubbing his temple as he hunched over on his seat. A small silence settled between them before Potter shook his head and continued. “Anyhow, are we done here, Malfoy?” She gasped lightly, scoffing in offense.

“Wow… in such a rush to discard me, Potter?” She pouted, puffing out her cheeks as Potter simply blinked at her with disinterest. “I mean… yeah? A handshake changes nothing if you’re going to keep being so mean.”

“Well… maybe if you hadn’t been so mean to me, before!”

“You called Ron ‘the wrong sort’!” he exclaimed, putting his hands out as though confused. “Was I supposed to be nice to you after that? He’s my best friend!”

“Well, I…” She thought for a moment, her expression shifting slightly before continuing to pout, glancing away from him yet again. “I… I guess you might be right, Potter. Fine. Still, what kind of fool doesn’t want to be friends with me?” To this, Potter sighed, glancing away as he pondered how he’d respond next. His expression looked like he was heavily weighing something in his mind before he finally spoke after a few moments.

“Well, we still could be if—”

“Really?!” she exclaimed, her eyes widening in surprise and excitement, her voice a little louder than she’d intended. Immediately she slammed her hand onto her mouth.

You idiot! You idiot! You shouldn’t be this excited and you especially shouldn’t be showing it!

Potter nearly recoiled, his eyes locked on her, bewildered. “Malfoy…?” Dracaena sat deadly still for a moment, unsure what to even do or say next. Blurting things out at the wrong moment seemed oddly common around Potter.

“I… um…” Dracaena fumbled her words, fidgeting with her hands as her eyes darted all around the compartment. “I…” She caught Potter tilting his head out of the corner of her eye, like he was analyzing her.

Get me out, get me out, get me—

The compartment door opened.

“Malfoy?!” The Weasley boy sneered. “What are you doing here?” Granger stood behind him, looking equally bothered by her presence. Dracaena, seizing the moment, got her composure and stood up carefully. “Just warning your friend to stay out of my way, Weasley. And that goes for you, too.” She wiped off her robe as she frowned.

Granger, a scowl on her face, stepped in front of Weasley, pointing an accusatory finger at Dracaena. “You’re a nuisance, Malfoy. You sent Crabbe and Goyle after us, didn’t you?”

“Why would I bother sending them after you two fools? I could’ve dealt with you myself.” Dracaena responded, leaning forward as she towered over Granger. “Yeah right! You’re somehow even dumber than your two cronies.” Granger spat, inching closer as she balled her fists.

Dracaena snarled, balling up her own fists, but caught herself as she glanced over at Potter. His eyes were focused on her with a warning, throwing her for a loop, as she turned back to Granger. “Whatever. Enjoy your time with this fool.” She waved her hand as though to disregard him, pushing past both Weasley and Granger. “See you next year, Potter! Maybe next time you won’t disgrace your entire house.” She waved a sarcastic goodbye his way with a smirk as she left the compartment, shutting the door behind her.

When it all caught up with her, she… didn’t know how to feel. Maybe Potter was right, that she was too mean. But Father wanted her to keep her pride, her mannerisms, for the sake of the Malfoy name. She didn’t know if she wanted that, though. Did she? Of course she did, but…?

As she walked down the corridor, she finally found the familiar compartment she shared with Crabbe and Goyle. She let out a deep breath as she prepared herself with a lie to tell them.

I was warning him, that’s all. To stay out of our way.
