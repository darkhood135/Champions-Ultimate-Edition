The Ancient Legacy is the prequel story arc of in , set a century before . It follows , a student who could see the traces of , through the goblin rebellion of 1890 and the race against for the last of ’s repositories.

Beneath that race lies an older story: four professors called , the student they trained, and the curse one of them used to stop her. Duplicity was liked by everyone who knew him and helped anyone who asked. He was also one of the most dangerous wizards of his age, and he left a trail of dead behind him that no one thought to lay at a schoolboy’s feet. The arc ends with what he chose to do with Isidora’s power, and what that year cost him.

The Tale of the Keepers

The Keepers

Centuries before Duplicity’s time, four Hogwarts professors shared a secret. Percival Rackham could see the traces that ancient magic left in the world, a gift so rare that he had found no one else alive who shared it. He confided in three colleagues: Niamh Fitzgerald, the headmistress; Charles Rookwood, a Slytherin who taught Transfiguration and was fascinated by every way magic could be bent; and San Bakar, the professor of Beasts, who had come to Hogwarts from Yemen. Together they studied ancient magic, used it with care, and guarded it from anyone who might abuse it. They called themselves the Keepers.

Once, they used it to end a drought. A hamlet in the Highlands had watched its fields die, and the Keepers came quietly and brought the land back to life. A girl in the hamlet watched them work and saw swirls of light in the air around them. Her father told her she had imagined it.

Isidora

The girl was Isidora Morganach. The rain came too late for her younger brother, who died before the fields recovered, and her father fell into a grief so deep that he stopped speaking. When Isidora came to Hogwarts she recognized Professor Rookwood as one of the strangers from her childhood, and asked him what the swirls had been. Within days she stood before all four Keepers. Rackham saw at once that she could see what he saw, and took her as his apprentice.

She was the most gifted student he ever taught, and the most impatient. Again and again she asked him why ancient magic, which could raise a dead field, could not take away pain. Rackham told her it was no cure for everything, and that some things it should not be asked to do. He never told her why. Isidora took his answer as caution, and caution as a failure of nerve.

What Was Given

Years later, after travels of her own, Isidora went home to the father who still had not spoken. He asked her to take the grief away if she could. She drew it out of him as something real, a dark thread carrying the traces of ancient magic and something else besides, and sealed it in a jar. For the first time in years her father spoke, and he thanked her.

It was a true working of ancient magic: something offered freely and taken with consent. A memory charm could have removed his memory of his son and left the wound behind, so that he hurt without knowing why. Isidora had taken out the feeling itself. The Keepers had watched her do it, and it frightened them.

What Was Taken

Isidora did not stop. During a plague she drew the pain out of a dying man who reminded her of her father, and she never asked whether he wanted it gone. She returned to Hogwarts as the professor of Defence Against the Dark Arts and began taking pain from students who never knew she had done it, in caverns beneath the castle. She stopped letting what she took go. With the help of the goblin craftsman Bragbor she built vast repositories to hold it, and kept secret workshops across the region that the Keepers never found. Each time she drew pain out she felt stronger, and in time she began to breathe some of it in. When she tried her art on Headmistress Fitzgerald without asking, she was stopped only just in time.

The Keepers had reason to be afraid. For years the Dementors had guarded Azkaban, creatures of taken joy that fed by taking more, and in Isidora they saw a witch walking the same road with a wand in her hand. They told her no. They never told her why, and they never studied what she had found. The fear was well founded; the way they dealt with it was not.

The Caverns

The end came when Bakar visited her father and found a man emptied of everything, his grief gone and his joy with it. Isidora had kept coming back, and had kept taking. Bakar ran to warn the others, and the four Keepers confronted her in the caverns beneath Hogwarts. Rackham ordered her to put down her wand. She threw the Keepers off their feet and knocked the headmistress senseless, then dueled her own teacher and slowly began to overpower him. Rookwood came to Rackham’s side. Bakar, kneeling over Fitzgerald, saw that the two of them were losing, raised his wand, and killed Isidora with the Killing Curse.

The Keepers’ Shame

The Keepers never agreed about that night. Rackham had wanted to understand her, and he never forgave Bakar. Others called it the only thing that could have been done. They sealed the caverns, hid the repositories, and built a chamber of maps beneath the castle, with trials that only someone who could see the traces would ever find. They hunted down and destroyed the enchanted portraits Isidora had painted of herself, so that no likeness of her could reach whoever came next. Then they left portraits of their own to wait for that person, and went on arguing in their frames for centuries.

Hiding Isidora’s power also hid what they had done to her. And there was one portrait of her that they never found.

Story

The Early Years

Duplicity Magnaloid came to Hogwarts in 1886 and was sorted into Ravenclaw. Within a year most of the castle knew him as the polite, endlessly helpful boy who would carry a first-year’s books, look for a lost Kneazle, or spend an afternoon on someone else’s chore for no reason he would give. He saw things in the old stones of the castle, faint lights where no lights should be, and his housemates put it down to imagination. Only one teacher took it seriously. Eleazar Fig, who taught Magical Theory, noticed that the boy kept looking at the same places and asked him what he saw. Fig wrote about him to his wife, Miriam, who was travelling the world in search of a magic that seemed to be disappearing.

Over four years Duplicity collected friends in every house. In Slytherin he had Sebastian Sallow, brilliant, restless and drawn to every door marked forbidden, and Sebastian’s closest friend Ominis Gaunt, the blind son of an old family steeped in the Dark Arts, who had refused everything his family prized. Natsai Onai, called Natty, came to Gryffindor from the African school of Uagadou before their fourth year, when her mother took up a post teaching Divination. Poppy Sweeting, a Hufflepuff who trusted creatures more than people, befriended him after he helped her stop classmates tormenting a Kneazle. Sebastian found in him the one friend willing to follow wherever his curiosity led, and slowly drew him toward the Dark Arts.

The Rebellion

Outside the castle, the goblins had been angry for generations. The Code of Wand Use forbade any non-human from carrying a wand, and wizards enforced it with whatever force they liked. When Ranrok was young, a wizard beat him nearly to death for holding one. Ranrok grew up to lead the uprising his people had long been waiting for, and his followers, Ranrok’s Loyalists, began raiding wizarding villages in the 1880s. Ranrok believed Isidora’s power was his by right, because his ancestor Bragbor had built her repositories. To find them he made an uneasy alliance with Victor Rookwood, a dark wizard descended from the Keeper Charles Rookwood, whose gang of Ashwinders ran smuggling and extortion across the Highlands. Each despised the other.

The grievance was real. Ranrok’s answer to it was to seize a power built out of other people’s pain, and he poured it into his own Loyalists. Not every goblin followed him. His younger brother Lodgok marched with the rebellion at first and came to believe that it was turning into the thing it was fighting. The Ministry of Magic, meanwhile, treated the raids as an inconvenience. When the Loyalists and Rookwood’s men attacked Feldcroft, Victor Rookwood cursed a girl who ran out to help the wounded: Sebastian’s twin sister, Anne. She was left in constant pain and could not return to school.

Miriam Fig had been warning the Ministry about Ranrok for months, through her old friend George Osric. Not long before Duplicity’s fifth year, searching for the last of Isidora’s repositories, she was killed far from home. Fig blamed himself for not being with her.

The Road North

Miriam’s last letter had named one person who might be able to finish her work: the boy Eleazar had written to her about. A few days before Duplicity’s fifth year began, Fig came to collect him, with Osric and a small container Miriam had sent from abroad. In the carriage it glowed, and only Duplicity could see it. Then a dragon fell on them out of the sky. Osric was lost, and Fig saved the two of them by summoning a Portkey without a wand. It carried them to ruins in the Highlands, and the ruins led into a forgotten vault of Gringotts, where a Pensieve showed Duplicity traces of the past that Fig could not see. Ranrok and his Loyalists broke in after them. A guardian of living memory rose out of the Pensieve to hold the goblins back, and a doorway opened onto Hogwarts.

Duplicity arrived at the start of term like everyone else, with a story he could tell no one, and a teacher who now needed him more than any teacher should need a student.

The Map Chamber

Through the autumn Fig and Duplicity followed Miriam’s trail around the castle and beyond it: a locket, a map, and books in the Restricted Section of the library, which Sebastian helped him break into. It ended in a chamber hidden deep beneath Hogwarts, where a great map of the land lay across the floor and four empty frames hung on the walls. The Keepers’ portraits returned to them one by one. Rackham told Duplicity that four trials waited for him, one set by each Keeper, and that he would have to face them alone. Each trial would end in a memory, and the memories would tell him the truth.

The first trial was Rackham’s, and the second was Rookwood’s. They showed him a girl in a dying hamlet, and the teacher who took her on.

Through Green Glass

That same autumn Sebastian took Duplicity into the Undercroft, a forgotten room beneath the castle, and showed him what he had been reading in secret. Over the following months Sebastian taught him the Cruciatus Curse, the Imperius Curse and the Killing Curse. He meant them as tools for curing Anne. Ominis would have no part in it, and told them both so.

Duplicity took his first life on the moors, at an Ashwinder camp where Rookwood’s men had been waiting for him: a wizard, with the Killing Curse. He did not stop at one. Over that year he left whole camps dead, Ashwinders, poachers and Loyalists, and among the goblins were some who had only been defending their own camps. When the Aurors found the bodies they blamed rival gangs, or the goblins, or each other. No one looked for a fifth-year from Ravenclaw, and Duplicity went on being kind to everyone he met at Hogwarts.

One person did know. Fig heard enough, and saw enough, to understand what his student was doing, and said nothing. Miriam’s work had to be finished, and the boy was the only one who could finish it.

Lodgok

Lodgok came to Duplicity for help. He had met Miriam before her death; she had treated him with a respect he had rarely had from wizards, and he suspected that his brother had a hand in killing her. He had seen what the stolen magic was doing to Ranrok, and he wanted to bring his people back to the cause without the poison in it. Duplicity recovered for him the Helmet of Urtkot, the old symbol of the goblin clans, from Rookwood’s men. Then, with a classmate who could read Gobbledegook, he slipped into a mine Ranrok controlled and learned that the Loyalists were building a machine to drill down to the repositories.

Lodgok went to reason with his brother alone. He carried Bragbor’s last journal, which he had kept hidden from Ranrok all along, because it showed where the final repository lay. Rookwood’s men caught him and dragged him before Ranrok. When Ranrok read that the last repository lay beneath Hogwarts, and understood that Lodgok had known it all along, he called his brother a traitor and killed him with the corrupted magic he had taken.

Fitzgerald’s Memory

The third trial was the headmistress’s. Its memory showed Isidora as a professor, taking pain from a student who never knew it, and keeping what she took. Fig and Duplicity also followed Miriam’s trail to its end at Rookwood Castle, built by the Keeper Charles Rookwood on top of a repository, where Miriam had gone looking for Bragbor’s journal and found Ranrok instead.

The One They Missed

Sebastian had been searching for the source of Anne’s curse, and he took Duplicity to the place where she had been attacked: a cellar beneath an abandoned house at the edge of Feldcroft. It had been the Morganach family home. On its wall hung a painted triptych, and in its centre panel Isidora Morganach looked down at Duplicity and spoke to him. It was the one portrait the Keepers had never found.

She was neither the monster in the Keepers’ memories nor the saint she believed herself to be. She told him her side: a father who had asked, a teacher who never explained, and four professors who killed a woman they had never tried to understand. She did not pretend she had been right to take pain from people who never offered it. She asked whether the Keepers had told him how she died. Duplicity came back to her more than once that year, and she was the only person he told everything.

Judge and Executioner

The fourth trial was San Bakar’s, and its last test was a question. Bakar’s portrait asked Duplicity about the camps: how many he had killed, whether every one of them had deserved it, and who had made him their judge. Duplicity answered every question, and then asked Bakar the same ones. Bakar’s memory showed what he had found in Isidora’s father, and what he had done in the caverns. He had killed someone he judged beyond saving and called it protection, and in all the centuries since he had never been sure he was right. Neither of them gave the other an answer.

The Keepers’ Wand

From the relics of the four trials, the wandmaker Gerbold Ollivander made Duplicity a wand that would open the way to the final repository. Victor Rookwood was waiting for him outside the shop in Hogsmeade, with his Ashwinders. Duplicity killed him, and so the man who had cursed Anne Sallow died at the hands of a student.

The Final Repository

Ranrok drilled into the Keepers’ caverns beneath Hogwarts. While Professor Matilda Weasley and the rest of the staff held the Loyalists back from the castle, Fig and Duplicity went down to the repository. Ranrok reached it first. He broke it open with Miriam’s wand, which he had taken from her body, and drank in what it held: centuries of pain that Isidora had taken from people who never gave it. It made him a dragon of corrupted magic. Duplicity fought him in the caverns and brought him down, and Ranrok died in his own shape. Fig had been mortally wounded.

Looked Away

Fig did not die at once. He asked Duplicity to take what was left in the repository, to finish Miriam’s work, so that she would not have died for nothing. Then he confessed. He had known about the camps for months and had said nothing, because he needed what the boy could do. And Miriam had chosen Duplicity over him: only someone who could see the traces could follow her work, and she had wanted to keep her husband away from the danger. Fig had grieved for both, and had made a child carry the cost. Eleazar Fig died beneath the castle, beside the open repository.

What Is Given

The repository was still open, and everything argued for claiming it. Fig had asked with his last breath, for Miriam. Sebastian had asked all year, for Anne. The repository called to Duplicity itself, the pull of a power that anyone who had taken would want more of. Duplicity had taken more than anyone in that cavern, and he understood exactly what it was offering him. He sealed it, with magic of his own that no one has seen worked since.

At the end of the year Headmaster Phineas Nigellus Black gave Fig a brief eulogy, and Professor Weasley a long one. The rebellion was broken, and the Code of Wand Use stood unchanged.

Come Quietly

That summer the Aurors came for Duplicity. The Ministry had not found him through his friends: Ominis Gaunt knew what Duplicity had done, and what Sebastian had done, and he never said a word against either of them. The evidence came from Theophilus Harlow, Rookwood’s lieutenant, who had been in Ministry custody since the spring and bargained for his own sentence by naming the student who had wiped out his men. Duplicity did not resist.

He was convicted for the wizards he had killed. The goblins were not counted, because the Ministry did not count goblin lives. The trial was held behind closed doors and its record sealed. A public trial would have asked why a schoolboy had been left to stop a rebellion the Ministry had been warned about for months, while it enforced the Code that started it and let Rookwood and Harlow run the Highlands unchecked. Duplicity Magnaloid was sent to Azkaban, where he wasted away over the years that followed until he died. He made no Horcrux. The goblin rebellion of 1890 went into the history books, and he was left out of them.

Companion stories

Sebastian and Ominis

Sebastian Sallow wanted one thing that year: to cure his sister. Their uncle, Solomon Sallow, who had raised the twins, wanted only to keep Anne comfortable and far from the Dark Arts, and Anne agreed with him. Sebastian did not. He searched the cellar where she had been cursed, the Undercroft and a scriptorium of Salazar Slytherin’s, and every dark book he could reach. With every step Ominis argued harder that he should stop, and Duplicity went with him anyway. When the fighting came to Feldcroft, Sebastian used the Imperius Curse on a Loyalist to protect Anne, and Solomon banned him from the house.

In the catacombs beneath Feldcroft, Sebastian found a relic that promised to undo Dark magic in exchange for a dark sacrifice. Anne refused to be part of it and told Solomon, who went down to stop his nephew. Sebastian killed him with the Killing Curse. Anne carried her uncle’s body home and told the village he had died in his sleep. She left Sebastian a letter: she would always love him, she might never forgive him, and he should answer for what he had done. She was the only mourner at Solomon’s funeral.

Duplicity did not turn Sebastian in. To condemn Sebastian for one killing, he would have had to condemn himself for many. Ominis knew about both of them, and was the only one of the three who had ever said no. He kept their secrets, and carried them. Sebastian was never brought to trial for his uncle’s death. Duplicity went to Azkaban for the same crime, and Sebastian walked free.

Anne could have offered her pain freely, and given ancient magic might have eased it. It could not lift the curse, and that was why the repository went on tempting Sebastian to the end.

Natty

Natty’s father had been killed by bandits in Zimbabwe when she was nine, protecting her. She grew up at Uagadou with a gazelle as her Animagus form and a hatred of anyone who preyed on the helpless. At Hogwarts she set herself against Theophilus Harlow, Rookwood’s right hand, whose Ashwinders were terrorizing the villages around Hogsmeade. When the Ministry would not act, she went from victim to victim gathering testimony against him. Her grief turned into a hunger for vengeance, and it took her most of the year to find her focus again.

When they finally cornered Harlow in a manor on Manor Cape, Natty chose to hand him over rather than kill him. She also watched Duplicity kill Harlow’s men around her, and had to decide what that made the friend who had helped her all year. She never entirely did. Harlow went to the Ministry alive, and it was his testimony that later put Duplicity in Azkaban.

Poppy

Poppy Sweeting’s parents, Angus and Violet, were poachers who hunted and sold magical creatures. She was raised by her grandmother, who studied creatures without harming them. As a child Poppy freed a hippogriff her parents’ associates meant to kill, and kept it as her secret friend. She named it Highwing. At Hogwarts she found Duplicity willing to help with anything, and she brought him into her war on the poachers, who had joined Ranrok’s Loyalists. Together they broke up Horntail Hall, where a captive Hebridean Black dragon was being made to fight, and set the dragon free. Then they returned the egg the poachers had stolen to her nest in the mountains.

When the poachers went after her grandmother in revenge, they left behind notes about Golden Snidgets, a species believed to be extinct. Poppy swallowed her fear that the centaurs would learn what her family was, and went to them for help. Elek, their leader, met her with hostility, but Dorran sent her to the Moonstone Garden, where mooncalves danced in the moonlight, and then to the Gilded Perch, the Snidgets’ last refuge. Poppy, Duplicity and the centaurs drove off the poachers’ ambush together, and Poppy watched three Snidget eggs hatch.

Afterwards she found the camps. The boy who had freed a dragon and saved the Snidgets beside her was the same one who had left the poachers dead. Poppy could not make the two of them into one person, and she never stopped trying.

Changes from Canon

  • The arc retells Hogwarts Legacy: its main story and the Sebastian, Natty and Poppy questlines. The protagonist is a named character, , a Ravenclaw, and the story judges him rather than treating him as a hero.
  • Duplicity is not a late transfer. He has been at Hogwarts since his first year, and his first four years are part of the story. Fig, his Magical Theory teacher all along, comes to collect him because Miriam’s last letter named him.
  • Isidora’s first working was given: her father asked her to take his grief. Her fall was taking pain from people who never offered it, and keeping what she took.
  • The Keepers are judged. Bakar’s use of the Killing Curse is their founding shame, Rackham never forgave him, and they still argue about it in their portraits.
  • The Dementors are the reason the Keepers feared Isidora’s art: they had already seen what feeding on taken joy makes of a creature.
  • One portrait of Isidora survives in the cellar of her old home at Feldcroft, and she speaks for herself.
  • Fig knows what his student is doing in the Highlands and keeps silent because he needs the results. He confesses before he dies.
  • The goblin rebellion is reworked. The goblins’ grievance against the Code of Wand Use is legitimate, Ranrok’s seizing of taken magic is the real villainy, and Lodgok stands for the cause without it. The Ministry looks away throughout.
  • The protagonist’s killings are real deaths with consequences. Among the goblins he kills are some who were only defending their own camps, and the story says so.
  • Sebastian teaches Duplicity all three Unforgivable Curses over the autumn, before Solomon’s death rather than after it.
  • The trials become a reckoning. Bakar’s trial ends in questions about Duplicity’s killings, and the final choice over the repository is weighed against what everyone wants from it.
  • The story continues past the end of the school year.

Trivia

  • Duplicity’s name is the theme of the arc: a boy known to everyone as the kind one.
  • The arc’s tale and its story mirror each other. Isidora, a gifted student, is killed by the teachers who would not explain themselves; Duplicity, a gifted student, is failed by the one teacher who knew and said nothing.
  • Duplicity and San Bakar each killed someone they judged beyond saving and called it protection. Bakar’s trial is the only place where either of them is asked about it.
  • Natty’s choice to take Harlow alive is what sends Duplicity to Azkaban.
  • Sebastian and Duplicity commit the same crime. One goes to Azkaban and the other walks free, and Ominis knows the truth about both.
  • A century later, Professor Binns sets Harry’s year an essay on the goblin rebellion of 1890. Binns teaches the official history, the one with no student in it ().

Behind the scenes

  • Duplicity Magnaloid began as the author’s own Hogwarts Legacy character. The arc keeps that playthrough’s choices: a Ravenclaw who learned the Unforgivable Curses, never turned Sebastian in, and sealed the repository.
  • The banner and the arc icon are official Hogwarts Legacy art.
