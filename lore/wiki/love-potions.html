A love potion is any potion brewed to make its drinker infatuated with a particular person. None of them creates love. What they create is an obsession the drinker cannot tell apart from love, and in doing so they take away the drinker’s consent to whatever follows. They are as old as potion-making, but they became truly dangerous in the nineteenth century, when the potioneer perfected them and brewed the strongest of all, .

Love potions were outlawed in Laverne’s own lifetime, with her support. Brewing, selling or giving one is a crime against the person, punished with Azkaban. They have never been stamped out: like any forbidden trade, they are still brewed in secret by more hands than the Ministry can catch., “What is taken” The best-known victim of one was the father of ., “The Lessons”

Effects

What a love potion does

A love potion fixes the drinker’s whole attention on one person, usually whoever gave it to them. They think of little else, want nothing but that person’s company, and will do what that person wants, often against their own interests and against everything they would choose for themselves. To the drinker it feels like falling in love. To anyone watching it looks like nothing of the kind.

Strength varies with the brew. A weak potion makes a drinker foolish for an afternoon. A strong one, Amortentia above all, can hold someone for as long as the doses continue.

What it cannot do

No potion can make one person love another. Potion-makers have known this for centuries, and Horace Slughorn told his students as much in 1996., “The Prince” A love potion counterfeits the feeling without the substance. Nor can it last on its own: it fades unless the drinker is dosed again. When it lifts, nothing is left behind that the drinker did not feel before, and often a good deal less.

That is the deepest reason the oldest magic counts love potions among the things that take. Love is the one thing that has to be given. A love potion manufactures its likeness by taking the giver’s choice away.

The antidote

An antidote exists, and works quickly. It may be brewed only under licence, by Healers and a few licensed potion-masters, and victims are treated at St Mungo’s Hospital for Magical Maladies and Injuries. Recovering from the potion takes minutes. Recovering from what was done while it held can take far longer.

The law

The ban

The wizarding world outlawed love potions in the nineteenth century, within a few years of Amortentia’s appearance, and one of the voices raised for the ban before the Wizengamot was its own inventor’s. The law draws no line between strong potions and weak ones, or between a joke and a design. To brew a love potion, to sell one, or to give one to anybody is a crime against the person, judged like any other attack on someone’s will, and its sentence is Azkaban.

Licensed exceptions

Two exceptions are allowed, both under Ministry licence. Healers may brew the antidote. Schools may keep a sealed sample of Amortentia so that students can learn to recognize it. At it is shown and never taught: no student learns how it is brewed.

The illegal trade

Like any forbidden thing that people will pay for, love potions never vanished. Laverne de Montmorency spent her last years helping the Ministry hunt down every bottle and copied recipe she could, and she could not find them all. From those copies grew a trade with no single source: many small brewers, each working in secret, selling through the back rooms of Knockturn Alley, through anonymous owls, and in bottles passed off as perfume or sweets. The Ministry’s raids catch some of them, and others take their place.

History

Before the ban

For most of their long history love potions were crude, weak and short-lived, and the wizarding world treated them as a joke, or at worst a nuisance. Few people asked what it meant to drink one, or to be given one without knowing.

Laverne de Montmorency

(1823–1893) was the most gifted love-potion maker of her age. She refined the old recipes into potions far stronger and longer-lasting than any before them, and her masterwork, Amortentia, became the most powerful love potion ever brewed. For a time she was celebrated for it.

Then she saw what her potions were used for. The strength she had worked so hard to give them turned every bottle into a way to take someone’s will, and those who bought them seldom meant anything kinder. Laverne came to call them the worst mistake of her life. She spoke before the Wizengamot in favour of the ban, and spent the rest of her years working beside the Ministry to find and destroy what she had made: stock, notes and the copies of her recipes that had already spread. She died in 1893 knowing she had not found them all.

Merope Gaunt

Main article:

The case the wizarding world remembers best was decades after the ban. Merope Gaunt, the last daughter of an old, ruined pure-blood family, grew up poor and mistreated in a hovel outside Little Hangleton, and fell for a handsome Muggle from the village, Tom Riddle. In the 1920s she gave him a love potion. He left his own life to marry her, against his will and without knowing it.

Merope broke the law to do it, and in time she stopped dosing him, perhaps hoping he had come to love her by then. He had not. He left her, already expecting their child, and she died soon after giving birth to a son in a London orphanage. The boy grew up to become . Albus Dumbledore showed Harry Potter all of this in 1996. Voldemort was born of a love that was taken, never given, and grew into a man who would only ever take.

At Hogwarts

Main article:

Love potions are banned at Hogwarts as they are everywhere else. In 1996 Horace Slughorn opened his first lesson for the sixth-years by showing them a cauldron of Amortentia, brewed under licence, so that they would know it on sight and by smell, and know why it was outlawed. Fred and George Weasley, whose joke shop in Diagon Alley would sell almost anything that made people laugh, never made or sold a love potion, and never would.

Changes from Canon

  • Love potions are outlawed everywhere as a crime against consent, punished with Azkaban, rather than merely banned at school and sold openly elsewhere.
  • Laverne de Montmorency regretted creating them, supported the ban and helped the Ministry destroy them.
  • Fred and George Weasley never make or sell love potions; Weasleys’ Wizard Wheezes keeps its jokes and nothing that removes consent.
  • Romilda Vane’s spiked chocolates are cut, and nobody is ever dosed for a joke.
  • Merope Gaunt’s use of a love potion is named as the crime it is.
  • Amortentia is shown at Hogwarts only so students can recognize it, and its brewing is never taught.

Trivia

  • The love-potion ban is one of the oldest laws on the wizarding statute book that protects a person from magic rather than magic from Muggles.
  • The one who argued hardest for outlawing love potions was the witch who had made the best of them.

Behind the scenes

  • The original books mostly play love potions for laughs. Weasleys’ Wizard Wheezes sells them, Romilda Vane slips them into chocolates, and Ron Weasley’s accidental dosing is a comic scene. The Archive treats them as what they are, a way of taking away someone’s consent, and makes them a serious crime. It is one of the most deliberate changes in this continuity.
  • In canon, Laverne de Montmorency appears only on a Famous Witches and Wizards card as an inventor of love potions. Her regret and her part in the ban are the Archive’s.
  • The infobox picture is an officially licensed love-potion pendant by The Noble Collection, styled after the heart-shaped love-potion bottles of the films.
