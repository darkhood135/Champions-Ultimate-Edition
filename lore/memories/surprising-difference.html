Memory

Surprising Difference

Harry Potter Memory · The Wizarding World

The Hogwarts Express sat waiting at the platform, its horn blaring, as Harry prepared for his departure from Hogwarts. It’d been a great first year, all things considered, but now as he stood in front of the train he couldn’t help but feel glum. He was dreading the inevitable reunion he’d have with the Dursleys, the looks they’d shoot him and the treatment they’d give him. Would they even let him back inside? Harry didn’t know, but he had nowhere else to go. He glanced around, seeing if Hermione or Ron were anywhere near to hopefully cheer up his spirits. They must still be grabbing their stuff, he thought, taking the hesitant walk up to the car door as other students ran past him with excitement he wished he had. When they didn’t show at first, he opted to board alone, sighing as he took a step onto the steps of the train, the start of his return to a miserable old life.

He made his way to his usual compartment, the same one he’d sat in with Ron and Hermione at the start of the year. Wow, he thought, we really misjudged Hermione back then. He still found himself feeling awful for never speaking out against Ron's comments. Regardless, they were friends now, and he couldn’t be happier.

As he sat down, dropping into the blue, cozy seat, he took a moment to take it in. He glanced around the room, realizing it’d be the last truly magical thing he’d see for another few months. Even the train seemed to carry a bit of Hogwarts with it; the maroon curtains and the dim light of the candles kept the compartment feeling warm and welcoming, a light scent of sandalwood amidst the noise of people bustling outside. He closed his eyes for just a moment, thinking maybe he’d take a nap, when the compartment door slid open. Opening his eyes slowly, Harry turned and expected to see Ron or Hermione.

Who he did not expect was Malfoy.

He immediately woke up, his energy coming back in an instant. His mind on guard and ready for anything, he waited to see what she could possibly want. Without saying a word, she simply sat down across from him. He waited a moment, his heart racing now, for her to speak up and explain herself. When she didn’t, Harry began to speak before she cut him off with her hand.

“Before you say anything,” she started, “I’m not here to take the seats from your half-wit friends.”

“Then… what do you want?” he responded, already agitated as he frowned at her, wishing that she’d just leave. She averted her eyes as she laid back in the seat of the compartment, nearly kicking his shins as she got comfortable. “I wanted to say…” She hesitated as she let out two more words, a frustration visible on her face. “Well played.”

“What?” Harry, confused, responded. Malfoy continued. “You lost, of course. We were destined to win from the start but… all things considered? You weren’t too bad, for a half-blood, Potter.” Malfoy admitted, her eyes remaining focused on the window as she ignored his direct presence. He looked at her blankly before she finally turned towards him with a bored expression. “This is about the… House Cup? Really? That’s all you wanted to say?” he spoke, unmoving. Malfoy scowled as she looked at him, her usual brooding antagonism coming to the forefront.

“Well yes, of course. What else would I be referring to, genius?” she spat, crossing her arms.

“…So what? You just wanted to rub it in my face?” he responded. She groaned audibly and dramatically, looking up at the ceiling with frustration as her ears began to glow red. “Are you even listening to me, moron? I told you already… well played. You… you did well, is all I’m saying. So… good job, anyway, despite your pathetic loss.”

Harry blinked. Malfoy, of all people, trying to be a good sport? I mean, she was definitely still rude about it, but…

How odd.

Even odder, however, was when she extended her hand towards him. Immediately, he was brought back to their last ride on this train. The moment he stood by Ron, when Crabbe and Goyle were attached to her hip. Unsure what to even think, a little baffled, he looked at her hand with hesitation. This wasn’t a friendship request, just a… good game. A show of sportsmanship.

Of course she’d be sportsmanlike when she won.

Still, he figured he’d oblige her. She was relatively nice in comparison to her usual antics, after all. Maybe this could be a moment she turns things around. He nearly laughed out loud at the idea, like there’d ever be a moment where she’d be truly nice to him. As he finally decided, he shook her hand (slightly) begrudgingly.

“Alright then… fine. Congratulations, I suppose.” he responded, annoyed but genuine. Despite all she did to him and his friends, she had earned more than her share of Slytherin’s points that year, her hand shooting up in nearly every class. As he shook her hand, she seemed to get distracted, her expression shifting a little as she simply stared with surprise. Off-put, Harry raised an eyebrow at her before she retracted her hand with unexpected speed, returning to her casual position as though she hadn’t done that at all. She laughed awkwardly as her next words came out a little mangled.

“What a shock, a Gryffindor actually willing to admit their inferiority. Well, I suppose you were always a good sport, Potter.”

Is that a compliment?

A strange realization crossed his mind that she was being a little more tolerable.

“I try to be. Though, it’s pretty tough when you try to rub it in my face all the time.” he argued, looking at the compartment door with quick glances. Where was Ron? Hermione? He couldn’t wait for them to show, if only to throw aside the confusion he was feeling right now.

“Why wouldn’t I? You seem to always need reminders on how much better I am.” Malfoy laughed, twirling her hair with her index finger as she eyed him down, a strange playful look mounting her face. He crossed his arms as he sighed, unsure how to feel or what to say. “I mean… the other houses still did well, all things considered. Gryffindor was pretty upset since—”

“You got that detention? Yeah, I’m pretty surprised myself that they scraped together all those points after you and Longbottom nearly doomed them.” she remarked, smugness radiating from her voice as she leaned her head on her hand. Harry narrowed his eyes at her, recalling that night vividly. “… Are you serious?”

“What?”

“You act like you weren’t there with us.” he said, tilting his head a little as she waved her hand to dismiss him. “Yeah, yeah. Sorry, I must have forgotten, given I so courageously faced the dangers.” she spoke. A slight blush mounted her face as Harry looked at her blankly, the memory of her frightened self clinging to him coming to the forefront of his mind. “Right…” he groaned, rubbing his temple as he hunched over on his seat. A small silence settled between them before he shook his head and continued. “Anyhow, are we done here, Malfoy?” She recoiled slightly, scoffing like she took offense. “Wow… in such a rush to discard me, Potter?” She pouted, puffing out her cheeks. Harry shook his head as he argued. “I mean… yeah? A handshake changes nothing if you’re going to keep being so mean.”

“Well… maybe if you hadn’t been so mean to me, before!”

“You called Ron ‘the wrong sort’!” he exclaimed, putting his hands out in agitation. “Was I supposed to be nice to you after that? He’s my best friend!” He knew she knew this already, how couldn’t she? She’d only ever been rude to him since that day. Strangely enough, she actually seemed to think on what he said for a moment, glancing around the room with a speculative expression before finally opening her mouth again.

“Well, I… I guess you might be right, Potter. Fine. Still, what kind of fool doesn’t want to be friends with me?” She tossed her hair as she smirked, her usual pride coming to the forefront. To this, Harry sighed, glancing away as he pondered how he’d respond next. If he was honest with himself, he didn’t hate her. She hadn’t done anything quite so awful that he’d never forgive her. If anything, it’d be kind of cool to be friends with a talented Slytherin. But still, her pride and her treatment of his friends were too much for him. But maybe, if he told her…? He let the offer spill out of his mouth before he could think any further.

“Well, we still could be if—”

“Really?!” she exclaimed, her eyes widening in surprise and excitement, shocking Harry as he sat up in surprise. Immediately she slammed her hand onto her mouth as he looked on, bewildered. “Malfoy…?” Malfoy sat deadly still as she maintained eye contact with him, red spreading across her face.

“I… um…” She fumbled her words, fidgeting with her hands as her eyes darted all around the compartment. Harry tilted his head as he looked at Malfoy, like he was looking at a new creature.

Malfoy? Excited? To… be friends with me? Seriously?

Before he could ask her anything more, the compartment door opened and she immediately shifted her position. Ron and Hermione appeared in the doorway.

“Malfoy?!” Ron sneered. “What are you doing here?” Hermione stood behind him, looking equally bothered by her presence. Malfoy stood up carefully, calculated, like she hadn’t just reacted as she did. If Harry hadn’t been there he’d have no idea she was so embarrassed just a moment ago. He breathed in deeply as he dreaded their inevitable argument to come.

“Just warning your friend to stay out of my way, Weasley. And that goes for you, too.” She wiped off her robe as she frowned. Hermione, a scowl on her face, stepped in front of Ron defensively, pointing an accusatory finger at Malfoy.

“You’re a nuisance, Malfoy. You sent Crabbe and Goyle after us, didn’t you?”

Harry blinked. Suddenly, it made sense. No wonder Crabbe and Goyle weren’t with her. She… she intended to speak with just him to begin with. Why didn’t she just…? Harry was confused, thinking on all he knew about her and weighing it in his head. He truly did think she hated him, but… maybe she didn’t? Maybe he could convince her to—

Harry’s thoughts cut off as he tuned back into their argument.

“Why would I bother sending them after you two fools? I could’ve dealt with you myself.” Malfoy responded, leaning forward as she towered over Hermione. “Yeah right! You’re somehow even dumber than your two cronies.” she spat back, inching closer as she balled her fists. Malfoy balled up her own fists, looking briefly towards Harry as he looked at her with warning, hoping she’d back down and leave rather than continue to bother his friends. Strangely, her eyes showed some regret, a slight flicker of change as she turned to Hermione again. “Whatever. Enjoy your time with this fool.” She waved her hand as though to disregard him, pushing past both Ron and Hermione. “See you next year, Potter! Maybe next time you won’t disgrace your entire house.” She waved a sarcastic goodbye his way with a smirk as she left the compartment, shutting the door behind her.

Harry stared at where she left, dumbfounded.

What just… happened?

Ron sat next to him as Hermione sat across from them. She thoroughly wiped down her seat as though Malfoy’s germs were poisonous.

“So, Harry… what was that about?” Hermione asked, opening up a Chocolate Frog as she got settled. “Honestly? I have no idea. She… seemed like she wanted to be friends.” Harry replied, unsure how to describe what just transpired. Ron reacted first, his head turning towards him in an instant.

“You’re kidding!” He laughed, slamming his hand on his mouth as he cackled. “Her? After what she did at the start of the year? Unreal!” Hermione, in turn, laughed a little. “Doesn’t she make fun of you at every given opportunity?”

“Yeah, pretty much. That’s why I’m so confused.” Harry admitted, scratching his neck. “I started to tell her I’d consider it if she’d be nicer, but—”

“Oh come on, Harry! The bully of the school? She just wants you to become another follower.” Ron exclaimed, throwing his hands in the air. Harry glanced to Hermione for her opinion as she simply nodded her head lightly in agreement. “I mean… I guess you’re right. I didn’t get the chance to finish, anyways. She cut me off with excitement before I even got to say anything.”

“Well, let’s hope she doesn’t bother us again. I’ve heard enough of her insults for a lifetime.” Ron said, leaving it there as he took out his own Chocolate Frog. “Want one, Harry?”

Harry blinked at the frog before smiling, taking it in his hand. Maybe they were right. But who knows what the future would bring?

See you again next year, Malfoy. For better or for worse.
