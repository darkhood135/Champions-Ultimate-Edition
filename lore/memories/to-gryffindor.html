To Gryffindor

Harry Potter Memory · The Wizarding World

“Welcome, one and all, to Hogwarts. I am Professor McGonagall, the Head of Gryffindor House.” Her voice echoed through the chamber, all of the first-years packed tight into the room as they prepared to enter the main entrance hall. “As you all should know by now, today we will be hosting the sorting ceremony. Each and every one of you, by the end of tonight, shall be placed into one of the four designated houses: Gryffindor, Hufflepuff, Ravenclaw or Slytherin. Your house, while you’re here, shall be like your family. You’ll share a table, a dormitory and a common room with them, and be responsible for their actions as much as your own.”

“Doesn’t that seem a little much?” Harry whispered to Ron, keeping his voice as low as possible. “I mean, what if we don’t get into the same house?”

“I’m sure we will.” Ron replied, a little louder than Harry would’ve liked. “My family has always been in Gryffindor so I ought to be there too. And you’re the Boy Who Lived! If anyone’s destined for Gryffindor, it’s surely you.” Harry shrugged, pointing his attention once more towards McGonagall.

“The House Cup, by the end of the year, is given to the house with the most points. Each house has a tally. For every good action performed during the year, points will be given. For every bad action performed, points will be deducted. Each and every one of you will be in a shared pool with the rest of your house, meaning that your every action determines the fate of the victor. I do hope you will act responsibly with this in mind, since all teachers at Hogwarts are permitted to deduct and grant points as they please.”

“All of them?” Ron gulped. “I’ve heard some of the Slytherin teachers can be real dodgy…”

McGonagall continued. “When the sorting commences, I do expect you all to stay respectful of every other student. Should I hear of you mocking another, your house will be deducted immense numbers of points. And let me be upfront: I will not treat you any differently should you be placed in Gryffindor. Do I make myself clear?”

The students nodded (“Yes, Professor”) as she resumed once more. “Good. Alright then; tidy yourselves up, and follow me. Single-file, please.” She walked near the door of the chamber, waiting for the students to organize themselves properly as Harry and Ron found their spots next to each other. A few arguing ghosts glided through the hall, surprising the students, before departing just as quickly.

“Do you have any idea how this actually works?” Harry asked Ron, turning to him. “Not sure.” Ron shrugged as he curled his lip nervously. “My brother Fred told me it hurts.”

“Surely not! Blimey.” Harry worried, his nerves definitely higher now. Would there be a test? Public embarrassment? Would he be a Gryffindor? A Slytherin…? A…

“Let us depart.” With a flick of McGonagall’s wand, what seemed like hundreds of candles began to soar around the students, appearing like guiding lights for those who’d lose track. As they entered the entrance hall, students gaped at the architecture; a magnificent emblem adorned the staircase ahead, representing all of the houses with a huge H in the center. Statues and lanterns sat against the wall at the top of the stairs, feeling grand beyond anything Harry had ever seen. As McGonagall led them past the stairs to the right, she opened the doors to where they’d be sorted: the Great Hall.

“We’ve arrived, children.” As if it couldn’t get any more magnificent, Harry glanced around in utter wonder. The room was massive; the ceiling towered above them, looking like the starry sky itself had been imbued into the very materials used to construct it. Pillars stretched from the floor to the top, reaching so high he could barely fathom their scale. The candles, previously used to guide the students, soared past them and took their positions around the room, lighting it with a warm glow. Four huge tables adorned in house insignias sat along the pillars, with one High Table at the front for the teachers themselves. Students of older years sat in their respective houses, each awaiting the new students. A stained glass window sat above their table alongside various paintings (moving!) showcasing wizards and witches of various houses and eras.

“This is… wow.” Harry muttered, trying to take in everything at once. “I know, right? It’s bloody beautiful.” Ron replied, his gaze looking as mesmerized as Harry’s. McGonagall clapped her hands together as the two of them jumped.

“Please stay focused, now. You will see much more wondrous sights than these.” She led them through the center of the hall, the tables lining both sides of them with older students. Stopping them in place as they neared the front, she walked up the stairs to the High Table and stood at the center. Harry recognized a few teachers, one being Hagrid. They exchanged small waves as Hagrid smiled at him before McGonagall resumed.

“Before we begin, students, a word from our headmaster, Albus Dumbledore.” She bowed his way before standing to the side slightly, Dumbledore standing from his center spot at the table.

“Greetings, children! My name is Albus Percival Wulfric Brian Dumbledore. No, you need not remember it; simply call me Professor Dumbledore. I’ve come to bear small notices to remember before we begin with the ceremony. Firstly, I must once again remind you that no one house is better than another. Though you will be rivals, though you will be competitive, there is no need to discriminate against one another.” Harry glanced over at a mumbling student at the Slytherin table who looked unamused. Dumbledore continued. “Secondly, something every first-year must know: do not enter the Forbidden Forest. I assure you we have granted it that name for a reason, and if it is found out any of you have entered said forest then there will be consequences beyond simple point reductions. It is for your own safety, I assure you; I do not mean to intrude on your adventurous spirit beyond where I must.” He smiled warmly with a light laugh as he gave one final notice. “Thirdly, Mister Filch…” Dumbledore motioned in his direction. “… has asked me to remind you that the third-floor corridor on the right side is currently off-limits. Do not, and I truly mean it, enter said corridor. We’ve blocked it off with magic but I know how you young folks can be sometimes. I will let you know in no uncertain terms that anyone who enters this corridor will likely find themselves in grave danger. That is all for now, students. Thank you.” Dumbledore bowed before returning to his seat, the students clapping as McGonagall took center stage once more.

She motioned for assistance from one of the other teachers, a man in purple with a turban on his head. Stepping off to the side, he came back with an old, tattered hat which he placed upon a stool in the front and center. Unsure what they were looking at, Harry and Ron glanced at each other. It seemed to have face-like qualities, tears and rips that looked like the vague shape of eyes and a mouth.

“This, children, is the Sorting Hat.” McGonagall called, holding it high above her head. “It shall read your thoughts, your mind, and determine to which house you belong. I shall warn you that your aspirations will only get you so far, so please do not get attached to any given house until you’ve been assigned.” Harry, hearing this, looked over at Ron, who remained undeterred. Suddenly and shockingly, a tear in the hat opened up as it began to talk.

“Greetings, children.” Gasps and screams echoed from the first-year students, each one being unsure how to react. It spoke in a raspy, loud voice easily audible to the entire room. “Come on then, I’m not that horrid! Calm yourselves this instant.” In response, and maybe fear, the students all became deadly silent. “Right then. I have a song for you today, so pay attention now!” In response to the hat’s words, McGonagall clapped and the candles all went out, only a few around the hat remaining, which lit it with a small spotlight.

“I may not be pretty,
I do admit,
But no one can match
My knowledge and wit.

You will surely find
In time, I do tell
That I know your heart
Yes, I know it quite well!

I read your mind,
Your thoughts, your souls
And assign you a house
To accomplish your goals.

The houses all come
From important folk,
Four in specific,
With one masterstroke.

Godric Gryffindor,
An honorable sort,
Prized courage and strength,
Thus creating his court.

His house endures
For those who are brave,
The ones who stand up,
When others misbehave.

Helga Hufflepuff,
Kind above all,
Was fiercely loyal
A beacon standing tall.

Her house stands
For those who remain
When all else would abandon
Someone in pain.

Rowena Ravenclaw,
With an intelligent plan,
Made her mark on the school
And established her clan.

Her house exists,
For those who seek wit,
Who treasure all learning
And live by it.

Salazar Slytherin
Was great, though tragic,
Since he got himself involved
In some dastardly magic.

His house pushes on,
A beacon of cunning,
With leadership and ambition
That leaves their foes running.

Though their fates went apart,
Their stories still stand,
Friends who united
From distant lands.

And though they had flaws,
Their impacts still last,
And those who get sorted,
Discover their past.

So come be sorted,
Join those long before you,
Learn and grow wiser
To become something new.

Though many will worry
What their impact will be,
When houses unite
There is no greater key.”

As the song ended, the room applauded, teachers and students alike, as McGonagall lit the candles once more. Harry pondered to himself what house he’d wind up in, unsure whether he had any preference at all. He’d had bad experiences with Slytherin, sure, but they couldn’t all be bad. He supposed, if he would be in any, he’d like to be in the same house as Ron. “I can’t believe it’s just a hat. I’m going to kill Fred.” Ron whispered, annoyance present in his voice.

“I will call your names alphabetically.” McGonagall called, as she pulled out a long sheet of parchment. “You shall try on the hat and it shall assign you to your house. Then you are free to take a seat with your classmates.” As she cleared her throat, she read the first name on the list. Then another, and another.

Hannah Abbott, Hufflepuff. Susan Bones, Hufflepuff. Terry Boot, Ravenclaw. Many more names came and went as Harry and Ron waited their turn.

“How long until it reaches us?” Ron asked, tilting his head. Harry blinked at him, laughing a little. “It’s alphabetical, Ron.”

“Oh, right.” Ron laughed in turn, as they refocused on the hat.

“Hermione Granger!” McGonagall called out, as the excited Hermione ran up to the stool. Harry heard Ron groan beside him as he watched on. She put the hat on her head with excitement, a big smile on her face.

The Hat shifted on her head as it pondered. It thought for nearly a minute as Hermione began to shift a little awkwardly, glancing around the room. Finally, it called out. “Ravenclaw!” Ravenclaw celebrated as Hermione’s smile re-emerged and she ran over to her new house with a pep in her step.

“Phew, what a relief.” Ron spoke. “I don’t know if I’d be able to bear her presence in Gryffindor.” Harry, a little uncomfortable at his comments, didn’t respond.

He sounds a little like Malfoy.

“Neville Longbottom!” McGonagall called next, as a familiar round-faced boy walked up to the hat. Harry recalled him from the train; the one who lost his toad. He put the hat on timidly, an unsure look on his face. In an instant, the hat had decided. “Gryffindor!” Shocked, Neville opened his mouth slowly into a grin before taking off the hat and heading to his house.

“Dracaena Malfoy!” McGonagall called, as the familiar blond strode her way up to the stool. Before even putting on the hat, she tossed her hair aside dramatically. Harry heard Ron smack his face in judgment as Malfoy donned it. It took… surprisingly long. Whispers began to echo around the room as it took one minute… then two. The Hat, expressive as it was, appeared a little unsure. Malfoy, turning a little red now, opened her mouth. “Are you going to put me into Slytherin or what?”

A second later, a horrified look flashed on Malfoy’s face. “What?! What could you possibly mean? I’m… I’m destined for Slytherin!” she cried, looking a little mortified now. Harry wasn’t sure whether to feel bad or find it funny. Ron, however, found it hilarious.

After a few more moments, the Hat finally called “Slytherin!” as Malfoy let out an excited scream, her mood changing instantly. Slytherin roared with excitement and, like she was royalty, she bowed with the hat in hand before walking with swagger to her table.

“Can you imagine that? Malfoy in anything but Slytherin? Yeah right.” Ron chuckled.

A few more names went by as Harry waited nervously. His name would be coming up soon, he was sure of it.

“Harry Potter!” McGonagall finally cried, as the room fell deadly silent. All of a sudden, the pressure was on. Who would the great Harry Potter be? Would he live up to the expectations? Harry gulped as he walked up to the stool, gaining his composure with a deep breath as he put on the hat. Immediately the hat began speaking to him in his mind, its voice echoing through his very thoughts. “Mmmm, yes… many options with this one. Courage, you have in strides. A great mind as well… loyalty, in abundance. As great as them all is a thirst… to prove yourself.” It nodded as Harry listened, waiting to hear his verdict as the room looked on in great interest. All eyes were on him as he felt the pressure begin to mount. “Perhaps… Slytherin? Or… no… no, that won’t work at all. You could be nothing but a…” The Hat cried out loud at last. “Gryffindor!”

Harry couldn’t believe it. The Gryffindor table launched out of their seats with more excitement than for any other student, cheering and yelling. Harry saw Ron excitedly hop up and down as he took off the hat and walked over to the Gryffindor table with a smile on his face. He’d found his house; the hard part was over. Three ginger boys, whom Harry instantly recognized as Ron’s brothers, pumped their fists in the air as they chanted his name. When he finally sat down, he looked at the High Table to see Hagrid giving him a thumbs up.

A few more names passed as Harry awaited Ron’s entrance to Gryffindor.

When McGonagall called Ron’s name, the whole room was waiting for the expected verdict. The Weasley family was known to be Gryffindors at heart, to their very core. Any other option was highly unlikely, maybe even impossible. Ron walked his way up to the stool with care, rolling up his sleeves a little as he put on the hat. The Hat took but a moment, before calling out his house. “Hufflepuff!” The room went deadly quiet as Harry’s eyes widened. He turned to face the Weasley twins, who looked equally as shocked, their expressions morphing from joy into confusion. Ron blinked, then began to glance around nervously. “H-Hufflepuff…?” Ron asked, his face turning a sickly green now. “A-are you sure…? B-but what if I… what if I don’t want to be a Hufflepuff? What about Gryffindor?” The Hat shook its head. “To Hufflepuff!” And just like that, he was sorted. McGonagall took the hat off his head with a pat on the back as he stood up with a thousand-yard stare. Harry heard three people around him stand up first as Ron’s brothers stood up cheering for him, their enthusiasm just as intense as it was for Harry. Some of Hufflepuff cheered, but Harry noticed many simply seemed confused. Ron, still looking mortified, walked his way over with a quick look at Harry, his eyes teary. The Gryffindor table looked between each other as some of them began to whisper before Percy shut them down.

Before long, the sorting was over. McGonagall rolled up her scroll and said her final words of the night. “Alright, children. It is time, then, for the feast. Professor Dumbledore, if you will.” Dumbledore uttered a few nonsense words as loads of food emerged onto their tables. Students gawked and rubbed their hands together in excitement, as they prepared to eat. It did look quite incredible, but Harry couldn’t focus on the food.

He glanced at Ron before he even began to eat, worrying for his friend. Ron looked like he couldn’t stomach anything at all as a few students around him tried to comfort him. Even seeing him in Hufflepuff, Harry knew he wouldn’t let that stop them from being friends.

What if I… walked over and sat with him?

Harry almost convinced himself not to. He’d make a scene to go sit at the Hufflepuff table. It’d seem traitorous, but… what did it really matter? And if Ron would have to deal with it alone, that wouldn’t be fair… he thought back to the cupboard. When he felt all alone, isolated under the stairs. Harry made his choice, then, as he stood up. Students around him looked up at him, the only student standing in the room. Immediately, Harry felt the pressure, the eyes. If it were for a selfish reason, he would’ve sat back down. But, now…

He took one step, then another, towards Hufflepuff. Like they knew his intentions, the students began to make room for him, parting from the spot next to Ron. Ron looked at him in wonder, his mouth opening in surprise as he raised his eyebrows. Harry glanced at his classmates and his teachers, to see judgment, envy and… hatred. A long-haired man at the High Table looked at him with contempt in a way that almost broke his composure. Yet, Harry sat. The room was deadly silent, all eyes on the boy who broke the rules. The teachers looked between each other, each waiting to see if someone would do something. McGonagall, looking confused (and like she had a lecture at the ready), glanced to Dumbledore. Harry looked over hesitantly at the headmaster as they exchanged glances. To his surprise, an understanding expression crossed Dumbledore’s face and… he smiled. Speaking loudly to his fellow teachers, Dumbledore gave the final word. “You may leave him be.”

“You can’t be serious! That foolish boy’s breaking the rules already!” cried the long-haired one, his anger so abundant he looked like he’d explode. “I said leave it be, Severus.” Dumbledore said sternly before nodding his head towards Harry with a smile. McGonagall, looking baffled, cleared her throat and magnified her voice. “Let it be known, children, that this is a one-time exception by the headmaster’s orders. You are still all to remain with your houses.” Finally, people began to settle, as talking began again in the hall. Harry still felt some eyes on him, and notably heard some upsetting discussion. But still, when he glanced back to Gryffindor, he saw that the Weasleys were proud of him. They all gave him a thumbs up as Harry smiled back, before returning his attention to Ron. “Sorry for making a scene, mate.” Harry said, adjusting his glasses.

“Are you kidding me?” Ron laughed, patting him on the back. “That’s one of the nicest things anyone has ever done for me. I can’t believe you got away with it!”

They ate and they laughed, and when it was finally over they went their separate ways. Harry rejoined Gryffindor with a smile as they huddled around him in awe. Some sat closer to him, and the betrayal Harry thought they’d feel wasn’t present whatsoever. After all, he’d done the unthinkable. He’d broken the rules.

I guess I am a Gryffindor, he thought.
