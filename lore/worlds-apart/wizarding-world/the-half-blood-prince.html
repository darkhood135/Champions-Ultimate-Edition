The Half-Blood Prince is the sixth story arc of in . It covers ’s sixth year at . Dumbledore teaches him the secret of Voldemort’s , while , branded and sworn to a task meant to destroy her, works to let the Death Eaters into the castle and kill the headmaster. It ends on the Astronomy Tower, with and Dumbledore dead.

Story

Spinner’s End

The war the Ministry had denied for a year was a war in the open now. People vanished, the Dark Mark hung over houses, and Cornelius Fudge was gone, replaced as Minister by the hard-faced Auror Rufus Scrimgeour. Lucius Malfoy had been caught at the Department of Mysteries and sent to Azkaban, and the Dark Lord did not forgive failure. The punishment he laid on the Malfoys was not death. It was their daughter.

Brought before Voldemort that summer, a presence vast and god-like in the room, Dracaena bowed. The Dark Mark was burned into her arm, whether she would have it or not, and she was given her task: mend the broken Vanishing Cabinet in the Room of Requirement so the Death Eaters could enter Hogwarts, and murder Albus Dumbledore herself, with her parents’ lives forfeit if she failed. Lucius and Narcissa Malfoy were poison to the whole world and tender only toward their daughter, and Voldemort had found the one lever that would move a girl who would never have killed for a cause. Her aunt Bellatrix spent the rest of the summer teaching her to close her mind, so that no one at Hogwarts could read what she was hiding. And Narcissa, out of a mother’s love, went to a narrow house at Spinner’s End and bound Severus Snape with an Unbreakable Vow: to watch over her daughter, and to finish the task himself if Dracaena could not.

The Train

Dumbledore collected Harry from Privet Drive himself that summer. His right hand was blackened and dead, and he wouldn’t yet say why. He took Harry to persuade a retired Potions master, Horace Slughorn, to come back to teach.

On the Hogwarts Express Dracaena found Harry alone, and for once she didn’t sneer. She said, at length and quietly, everything she had never let herself say: a warning that was also a confession. He should keep away from her and from all of it. There were things coming she could not stop and could not explain. Whatever might have been between them belonged to a future neither of them would be allowed to have. Then she froze him where he sat and left him there. The stillness she left behind was worse than any curse she had ever thrown. Tonks found him on the empty train hours later and took him up to the castle.

The Prince

Snape had at last been given the Defence Against the Dark Arts post, and Slughorn took Potions. Harry, short of a textbook, borrowed an old copy from the cupboard. Its margins were crammed with corrections and nameless spells in the hand of someone who called himself the Half-Blood Prince, and overnight it made Harry the best brewer in his year. Hermione disapproved on principle, and went looking in old copies of the Prophet for who the Prince might have been. She turned up only an old clipping about a witch named Eileen Prince, and no one knew what to make of it. Harry kept the book, telling himself its secrets might give him an edge against Voldemort when the time came, and used its spells without once asking what they did.

The Lessons

Dumbledore did something that year no adult had done before: he took Harry into his confidence. In private lessons over a stone basin of memories, he walked him back through the life of the boy Tom Riddle had been, from his mother’s miserable family, the Gaunts, to the orphanage, to the student who charmed his teachers and frightened everyone else. Voldemort had not simply cheated death, Dumbledore told him. He had torn his own soul into pieces and hidden them in objects, called Horcruxes, so that while even one remained he could not die. The pieces did more than keep him alive. They fed the appalling power Harry had seen rise from the cauldron. The diary Harry had destroyed in his second year had been one, and Voldemort had been the weaker for its loss without knowing why.

Dumbledore had found and destroyed a second over the summer: an old gold ring of the Gaunt family. It had cost him his hand. He kept the broken ring on his desk. Its black stone was cut with a strange sign, a triangle around a circle, split by a single line, and Harry had seen it before, on a string around Luna Lovegood’s neck. When he asked about it, Dumbledore turned the question gently aside. It was not the only thing he kept to himself that year.

Ada

Harry spent the whole year certain Dracaena was up to something terrible. He was right, and it did him no good at all. Everyone he told had the same gentle answer ready: he was obsessed with Dracaena Malfoy because of the Yule Ball, and his suspicion was a lovesick boy inventing reasons to watch a girl he couldn’t stop watching. There was just enough truth in it to make it unanswerable. He tracked her on the Marauder’s Map and lost her again and again to a room the map couldn’t show.

The one person who might have reached her was the one Dracaena would not endanger. Ada loved her plainly and asked nothing of her, and watched that year as Dracaena came apart at the seams, grey and sleepless and flinching at shadows. Ada tried, gently and stubbornly, to reach whatever was wrong, never understanding why her friend wouldn’t let her in. Dracaena would sooner have died than told her. To tell Ada was to put Ada in the path of it. So she lied, and pushed her away in small ways, and carried the whole weight alone, and Ada kept on loving her anyway. Crabbe and Goyle watched them both.

The Attempts

The attempts, when Dracaena nerved herself to them, were as desperate and botched as a frightened girl’s would be. A cursed necklace meant for Dumbledore struck Katie Bell, a Gryffindor Chaser, on the road from Hogsmeade and nearly killed her. On his birthday, in March, Ron drank from a bottle of poisoned mead meant for the headmaster and came within a breath of dying on the floor of Slughorn’s office. Harry forced a bezoar down his throat and saved his life. Ron had spent most of the year tangled up with Lavender Brown while Hermione pretended not to mind. Hermione sat by his hospital bed for days, and the Lavender business ended soon after.

Dracaena was no assassin. She was a child being made to play one, failing at it, maiming bystanders in the failing, and hating herself more with each one. In the thick of it a letter reached her from her mother. There was not one word of the task in it, nothing of the Dark Lord or the mission, only love: the plain, fierce, private tenderness of a woman who was poison to all the world and gentle to her daughter alone. It was the one warmth Dracaena had to hold against everything she was being made to do.

Slughorn’s Memory

The crucial piece of Riddle’s story lay locked in Slughorn’s own memory. As a young teacher he had told the boy Riddle about Horcruxes, and he was too ashamed to give up the truth of it. He had handed Dumbledore a doctored version years ago. Dumbledore set Harry to get the real one. In the end Harry used no trick at all. He went to Slughorn alone and told him the truth: what the memory was for, what Voldemort had become, and that the boy asking was Lily Evans’s son, whose mother had died to save him from the man Slughorn had once taught. Slughorn wept and gave it up. In it the young Riddle asked whether a soul could be split into seven.

Sectumsempra

In May, Harry finally caught Dracaena’s name on the map in a sixth-floor bathroom. He found her weeping over a sink to Moaning Myrtle that the Dark Lord would kill her and her parents. She saw him in the mirror and fired. They wrecked the room between them, Harry begging her to stop and listen, until she began a curse he recognized and bit it off half-spoken. Terrified of what she would cast next, he reached for a spell he had found in the Prince’s book that same day, marked “for enemies”: Sectumsempra. It opened her like a blade.

He caught her before she hit the floor and held her as she bled, begging her to stay awake, apologizing over and over. And she, impossibly, was apologizing too. Snape burst in, stopped dead at the sight of a boy destroyed by what he had done to a dying girl, then knelt and sang the wounds closed. He saved her life. Then he made Harry carry her to the hospital wing, through a crowd that now believed Harry Potter had tried to murder someone. Ron and Hermione were at the front of it. Crabbe and Goyle had to be held back. Ada stood with her hand over her mouth, crying. On that walk Harry stopped pretending to himself. Whatever Dracaena was, and whichever side she stood on, she was a victim and not a villain. And he loved her.

He went back to the hospital wing when she was out of danger and stood over the bed he had nearly made her deathbed. Then he burned the Half-Blood Prince’s book himself, every clever, poisoned page.

The Cave

Dumbledore believed he had located another of the soul-pieces, and took Harry with him to a cave at the edge of the sea. To reach it he drank, glass by glass, a potion built to break whoever drank it, and Harry had to make him keep drinking while he begged him to stop. They came back with Dumbledore barely alive and a locket that, when they opened it later, proved a fake. It had already been stolen and swapped by someone who signed only the initials R.A.B.

The Night the Death Eaters Came

They returned to find the Dark Mark burning over the castle. The Cabinet was mended. Dracaena had let them in.

The night came apart in fire and screaming. Death Eaters poured through the Cabinet into the school. Ron, Hermione, Ginny, Neville and Luna, the only members of the old Army to answer the warning Harry had sent through their enchanted coins, fought them in the corridors beside the Order. The werewolf Fenrir Greyback savaged Bill Weasley, and the Death Eaters set Hagrid’s hut alight on their way out. And Ada Khumalo was killed. She was cut down by Goyle’s father, one of the men Dracaena’s own hands had let inside, who found her as if he had been told exactly where she would be. Dracaena saw it happen. Then she was made to walk out through the ruin beside him, and say nothing, and keep walking, her friend’s killer at her shoulder and her friend’s blood on the stones behind her. She had never told Ada the truth. Something in her finished dying on those stairs.

The Tower

At the top of the Astronomy Tower, Dumbledore had frozen Harry under his Cloak against the wall, out of sight. Then the headmaster laid down his own wand, the old wand he had carried since the day he defeated Grindelwald, and waited. When Dracaena reached him, she found him wandless and failing. He spoke to her gently. She raised her wand, and stood there with the whole weight of what she had done pressing down: the necklace, the mead, the open door, the girl on the stones. She found she could not add one more sin to the pile, not even to save the parents she loved. She did not disarm him. She simply let her own wand fall from her hand. Snape came up the stairs into that silence, bound by the vow he had made her mother, and did what she could not. Dumbledore fell from the Tower, dead.

The Chase

The spell on Harry broke with Dumbledore’s death, and he went after them. For one moment in the chase he and Dracaena were close enough to touch, and he reached out his hand to her. She looked at it, and at him, and her face said no for her. There was nowhere for her to go, and her mother and father’s lives still hung on her staying. She turned and went with the others into the dark.

Harry ran on after Snape across the burning grounds, firing every curse he had, the Prince’s spells among them. Snape blocked each one with contempt. “You dare use my own spells against me, Potter?” he spat. He had invented them. He was the Half-Blood Prince, the son of Eileen Prince, and he had written Sectumsempra in that book as a boy. That was why he alone had known how to sing it closed. Then he was past the gates and gone.

The Funeral

Dumbledore was laid in a white tomb by the lake, with centaurs at the forest’s edge and merpeople singing beneath the water. Fleur Delacour stood by Bill Weasley’s scarred face and told his mother she was beautiful enough for both of them. Scrimgeour cornered Harry afterward to ask whether the Chosen One would stand beside the Ministry and be seen to, and Harry, who had watched the Ministry arrest a harmless Knight Bus conductor that year as a Death Eater just to look busy, told him no.

It should have broken him. Instead it set something in him like stone. She could be saved. They all could: Dracaena, and the castle, and the world the Ministry had failed and the Dark Lord meant to swallow. He would find the rest of Voldemort’s soul and destroy it and end him, whatever it cost. He would pull her off the side she was trapped on if she would let him, and stop her if it came to that. Hogwarts would not see him next year. Ron and Hermione told him, before he could argue, that it wouldn’t see them either.

Changes from Canon

  • Draco’s mission falls to as Voldemort’s punishment on her family. The Dark Mark is forced on her. Both her parents genuinely love her, and Narcissa’s Vow with Snape is made out of that love.
  • On the train Dracaena gives Harry a long warning that is also a confession, and freezes him.
  • Harry is right about her all year, and everyone puts it down to lovesickness over the Yule Ball.
  • , Dracaena’s original-character best friend, is killed in the invasion by Goyle’s father, and Dracaena is made to walk out beside him.
  • Harry uses Sectumsempra not knowing what it does, holds Dracaena as she bleeds, and admits to himself that he loves her. He burns the Prince’s book himself.
  • Harry wins Slughorn’s memory by telling him the truth. There is no Felix Felicis.
  • Horcruxes feed Voldemort’s power as well as his life. Each one lost weakens him.
  • The Gaunt ring bears the sign Luna wore, and Dumbledore carries the wand he won from Grindelwald. Both seed the Deathly Hallows.
  • Dumbledore freezes Harry, then lays down his own wand before Dracaena arrives, accepting his death. Dracaena never disarms him; she drops her own wand.
  • Snape reveals himself as the Half-Blood Prince in the chase, which is why he alone could heal Sectumsempra.
  • Harry and Ginny’s romance is cut.

Trivia

  • Hagrid’s hut, the trio’s meeting place since their first year, is burned the night Dumbledore dies.
