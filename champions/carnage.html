Carnage

Cletus Kasady

Plunderer

A remorseless serial killer fused with a savage offspring of the Venom symbiote, Cletus Kasady revels in slaughter as the crimson horror called Carnage.

Cletus Kasady was a monster long before anything alien ever touched him. Forged in an abusive childhood he answered with cruelty of his own, he grew into a serial killer without a shred of remorse — and worse, one who made a gospel of it, preaching that chaos is the only truth in a meaningless world and that true freedom is the will to do anything at all, up to and including murder. When the spawn of the Venom symbiote seeped into an open wound and mixed with his very blood, it did not corrupt him; it found a mind already delighting in exactly what it was, and the two fell into a union more intimate and more terrible than Venom ever knew. That is what sets Carnage apart from every symbiote before him. Venom is bound by a code and steered by a cause; Carnage answers to neither. He kills for the sheer joy of the act, negotiates nothing, wants nothing beyond the next scream — and, most horrifying of all, he is genuinely, gleefully happy being what he is. In Cletus and the symbiote there is no host straining against a passenger, only one will and one bottomless appetite, laughing together at the ruin they leave behind.

The power they share outstrips their bloodline. Carnage is stronger, faster, and more durable than Venom, scaling walls and reshaping his own body at a whim — sprouting blades, axes, and whipping tendrils of living crimson from any surface of himself, hardening flesh into weapons faster than the eye can follow. He can melt into his surroundings unseen, knit himself back together from grievous wounds, and, cruelest of all, he registers as nothing at all to the spider-sense that warns of every other danger, striking without the faintest whisper of a warning. Only the ancient banes of his kind — searing fire and shattering sound — can drive the symbiote screaming from its host. He is, in every sense, a nightmare that cannot be bargained with, because to Carnage the massacre was never a means to anything. It is, and has always been, the entire point.

"I was hollowed out inside... empty... just like my other. We were both sick... because we were incomplete... but not anymore. We healed each other... made each other whole. Neither one of us can survive without the other. Forget host. Forget symbiote. There is only Carnage."

Life or Death w/ Spider-Man

Spider-Man is Cletus Kasady's favorite toy. The wall-crawler's precious little rule — that he won't kill, not even something like Carnage — strikes Cletus as the funniest and most contemptible thing he's ever heard, a leash the hero fastened on himself that Carnage takes bottomless joy in yanking. He butchers where Spider-Man can see it, delights in a spider-sense that can't feel him coming, and dreams of the day he pushes the hero far enough to cross the single line he's built his whole miserable life around. To Carnage it was never about winning. It's about breaking the one man who won't break — and laughing the whole way down.
