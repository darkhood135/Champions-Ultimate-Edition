Duplicity Magnaloid (22 May 1875 – date unrecorded) was a Muggle-born wizard of and the protagonist of . A at from 1886, he was known to everyone who met him as the kind one: polite, endlessly helpful, and willing to give up an afternoon to anyone’s chore without saying why. He was also one of the rare few who could see the traces of , and one of the most dangerous wizards of his age.

In his fifth year Professor Eleazar Fig recruited him to finish the work of his late wife, and Duplicity followed the trials of to the last of ’s repositories while the goblin rebellion of 1890 spread across the Highlands. Taught the Unforgivable Curses by his friend , he left whole camps of Ashwinders, poachers and goblins dead, and no one thought to blame a schoolboy. He brought down , refused the power of the repository and sealed it, and that summer went quietly to Azkaban, convicted for the wizards he had killed and not for the goblins. The Ministry of Magic sealed the record of his trial. He wasted away in Azkaban until he died, and the history of the rebellion was written without him.

Physically, Duplicity was an ordinary teenage wizard. Peak-human Champions like Batman or Captain America would overpower him in close quarters, and anything at Spider-Man’s speed could close on him before he finished a curse. What sets him apart from every other wizard rated so far is what he was willing to do with a wand, and what answered him when he did it. The Killing Curse ends anyone with an ordinary body in a single word, and no shield charm can stop it. The Cruciatus Curse breaks a fighter’s focus, and the Imperius Curse turns a weak-willed opponent into a weapon. Ancient magic gave him a force no other wizard of his century could call on: enough to break fortifications apart, and to bring down Ranrok in the shape of a dragon.

Against city-level threats and beyond (the Hulk, Superman, a Super Saiyan) his raw power counts for little. Whether the Killing Curse, which takes a life rather than breaking a body, could touch them is an open question no one has had cause to test. A strong will throws off the Imperius Curse, as Harry Potter did at fourteen, so minds like Professor X’s are out of its reach. Within his own world he was the strongest student on record, deadlier in a fight than Harry or Dracaena Malfoy ever were and far broader in what he could do. He still fell short of the great masters of a later century, Dumbledore and Voldemort.

History

Worlds Apart

Family background

The Magnaloids were Muggles, and had been for as long as anyone in the family could remember. Duplicity’s father kept a small shop on the market square of a town in the North of England. His mother kept its counter and its accounts, and the family lived in the rooms above it. There was no witch or wizard on either side of the family that anyone knew of, and nothing in the house to prepare them for a son who would turn out to be one.

They were not wealthy, and the shop lived on its customers’ goodwill. Duplicity, their only child, learned early what that goodwill was worth. Behind the counter he was taught to greet every customer by name, to fetch and carry without being asked, and to keep a civil face whatever he thought of the person in front of him. Courtesy, his father told him, costs nothing and opens every door. It was the first lesson he ever learned, and the one he kept longest.

Early life (1875–1886)

Duplicity was born on 22 May 1875. He grew into a quiet, watchful boy, well liked in the town for running errands for half the street. Small things happened around him that his parents could not explain: a dropped plate that did not break, a door that opened before he reached it. They learned not to remark on them.

He also saw lights. In the oldest places in the town (the parish church, a stretch of old wall by the river, a standing stone on the moor road) he saw faint swirls in the air that no one else could see. His parents took him to an optician, who found his sight a little short and fitted him with spectacles. The spectacles sharpened everything else and did nothing about the lights. Duplicity stopped mentioning them, and learned young that some things were wiser kept to himself.

His Hogwarts letter came in the summer of 1886. As with every Muggle-born child, a member of the Hogwarts staff called at the shop to explain it to his parents, and took him to Diagon Alley for his things. There his wand chose him: elder, with a phoenix feather core, twelve and three-quarter inches long and unyielding.

Years one to four (1886–1890)

Main article:

Duplicity came to Hogwarts in September 1886 and was sorted into Ravenclaw. The habits of the shop counter went with him. Within a year most of the castle knew him as the polite, endlessly helpful boy who would carry a first-year’s books, look for a lost Kneazle, or spend an afternoon on someone else’s chore for no reason he would give. Teachers found him attentive and prefects found him no trouble. He was never seen to start a fight.

The lights followed him. In the old stones of the castle he saw them again, brighter than they had ever been at home, in places where no lights should be. His housemates put it down to imagination. Only one teacher took it seriously. Eleazar Fig, who taught Magical Theory, noticed that the boy kept looking at the same places and asked him what he saw (). Fig wrote about him to his wife, Miriam, who was travelling the world in search of a magic that seemed to be disappearing. Duplicity did not know it then, but Miriam remembered his name.

Over four years he collected friends in every house. In Slytherin he had , brilliant, restless and drawn to every door marked forbidden, and Sebastian’s closest friend , the blind son of an old family steeped in the Dark Arts, who had refused everything his family prized. Natsai Onai, called Natty, came to Gryffindor from the African school of Uagadou before their fourth year, when her mother took up a post teaching Divination. , a Hufflepuff who trusted creatures more than people, befriended him after he helped her stop a group of classmates tormenting a Kneazle.

Sebastian found in him the one friend willing to follow wherever his curiosity led. Over those years he drew Duplicity, a book and a whispered conversation at a time, toward the Dark Arts. Ominis watched it happen and argued against it, and Duplicity listened politely to both of them. By the end of his fourth year he knew a great deal more about forbidden magic than any Ravenclaw was meant to, and had used none of it.

Fifth year: the road north (1890)

Main article:

While Duplicity was at school, the goblins of Britain had been angry for generations. The Code of Wand Use forbade any non-human from carrying a wand, and wizards enforced it with whatever force they liked. led the uprising his people had long been waiting for, and in alliance with the dark wizard and his Ashwinders, he hunted for the hidden power that Isidora Morganach had stored centuries before. Miriam Fig had warned the Ministry about Ranrok for months, and the Ministry had treated the raids as an inconvenience. Not long before Duplicity’s fifth year she was killed far from home, searching for the last of Isidora’s repositories.

Her last letter named one person who might be able to finish her work: the boy Eleazar had written to her about. A few days before term, Fig came to collect Duplicity from his parents’ shop, with the Ministry official and a small container Miriam had sent from abroad. In the carriage it glowed, and only Duplicity could see it. Then a dragon fell on them out of the sky. Osric was lost. Fig saved the two of them by summoning a Portkey without a wand, which carried them to ruins in the Highlands. The ruins led into a forgotten vault of , where a Pensieve showed Duplicity traces of the past that Fig could not see. Ranrok and his Loyalists broke in after them. A guardian of living memory rose out of the Pensieve to hold the goblins back, and a doorway opened onto Hogwarts.

Duplicity arrived at the start of term like everyone else, with a story he could tell no one, and a teacher who now needed him more than any teacher should need a student.

Through the autumn the two of them followed Miriam’s trail around the castle and beyond it: a locket, a map, and books in the Restricted Section of the library, which Sebastian helped him break into. The trail ended in , hidden deep beneath Hogwarts, where a great map of the land lay across the floor and four empty frames hung on the walls. The portraits of the Keepers returned to them one by one. , who had once believed no one else alive could see what he saw, told Duplicity that four trials waited for him, one set by each Keeper, and that he would have to face them alone. Rackham’s trial came first and ’s second. Their memories showed him a girl in a dying Highland hamlet, and the teacher who took her on.

Fifth year: the Keepers’ trials (1890–1891)

Main article:

That same autumn Sebastian took Duplicity into the Undercroft, a forgotten room beneath the castle, and showed him what he had been reading in secret. Over the following months he taught him the Cruciatus Curse, the Imperius Curse and the Killing Curse. Sebastian meant them as tools for curing his twin sister, Anne, whom Victor Rookwood had cursed in a raid on Feldcroft. Ominis would have no part in it, and told them both so.

Duplicity took his first life on the moors, at an Ashwinder camp where Rookwood’s men had been lying in wait for him: a wizard, with the Killing Curse (). He did not stop at one. Over that year he left whole camps dead: Ashwinders, poachers and Ranrok’s Loyalists. Among the goblins were some who had only been defending their own camps. When he went hunting he wore a deep hood and a mask of dark metal lined in gold, and only the friends who rode out with him ever saw it. The few who escaped described a masked figure. When the Aurors found the bodies they blamed rival gangs, or the goblins, or each other. No one looked for a fifth-year from Ravenclaw, and at Hogwarts Duplicity went on being kind to everyone he met.

One person did know. Fig heard enough, and saw enough, to understand what his student was doing in the Highlands, and said nothing. Miriam’s work had to be finished, and the boy was the only one who could finish it.

The goblin , Ranrok’s younger brother, came to Duplicity for help. Miriam had treated him with a respect he had rarely had from wizards, and he suspected his brother had a hand in her death. Duplicity recovered for him the Helmet of Urtkot, the old symbol of the goblin clans, from Rookwood’s men. With a classmate who could read Gobbledegook, he slipped into a mine Ranrok controlled and learned that the Loyalists were building a machine to drill down to the repositories. Lodgok then went to reason with his brother alone, carrying Bragbor’s last journal, and Ranrok killed him for it. The same wand that had fought for Lodgok’s cause had killed goblins who had nothing to do with Ranrok.

The third trial, Headmistress ’s, showed Isidora as a professor, taking pain from a student who never knew it and keeping what she took. Miriam’s trail ended at , built by Charles Rookwood on top of a repository, where she had gone looking for Bragbor’s journal and found Ranrok instead.

Searching for the source of Anne’s curse, Sebastian took Duplicity to the place where she had been attacked: a cellar beneath an abandoned house at the edge of , once the Morganach family home. On its wall hung a painted triptych, and in its centre panel Isidora Morganach looked down at him and spoke (). It was the one portrait of her the Keepers had never found. She told him her side: a father who had asked, a teacher who never explained, and four professors who killed a woman they had never tried to understand. She did not pretend she had been right to take pain from people who never offered it. Duplicity came back to her more than once that year, and she was the only person he ever told everything.

His friends’ own wars ran alongside his. With Natty he hunted , Rookwood’s lieutenant, whose men were terrorizing the villages around Hogsmeade. When they cornered Harlow on Manor Cape, Natty chose to hand him over alive, and watched Duplicity kill Harlow’s men around her. With Poppy he broke up Horntail Hall, where poachers made a captive Hebridean Black fight for sport, set the dragon free, and drove off the poachers who came for the last Golden Snidgets. Afterward Poppy found the camps he had left (). And at Feldcroft, Sebastian killed his uncle, , with the Killing Curse he had taught Duplicity. Anne told the village their uncle had died in his sleep. Duplicity did not turn Sebastian in. To condemn him for one killing, he would have had to condemn himself for many.

The fourth trial was ’s, and its last test was a question (). Bakar’s portrait asked Duplicity about the camps: how many he had killed, whether every one of them had deserved it, and who had made him their judge. Duplicity answered every question, and then asked Bakar the same ones. Bakar had killed Isidora with the Killing Curse, judging her beyond saving and calling it protection, and in all the centuries since he had never been sure he was right. Neither of them gave the other an answer.

From the relics of the four trials, the wandmaker made Duplicity a wand that would open the way to the final repository. Victor Rookwood was waiting outside the shop in with his Ashwinders. Duplicity killed him, and the man who had cursed Anne Sallow died at the hands of a student.

Fifth year: the final repository (1891)

Main article:

In the spring of 1891 Ranrok drilled into beneath Hogwarts. While Professor and the rest of the staff held the Loyalists back from the castle, Fig and Duplicity went down to the repository. Ranrok reached it first. He broke it open with Miriam’s wand, which he had taken from her body, and drank in what it held: centuries of pain that Isidora had taken from people who never gave it. It made him a dragon of corrupted magic. Duplicity fought him in the caverns and brought him down, and Ranrok died in his own shape. Fig had been mortally wounded.

Fig did not die at once. He asked Duplicity to take what was left in the repository and finish Miriam’s work, so that she would not have died for nothing. Then he confessed (). He had known about the camps for months and had said nothing, because he needed what the boy could do. And Miriam had chosen Duplicity over him: only someone who could see the traces could follow her work, and she had wanted her husband kept away from the danger. Fig had grieved for both, and had made a child carry the cost. He died beneath the castle, beside the open repository.

Everything argued for claiming it (). Fig had asked with his last breath, for Miriam. Sebastian had asked all year, for Anne. The repository called to Duplicity itself, with the pull of a power that anyone who had taken would want more of. Duplicity had taken more than anyone in that cavern, and he understood exactly what it was offering him. He sealed it, with magic of his own that no one has seen worked since.

At the end of the year Headmaster gave Fig a brief eulogy, and Professor Weasley a long one. The rebellion was broken, and the Code of Wand Use stood unchanged.

Arrest and trial (1891)

The Aurors came for him that summer, to the shop on the market square (). The Ministry had not found him through his friends. Ominis knew what Duplicity had done, and what Sebastian had done, and never said a word against either of them. The evidence came from Theophilus Harlow, who had been in Ministry custody since the spring and bargained for his own sentence by naming the student who had wiped out his men. Natty’s mercy at Manor Cape was what put Duplicity in a cell. He did not resist.

He was convicted for the wizards he had killed. The goblins were not counted, because the Ministry did not count goblin lives. The trial was held behind closed doors and its record sealed. A public trial would have asked why a schoolboy had been left to stop a rebellion the Ministry had been warned about for months, while it enforced the Code that started it and let Rookwood and Harlow run the Highlands unchecked. His parents were told that their son had been convicted and sent to Azkaban. They were never told what for. Sebastian, who had committed the same crime, was never brought to trial.

Azkaban and death

Little of Duplicity’s imprisonment was recorded. He was sixteen when he entered , and he spent the rest of his life there, guarded by the Dementors: the creatures of taken joy whose example had once made the Keepers fear what Isidora might become. He wasted away over the years that followed until he died. The date of his death went unrecorded. He made no Horcrux, and nothing of him came back.

Legacy

The goblin rebellion of 1890 went into the history books, and Duplicity was left out of them. Many of the deaths he caused stayed where the Aurors had first put them: on rival gangs, and on the goblins, who were blamed for killings they had not done on top of the ones they had. The record of his trial stayed sealed in the Ministry’s archives. Sebastian walked free. Ominis carried both secrets for the rest of his life.

No one in Harry Potter’s time left any record of seeing the traces of ancient magic as Duplicity had, and few remembered that anyone ever could. A century after the rebellion, Professor Binns set Harry’s year an essay on it, and taught them the official history, the version with no student in it ().

Duplicity’s story ends before the merge.

Physical appearance

Duplicity was a slight, round-faced boy when he came to Hogwarts, with a mop of dark brown curls, brown eyes, freckles across his cheeks and nose, and two thin scars running diagonally across his right brow. He had the scars before he ever came to the castle, and never said how he got them. His spectacles, first fitted for the lights no optician could see, had thin rectangular frames. As a first-year he wore the ordinary black robes of the school with Ravenclaw’s blue at the collar and tie.

By his fifth year his face had lengthened and lost its roundness, though the curls never tamed and the scars never faded. His expression in repose was mild and attentive, the face of someone waiting politely to be asked for something. Outside lessons he favoured a dark coat and a long black scarf striped in white, wound twice around his neck, and switched his dark frames for a pair of pale metal ones. To almost everyone who knew him, that was all he ever looked like.

When he went hunting he wore something else. A deep hood of dark, weathered cloth framed a full mask of dark metal, its surface lined in thin gold that fanned out from a small dark stone at the brow, with narrow slits for the eyes. A heavy scarf of dark patterned fabric was wound high at the throat, so that no part of his face showed. No one at Hogwarts but the friends who rode out with him ever saw it. To the few on the other side who saw it and lived, it belonged to no one they could name.

Personality and traits

The kindness was not an act. That was what made Duplicity dangerous. The first-years whose books he carried were really helped, the lost Kneazles were really found, and the afternoons he gave to other people’s chores were really given. He had learned courtesy behind a shop counter before he learned anything else, and it had become the way he moved through the world: habitual, sincere, and also the surest way he knew to be let in everywhere. Nobody suspected the polite Ravenclaw, because there was nothing false about the polite Ravenclaw to suspect.

What his friends came to understand, each in their own way, was that he divided the world in two. There were the people he served, which was nearly everyone, and the people he had judged. Once someone crossed into the second group (a poacher, an Ashwinder, a Loyalist) nothing was left of the boy who carried first-years’ books. He did not kill in rage. He killed tidily and thoroughly, and went back to the castle in time for dinner. His cruelty was never suppressed beneath his kindness. It was reserved, kept for those he had decided deserved it, and as freely given to them as his help was to everyone else.

His name suited him in a quieter way than it first suggests. He rarely lied. He let people believe what they already believed about him, and kept the rest to himself, as he had kept the lights to himself as a child. The hooded mask made the division literal: everyone who saw it was either a companion or an enemy, and most of the enemies were dead. Only Isidora’s portrait, which had no one to tell, ever heard all of it from him.

He was a Ravenclaw to the bone, and he could give a reason for every death. When San Bakar’s portrait put him on trial he answered every question, then turned the same questions back on the Keeper. Having an answer for every killing is not the same as being right, and Bakar, who had spent centuries unsure of one, knew it better than he did. Duplicity had made himself judge of who deserved to live, as Bakar once had. Among those he judged were goblins who had only been defending their own camps.

He was not a hero, and none of the people closest to him ever called him one. His kindness was the reason no one looked for him, and Fig’s silence, which explains how far he went, does not excuse him for going. Yet he kept to limits that the wizards around him did not. He refused the repository because he understood taking better than anyone in the cavern, having done so much of it. And when the Aurors came he went quietly, accepting a judgment as calmly as he had dealt one out. His friends never agreed whether those were virtues, or only the last consistency of a boy who had made himself a judge.

Magical abilities and skills

Ancient magic

Main article:

Duplicity was born with the rarest gift in the history of the art: he could see the traces that ancient magic leaves in the world. Percival Rackham, who shared it, had found no one else alive with the same sight in his own lifetime. Where Duplicity saw a trace he could follow it, read what had been worked there, and in time draw on it directly. In a fight that meant an unseen force that answered no incantation, enough to tear defences apart and throw grown wizards and goblins across a camp. It was what he used to bring Ranrok down.

The Unforgivable Curses did not cost him his ancient magic. It judges the working, not the one who works it: it would not serve a working built on taking, but it did not police what else its wielder did. The moral cost of his killings stayed with the people around him, and with him. His last and greatest working was the seal on the final repository, magic of his own that no one has seen worked since.

The Unforgivable Curses

Main article:

Sebastian Sallow taught him all three in the Undercroft over the autumn of 1890. The Killing Curse became his weapon of choice: certain, silent once spoken, and beyond the reach of any shield. He used the Cruciatus Curse to break enemies who would not yield, and the Imperius Curse to turn them against one another. Few adult wizards of his time could cast the Killing Curse at all. He cast it more times than anyone ever counted.

Dueling and combat

Duplicity was among the most dangerous duelists of his age, and the finest of his years at Hogwarts by a distance. He fought outnumbered as a matter of course, against Ashwinder gangs, poachers and Loyalist war bands, and walked away from every fight. He killed Victor Rookwood and the guards around him in the street outside an Ollivander’s shop, and brought down Ranrok when the stolen magic had made him a dragon. He fought quietly when he could, unseen under a Disillusionment Charm until the moment he struck, and his hooded mask meant that the few who lived never knew who had come for them.

Magical theory

Fig’s student from his first year, Duplicity had a grasp of how magic works that went well beyond his lessons. He followed Miriam Fig’s research where trained wizards could not, read his way through the Restricted Section, and passed all four of the Keepers’ trials alone, though they were built for no one but a person like him.

Possessions

  • Wand: elder, with a phoenix feather core, twelve and three-quarter inches and unyielding. Elder is the rarest of wand woods, and wandmakers say it seldom stays long with an owner who is not remarkable. It was taken from him at his arrest, and what became of it was never recorded.
  • The Keepers’ wand: made by Gerbold Ollivander from the relics of the Keepers’ four trials, to open the way to the final repository. Its fate after the trial is likewise unrecorded.
  • The hooded mask: his disguise when he went hunting, a mask of dark metal lined in gold beneath a deep hood. No one outside his companions ever saw it.
  • His spectacles: first fitted by a Muggle optician for the lights only he could see.
  • A striped scarf: long and black, banded in white, worn in place of house colours through his later years at school.

Relationships

Family

His parents

Duplicity’s Muggle parents were decent, hard-working shopkeepers who taught their only son everything they knew about being liked, and never imagined what he might do with it. They understood little of his world and asked less. He wrote home dutifully and told them nothing that mattered. The first they knew of his fifth year was the Aurors at their door. Told only that their son had been convicted and sent to a wizarding prison, they were never allowed to learn what for.

Sebastian Sallow

His closest friend, and the most dangerous. Sebastian saw in Duplicity a kindred spirit, the one person who understood, as he did, that the Dark Arts could be turned to good, and spent four years drawing him toward them. In fifth year he taught him the three Unforgivable Curses, meaning them as tools to cure Anne. Duplicity followed him into the Undercroft, the Restricted Section, the cellar at Feldcroft and the catacombs beneath it, and was there for the curse that killed Solomon Sallow. He never turned Sebastian in, because he could not condemn Sebastian for one killing without condemning himself for many (). They had committed the same crime. One of them went to Azkaban, and the other walked free.

Ominis Gaunt

The conscience of the three of them, and the only one who ever said no. Ominis had grown up among the Dark Arts and wanted none of them. He argued with both his friends at every step and refused any part in what they did. He also never said a word against either of them, to the Aurors or to anyone else. He knew everything Duplicity had done and everything Sebastian had done, kept both secrets, and carried them.

Natsai Onai

Natty came to Hogwarts from Uagadou before their fourth year, with a hatred of anyone who preyed on the helpless that Duplicity shared in his own way. He helped her gather testimony against Theophilus Harlow when the Ministry would not act, and stood beside her when they cornered him on Manor Cape. She chose to take Harlow alive. She also watched Duplicity kill Harlow’s men around her, and had to decide what that made the friend who had helped her all year. She never entirely did. Her mercy was what sent him to Azkaban, though she never meant it to.

Poppy Sweeting

Poppy befriended him in their early years, after he helped her stop classmates tormenting a Kneazle, and in their fifth year she brought him into her war on the poachers. Together they freed a Hebridean Black dragon from Horntail Hall, returned a stolen egg to its nest, and with the centaurs saved the last of the Golden Snidgets. Then she found the camps. The boy who had freed a dragon and saved the Snidgets beside her was the same one who had left the poachers dead. Poppy could not make the two into one person, and never stopped trying.

Eleazar Fig

His Magical Theory teacher from his first year, and the first adult to take the lights seriously. Fig wrote about him to Miriam, and after her death came to collect him, because her last letter had named the boy as the one who could finish her work. Through that year Fig was his guide and his only confidant among the staff, and the one adult who could have questioned the killings and did not, because he needed what Duplicity could do. Dying beneath the castle, he asked Duplicity to take the repository for Miriam’s sake, then confessed that he had known all along. Of all the people who failed Duplicity that year, Fig was the one who should have known better.

Miriam Fig

Duplicity never met her. Miriam knew him only from her husband’s letters, as the student who saw what others could not, and chose him over Eleazar to finish her work: because only someone with the sight could follow it, and because she wanted her husband out of danger. Her choice set his fifth year in motion.

Anne Sallow

Sebastian’s twin, cursed by Victor Rookwood in the raid on Feldcroft and left in constant pain. Anne could have given her pain freely, and the given way of ancient magic might have eased it, but nothing could lift the curse. That was why the repository went on tempting her brother. She refused to be part of Sebastian’s dark cures and asked him to answer for what he had done to their uncle. Duplicity, who killed the man who cursed her, was also the friend who made sure Sebastian never had to answer.

Isidora Morganach

Isidora had been dead for centuries, but one of her portraits had survived, in the cellar of her old home at Feldcroft. Neither the monster of the Keepers’ memories nor the saint she believed herself to be, she told Duplicity her own side, and argued with the Keepers’ version of events, and with him. She took pain; he took lives. She was the only person he ever told everything. She built the repository, and he was the one who sealed it.

The Keepers

Four professors long dead, whose portraits set him the trials that led to the final repository. Percival Rackham, who shared his sight, guided him as he had once guided Isidora, and still had not forgiven San Bakar for killing her. Bakar became Duplicity’s mirror: a man who had killed someone he judged beyond saving and called it protection. In Bakar’s trial each of them questioned the other, and neither gave an answer. The Keepers hid the repository partly to hide their own crime, and in Duplicity they found an heir who understood that better than they would have liked.

Lodgok

Ranrok’s younger brother, who believed in the goblins’ cause and not in Ranrok’s way of fighting for it. Duplicity helped him recover the Helmet of Urtkot and uncover Ranrok’s drilling, and could not save him when he went to reason with his brother alone. Lodgok fought for the goblins Ranrok was poisoning. Duplicity, helping him, killed goblins too.

Ranrok

The leader of the rebellion, whose grievance against the Code of Wand Use was real and whose answer to it was to seize a power built out of other people’s pain. For a year Duplicity cut down Ranrok’s Loyalists in the Highlands, and in the end met Ranrok himself in the caverns, after the repository had made him a dragon of corrupted magic. Duplicity brought him down, and Ranrok died in his own shape.

Victor Rookwood and Theophilus Harlow

Rookwood, a descendant of the Keeper Charles Rookwood, led the Ashwinders, cursed Anne Sallow, and allied with Ranrok while despising him. Duplicity killed him outside the wandmaker’s shop in Hogsmeade. Harlow, Rookwood’s lieutenant, survived because Natty would not let him be killed. In Ministry custody he bargained for his own sentence by naming the student who had wiped out his men, and his testimony sent Duplicity to Azkaban.

Etymology

Duplicity means deceitfulness, the quality of showing one face while keeping another. It comes from the Latin duplex, “twofold.” Magnaloid joins the Latin magna, “great,” to the suffix -oid, from the Greek -oeidēs, “resembling” or “having the form of.” Together the name reads as a twofold nature that only resembles greatness: a boy who looked like the kindest student at Hogwarts, and who had the makings of one of the great wizards of his age.

Changes from Canon

  • Duplicity is the protagonist of Hogwarts Legacy made into a fixed, named character: a Muggle-born Ravenclaw, born on 22 May 1875.
  • He is not a late transfer. He has been at Hogwarts since his first year in 1886, and Fig has taught him Magical Theory since then. Fig collects him because Miriam’s last letter named him.
  • He has a family and an early life: an only child raised above his Muggle parents’ shop, who saw the traces of ancient magic long before he knew what they were.
  • The player’s choices are kept and judged. He learns all three Unforgivable Curses, does not turn Sebastian in, and seals the repository, and the story does not treat him as a hero.
  • His killings are real deaths with consequences. Among the goblins he kills are some who were only defending their own camps, and the story says so.
  • His hooded, gold-lined mask is a disguise he wears only when hunting, seen by no one but his companions. It is part of why the deaths are never laid at his feet.
  • Fig knows what his student is doing and keeps silent, and confesses before he dies.
  • Isidora survives as a portrait in the cellar at Feldcroft and speaks with him directly. Bakar’s trial ends in questions about Duplicity’s own killings.
  • The story continues past the end of the school year. He is arrested on Harlow’s testimony, convicted in a closed trial for the wizards he killed but not the goblins, and dies in Azkaban. He makes no Horcrux.

Trivia

  • Duplicity began as the author’s own Hogwarts Legacy character. Every image of him comes from that playthrough, and the painted look of his Classic outfit was applied in the game itself.
  • His wand is elder, the wood of the Elder Wand, with a phoenix feather core, the core of both Harry Potter’s and Lord Voldemort’s wands.
  • His spectacles were first prescribed for the lights. They never helped.
  • His Champion theme, “Forgotten to Time”, comes from a TEQ Bardock track in Dragon Ball Z Dokkan Battle, and is named for how completely the record let him slip away.
  • His line “You brought this upon yourselves” echoes one that the protagonist of Hogwarts Legacy can say after a fight with goblins.
  • He and Sebastian committed the same crime. One went to Azkaban and the other walked free, and Ominis knew the truth about both.
  • Natty’s choice to take Harlow alive is what sent Duplicity to Azkaban.
  • He killed Victor Rookwood, the man who cursed Anne Sallow, which was the one revenge Sebastian wanted most and never took himself.
  • He spent the rest of his life guarded by Dementors, the creatures whose example made the Keepers fear Isidora’s art in the first place.
  • A century after the rebellion, Professor Binns set Harry’s year an essay on it. The official history he taught has no student in it ().
