The Order of the Phoenix is the fifth story arc of in . It covers ’s fifth year at , when the Ministry of Magic denies Voldemort’s return and sends to take the school. Harry teaches his friends to fight in secret, and joins Umbridge’s enforcers and then defies her. The year ends at the Department of Mysteries with dead and the Ministry’s lie in ruins.

Story

Little Whinging

Harry Potter spent the summer after the graveyard in a silence that nearly broke him. He had watched a boy murdered and a Dark Lord reborn, and for his trouble he was shut up at Privet Drive with letters from his friends that said nothing at all. The wizarding world had chosen, almost to a person, not to believe him, because the Minister for Magic had decided that believing him would cost too much.

The silence ended in terror. Two Dementors came gliding down a Muggle alley after Harry and his cousin Dudley, and Harry drove them off with his Patronus, saving both their lives. Within the hour the Ministry had written to expel him for underage magic in front of a Muggle, and to snap his wand. That night a guard of the Order of the Phoenix came for him: Remus Lupin, the real Mad-Eye Moody, and a young Auror with bubblegum-pink hair, Nymphadora Tonks. They flew him through the dark to London.

Grimmauld Place

The Order was the secret society Dumbledore had raised against Voldemort once before, and had now raised again. It met in the grim, shuttered home of Sirius Black’s dead family at Grimmauld Place. For Harry it should have been a sanctuary: his godfather was there, and his friends, and the first adults willing to tell him some of the truth. But the house was a cage for Sirius, a wanted man who could not leave it. He paced the rooms of the childhood he had hated with a bitterness that spilled, too often, onto the ancient house-elf who had stayed behind. Kreacher was wretched, half mad with loneliness and loyal to a family of fanatics, and Sirius despised him for it and let him know it. The old pure-blood world had made them both: the elf into a servant who loved his chains, and Sirius into a man who had fled that world and still carried, without seeing it, its contempt for the creature beneath him. That explained Sirius’s cruelty. It never excused it.

There was other news at Grimmauld Place. Hermione’s letter made her a Ravenclaw prefect, which surprised no one. Ron’s made him a Hufflepuff prefect, which surprised Ron most of all. Molly Weasley cried over the badge, and for once Fred and George could only manage half a joke. The one Weasley missing from the celebration was Percy. He had sided with Fudge and the Ministry over his family, and no longer came home.

The Hearing

Harry was tried before the full Wizengamot, the whole high court, for a charge that should have been a footnote. The hearing had been moved and its time changed to make sure he arrived late and alone. Fudge presided. Beside him sat a squat, smiling woman in pink cardigans, the Minister’s Senior Undersecretary, Dolores Umbridge. Dumbledore arrived anyway, with a witness: Harry’s elderly neighbor Arabella Figg, a Squib who had seen the Dementors in the alley. Dumbledore asked the court the question no one else had wanted to ask. What were two Dementors doing in a Surrey suburb, and who had sent them? Harry was cleared, narrowly. It was an acquittal that felt like a warning: the people in charge had decided he was the enemy.

The Return

The carriages that took the students up to the castle that September were pulled by skeletal, black-winged horses Harry had never seen before. They had always been there. He could see them now because he had watched Cedric die. So could a dreamy, unblinking Ravenclaw fourth-year named Luna Lovegood, Hermione’s housemate, whose father edited a magazine called the Quibbler and who believed several impossible things before breakfast. Hermione found her maddening. Luna wore a necklace strung with an odd little sign, a triangle around a circle, cut through by a single line, and said it came from a story her father liked to tell.

Harry saw Dracaena for the first time that year across the Great Hall on the night they returned, and the sight stopped him where he stood. For a single moment her eyes found his and held, and then she looked away, scornful, the shutter coming down. She was not the sneering rival of the early years anymore. She was someone he could see all the way through, frightened and furious and breakable, standing on the far side of a war that had already chosen their sides for them. He would spend the year wanting to reach her, and refusing to admit to anyone, least of all himself, how much.

Umbridge

The Ministry’s war on the truth arrived at Hogwarts in a pink cardigan. Umbridge was installed as Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher, then, by one Educational Decree after another, as the High Inquisitor of the whole castle. She stripped her lessons of anything that might teach a child to defend themselves. She punished dissent with a quill that cut the writer’s lines into the back of his own hand in his own blood. Harry refused to say he had lied about Voldemort, bled for it week after week, and told no one. A government was torturing children in a classroom and calling it discipline, and most of the adults who could have stopped it looked away.

The Inquisitorial Squad

Umbridge gathered a corps of student enforcers to report on the rest, the Inquisitorial Squad, and Dracaena took its badge. She was resigned, after the graveyard, to being what her family needed. It was Ada who would not let it pass. Ada had never once raised her voice at her, and she was furious that Dracaena had pinned on the badge of a tormenting little cult and called it belonging. The argument was brutal, and the truth in it lodged in Dracaena and would not come loose. She stayed in the Squad regardless. She drifted back to Crabbe and Goyle, because being with them was safer than being known. The thing she could never say to Ada was that she hadn’t chosen the Squad so much as been caught between her father and the thing in the graveyard, with no road out.

Dumbledore’s Army

Denied any real defense by the Ministry, Harry’s friends pushed him into teaching it himself, in secret. Twenty-odd students from three houses crowded into the back room of the Hog’s Head and signed their names to a parchment. Dobby, who now worked in the castle kitchens for a wage, told Harry about the Room of Requirement, which became whatever its seekers needed, and they met there all year: Ron and Hermione, Neville, Luna, Ginny, the Hufflepuffs Ernie Macmillan and Justin Finch-Fletchley, and Chao Zheng. They called themselves Dumbledore’s Army. Neville, who had been hopeless at nearly everything for four years, improved faster than any of them, with a grim, quiet determination Harry didn’t understand until Christmas.

Quidditch

Ron made the Hufflepuff team as Keeper, and the Slytherins wrote a song for him, “Weasley Is Our King,” to sing whenever the Quaffle went past him. After Gryffindor’s match against Slytherin, Crabbe sneered about Harry’s mother, and Harry and George went for him with their fists. Umbridge banned both of them, and Fred, from Quidditch for life. Gryffindor never recovered. Ron did. By the last match of the season he was saving everything, and Hufflepuff won the Quidditch Cup on his saves, singing the Slytherins’ song back at them with new words.

Christmas

One night in December Harry dreamed he was a snake gliding down a Ministry corridor, and struck a man who had fallen asleep on watch. It was Arthur Weasley, and he was really there. Harry’s warning saved his life. Visiting him at St Mungo’s Hospital over Christmas, Harry, Ron, Hermione and Ginny turned a corner and found Neville with his grandmother, beside two quiet patients who didn’t know their own son. His mother pressed an empty sweet wrapper into his hand, and Neville put it in his pocket as if it were treasure. That was what Neville had been fighting for in the Room of Requirement.

The link between Harry and Voldemort had opened wide enough to frighten Dumbledore. He set Snape to teach Harry Occlumency, the art of closing the mind, and Dumbledore himself kept away from Harry all year in the hope of keeping Voldemort away from him too. Harry read it as rejection. In one lesson Harry fell into Snape’s own memories and saw his father, James, at fifteen, hanging a younger Snape upside down in front of a jeering crowd for no reason but boredom. Snape threw him out and ended the lessons. Harry carried the sight with him for months. His father, it turned out, had been a bully, and the people who had loved James had laughed along.

The Breakout

In January ten Death Eaters escaped from Azkaban together, among them Bellatrix Lestrange, one of the four who had tortured Neville’s parents. The Ministry blamed Sirius Black. Neville stopped talking much in the Room of Requirement and simply worked harder.

Valentine’s Day

Harry and Chao had grown close in the Room of Requirement, and on Valentine’s Day they went to Hogsmeade together. It didn’t work. She kept coming back to Cedric, whom she hadn’t finished grieving, and Harry kept losing the thread of the conversation to a girl in a green-and-silver scarf across the street. They each saw that the other was somewhere else. It ended without a quarrel, and they stayed friends, which suited them both better.

That same day Hermione talked Rita Skeeter, out of favor at the Daily Prophet and short of a story, into interviewing Harry for Luna’s father’s Quibbler: the whole truth of the graveyard, names and all. Umbridge banned the magazine, which made sure every student in the castle read it.

The Corridor

One night Harry and Dracaena clashed in an empty corridor. A few words caught and went up, and it became something neither of them had ever allowed: the venom they didn’t mean, and beneath it the truths they had never once said aloud. Harry was brutally honest. It pierced her because she could not deny a word of it, and she was left certain that she had nothing and no one: not Potter, not Ada, only two companions who weren’t friends and a future with the Dark Lord’s name already on it. Harry walked away feeling he had won nothing at all. He had told the truth and watched it break her, and it made no difference to which side either of them stood on.

Exposed

The Army was betrayed in the spring. Umbridge had learned that something was going on in the castle, and she had Chao dragged into her office and poured a truth potion down her throat. Chao told her everything without wanting to say any of it. The Army was exposed, and Dumbledore took the blame himself to spare Harry, then escaped the castle in a blaze of light rather than be arrested. Umbridge became headmistress. Harry refused to blame Chao, and said so, loudly. She came back to them the day she could stand to, and the Army met in secret again before the month was out.

Resistance

With Umbridge in charge, the Squad had its run of the castle, and Dracaena watched the cruelty mount past anything even her fear could stomach. In the end she broke her silence. She defied Umbridge openly, in front of the Squad and in front of the school, and very nearly paid for it. It was the first time in her life she had stood in the open against power and let it see her face. It carried her back to Ada. She went to her and made it right, and this time she didn’t hide it. She let the whole house see that the Malfoy girl’s truest friend was a half-blood she refused to be ashamed of any longer. Crabbe and Goyle, who had always assumed she was theirs, said nothing, and never forgave it. She hid only one thing from Ada still: how close the Dark Lord already stood to her family.

The Weasleys’ Departure

Fred and George, banned and done, left Hogwarts in style. They turned a corridor into a swamp, filled the castle with fireworks that multiplied whenever Umbridge tried to vanish them, and flew off on their brooms to open their joke shop. The castle cheered. Umbridge’s rule never recovered from it.

The Department of Mysteries

Through the open door in Harry’s mind Voldemort sent a lie: a vision of Sirius being tortured in the Department of Mysteries, where a prophecy about Harry waited on a shelf for the one it named. Harry reached, too late and too desperately, for some way to know. The two-way mirror Sirius had given him, which could have told him in a second, sat wrapped and forgotten in the bottom of his trunk. Kreacher, when Harry reached the house through a fire, said his master wasn’t home. So Harry went to the Ministry instead, into the trap. Ron, Hermione, Neville, Ginny and Luna flew with him on the Thestrals.

The Death Eaters were waiting, led by Lucius Malfoy. The battle shattered the Ministry’s hall of Time-Turners into ruin, every one of them unmade in a storm of breaking glass and spinning sand. The Order arrived as the six of them were being overwhelmed. And it took Sirius. Struck by his cousin Bellatrix’s curse, the godfather Harry had had for two years and loved for a lifetime fell through the veil between the living and the dead, and did not come back. Nothing brings back the dead: not grief, not a Time-Turner, not all the magic in the world.

The Atrium

What came after was the enemy entire. Voldemort himself arrived in the Ministry’s own atrium, and Dumbledore met him. For a few impossible minutes the two of them reshaped fire and stone and air between them, the Dark Lord bending the room around him like weather. He broke off and fled only when there was nothing left to gain. But the Minister and his people had seen him, standing in the heart of their government, alive. The denial was over. Fudge admitted, because he could no longer do anything else, that Voldemort had returned. The war began in earnest, a year late and with the Ministry’s credibility in ashes. Lucius Malfoy and the Death Eaters who had been caught went to Azkaban.

The Prophecy

In his office, among the instruments Harry had smashed in his grief, Dumbledore told him the prophecy at last. It bound him and Voldemort together so that neither could truly live while the other survived. In the end it would come down to the two of them. And the old man owned his failure plainly. He had loved Harry too much to burden him, and in loving him had left him unguarded. It was a mistake that had helped cost Sirius his life. It was the first time Harry heard the mentor he trusted say he had been wrong. It didn’t make the grief smaller, but it made it honest.

Kreacher

There was one more truth in the wreckage. Kreacher had slipped away from Grimmauld Place at Christmas to Sirius’s cousin Narcissa Malfoy, and told her everything a lonely, ill-used elf knew about his master and the boy his master loved. That was how the trap had been laid. Kreacher had helped lure Sirius to his death out of a lifetime of warped loyalty, and Sirius’s cruelty had helped make him into the creature who did it.

Sirius’s will left his house, his name and his servant to Harry. Harry, hollow with grief and with every reason to hate the elf, did the thing Sirius never managed: he set the old elf free. Kreacher left that same night, everything behind him, gone in pursuit of something no one in that house could have named. No one knew where he went.

Aftermath

Harry went back to his aunt and uncle’s that summer with a dead godfather, a prophecy that named him the hinge of the war, and the cold comfort of finally being believed. He had lost the last person who felt like family. He had watched a government persecute the truth and torture children to protect a lie. And he understood at last that the fight ahead would be his to carry, whether the world helped him or not. Behind all of it, quieter and never resolved, was a girl on the other side he could not stop wishing he could reach. Her father was in Azkaban now, and Harry did not yet understand what that would cost her.
